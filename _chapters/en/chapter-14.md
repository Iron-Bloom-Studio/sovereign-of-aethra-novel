# Sovereign of Aethra

## Chapter 14: The City That Waited

Asteron's gate stood open as wide as a royal road, yet Luca could not shake the feeling that they were passing through the mouth of something asleep.

The relic in his hand pulsed softly. Golden light traced the carvings along the arch, then faded as they crossed beneath the shadow of the wall.

No guards challenged them.

No bells rang. No wheels rattled. No merchants called from the streets.

There was only the wind, pushing dry leaves over stone.

Asteron was silent, but it was not empty.

Birds nested in roofless towers. Moss covered statues whose faces had been broken away. Roots as thick as a man's body split the main avenue and lifted paving stones like frozen waves. From a leaning balcony, a strip of gray cloth hung from a shattered pole—curtain, banner, or clothing, no one could tell anymore.

Serena raised one hand.

The party stopped.

Three scaled shapes were basking among the ruins of a house. Their bodies were low and long, but their backs were covered in overlapping plates of bone like natural armor. The largest opened one yellow eye.

“Armored Lizards,” Lysara whispered. “They usually won't attack unless their territory is disturbed.”

Pip looked at the road, most of which was occupied by the largest reptile's tail. “Of course their territory is our road.”

Serena shifted her shield forward. “We go around.”

“We could drive them off,” Luca said.

“And tell everything in the city that dinner has arrived?” Serena shook her head. “We don't know what lives here yet.”

They entered an alley to the east. Pip made a small chalk mark on the wall each time they turned: one line for a safe route, two for a dead end, a circle for danger. He did it without a joke, as if the empty city demanded a seriousness even he could not resist.

Zerak took the rear.

Several times, he stopped to study the dark windows above them.

“Something following us?” Luca asked.

“Not yet.” Zerak tilted his head. “But this city has too many places to hide.”

“That is not a comforting answer.”

“I was not trying to comfort you.”

The alley opened onto a broader street. Here, the ruins changed character. Small homes gave way to pillared stone buildings, some carved with suns, moons, flames, waves, leaves, and mountains. The symbols were not separated into districts. All six had been woven into the same lintels.

Lysara brushed moss from a pillar.

“Old Aethran,” she said. “Not an Arklune dialect. Older.”

“Can you read it?”

“Some of it.” Her finger followed grooves that were almost gone. “‘Beneath one sky, every current finds its road home.’”

Pip studied the six affinity symbols. “Sounds like a motto written by someone who never asked six mages to agree on where to eat.”

Lysara tried not to smile. Serena did not bother trying.

A heavy scraping noise came from behind them.

The largest Armored Lizard had followed.

Its head emerged at the end of the alley. It sniffed Pip's first chalk mark, and the scales around its neck spread wide. Then it advanced, claws grinding over stone.

“I don't think it appreciates culinary criticism,” Pip said.

“Don't strike its eyes,” Lysara warned. “You'll only send it into a rage.”

Serena stepped into the middle of the road and struck the base of her shield against the paving stones.

The clang rolled between the buildings.

The lizard lowered its body and charged.

Serena did not move until the final instant. When the creature's plated snout was about to hit her shield, she stamped one foot.

“Earth Wall.”

The road rose at a sharp angle. The lizard struck the new barrier, lost its footing, and rolled into the side of the street with a sound like a cabinet full of pans tumbling down a staircase.

Luca held Fire Aethra ready in his palm. Zerak had drawn his sword, though he kept the blade low.

The creature staggered upright, furious. Before it could charge again, Lysara sent a spiral of Wind across its face. The gust carried dust, the party's scent, and the smell of some old carcass from the opposite direction. The lizard stopped. Its tongue flicked out. After several uncertain seconds, it turned and lumbered toward easier meat.

Pip released his breath.

“A great victory for diplomacy.”

“A great victory for whatever corpse that was,” Serena said.

They moved on, faster now.

At the end of the next street, they found a courtyard enclosed by six narrow houses. A well stood at its center beneath an iron cover. Pip checked the hinges before Serena lifted it. The rope and bucket had rotted away, but somewhere far below, water was still dripping.

“If the city has a water source that hasn't gone dry, people could live here,” Luca said.

“People could hide here,” Zerak corrected.

Serena tied fresh cord around a metal flask and lowered it. The water came up cloudy at first, then cleared after several draws. Lysara tested a drop on a silver disk and smelled it.

“No poison I can sense. It would still need to be boiled.”

Pip took a blank map from his tube and marked the well. “Safe road, water, shelter. We are very close to becoming property agents.”

Luca looked up at the empty houses. One door stood open. Inside, a stone table remained beside three chairs that had almost rotted away. A child's height marks were scratched into the frame—small lines climbing year after year, then stopping abruptly far below the top.

For a moment, he imagined bowls being placed on the table, someone calling from an upper floor, and small feet racing across the courtyard.

“This city wasn't a fortress,” he said.

Serena followed his gaze. “No.”

“People lived here.”

“People live in every place that is later drawn as a point on a war map.”

Luca gently closed the door. They did not enter.

Across the courtyard, Lysara found a stone board carved with a district plan. Most of the names were gone, but the streets could still be matched to the route behind them. Pip copied it, adding his chalk signs and the position of the gate.

“If we have to leave quickly, we return through this courtyard,” Serena said. “Do not depend on the relic. It shows us a destination, not the road home.”

Luca nodded and forced himself to memorize the plan. The entrance. The eastern turn. The inscribed pillar. The well. An adventure sounded different when it was told inside a tavern. In a ruin, survival often meant remembering where someone had drawn a white line on a wall.

The closer they came to the city's center, the clearer it became that Asteron had not been abandoned peacefully.

Fire scars blackened the walls. Spearheads remained wedged between stones. At one crossing, they found a wagon that had become part of a tree's roots. Its frame was broken through the middle, and chain still wrapped one wheel.

The relic guided them under a stone arch and into a plaza.

A headless statue stood at its center. The figure wore long robes and held both arms wide, as if it had once welcomed the entire city. Now one arm was gone, and a deep crack divided its chest.

Around the statue lay the remains of a battle.

The bones had bleached white and were mostly claimed by the earth. Among them lay rusted orc weapons, a broken oni horn, human arrowheads, and fragments of dark blue armor.

Serena stopped beside one of those fragments.

She knelt and rubbed its surface with her thumb. Beneath the rust was the mark of a winged lion.

“Auregrad,” she said.

No one made a joke.

“The Beta War?” Luca asked.

Serena nodded. “The allied armies stopped Raizen at Asteron. That is what the military records say.”

“The records didn't say they fought in the streets.”

“Military records prefer lines, numbers, and commanders' names. They rarely mention the people who died beneath wagons.”

Luca looked across the plaza. History, which had always sounded like a tale told by someone else, now filled the place with names he would never know. An Auregrad soldier lay beside an orc axe. A few steps away, a large horned skeleton still gripped the hilt of a sword whose blade had long since broken.

Enemy and ally had become the same color beneath the dust.

Zerak stood at the edge of the plaza, his gaze fixed on the broken oni horn.

“Raizen,” he said quietly. “His name is still used to frighten children in Morvane.”

“In Auregrad too,” Serena said.

“Then all the kingdoms agree on one thing.”

“Do not become sentimental.”

Zerak looked at her. “I am trying to enjoy this moment of unity.”

Lysara had walked around the statue. On a side of the plinth protected from the rain, she found rows of names. Most had worn away, but one sentence along the bottom remained legible.

“‘For those who stood while crowns were late to choose,’” she translated.

Serena looked at the Auregrad emblem in her hand for a long moment, then returned it to the place where she had found it.

“We take nothing from here,” she said.

No one argued.

The relic pulled Luca toward the northern edge of the plaza. Its light had grown stronger, pulsing in rhythm with something deep beneath the ground. They followed it to a round building half concealed by vines.

Unlike the others, this structure was nearly intact.

Six pillars surrounded it, each carved with the symbol of one affinity. A bronze door three men tall sealed the only entrance. Six hollows formed a circle across its surface.

When Luca approached, two hollows began to glow.

Fire and Light.

“The door recognizes affinity,” Lysara said.

She touched the next symbol. Wind Aethra flowed from her fingers, and a third hollow came alive. When she added Water, a fourth answered.

Serena lit Earth. Zerak stood before the final mark.

“A city with remarkable trust in strangers,” he said.

“Or remarkable faith that six different people would never cooperate,” Luca replied.

Dark Aethra crept from Zerak's palm. The circle was complete.

Gears moved behind the door.

Then stopped.

The bronze remained shut.

Pip folded his arms. “Wonderful. After all that magnificent magic, we have successfully made the door cough.”

They tried again.

Luca reduced the flow of Fire. Lysara balanced Wind against Water. Serena kept Earth as low and steady as she could while Zerak restrained Dark so it would not swallow the light from the other hollows. For a moment, lines across the door shone from the outer rim toward the center.

The gears turned half a rotation.

And stopped again.

“It isn't the power,” Lysara said. “The mechanism accepts our balance.”

“Pip has no affinity.” Luca studied the hollows. “Maybe we're missing something.”

Pip raised his dagger. “I have a very persuasive opening tool.”

“Do not stab the ancient door,” Serena and Lysara said together.

Pip put the dagger away. “Fine. Today.”

He pressed one ear to the bronze, tapped several places with the handle of a tool, then knelt near the bottom of the door. Beneath a layer of dirt was a narrow grille. Pip began picking hardened mud from it with a thin wire.

“You lit everything,” he said. “But this part isn't moving.”

“Which part?”

“The part made by someone clever enough to know magic sometimes fails.”

He cleaned sand from a set of metal teeth, added one drop of oil from a tiny tube, then pushed two wires through the grille. His brow tightened. The tip of his tongue appeared as he concentrated.

There was a click.

Pip turned his wrist.

The physical lock released.

The entire building trembled. Dust fell from the pillars. All six hollows blazed at once, and the bronze door divided down the middle with a breath of ancient air that smelled of metal, wet earth, and the moment after lightning.

Beyond it, a wide staircase descended into darkness.

Pip stood and wiped his hands on his trousers.

“Six affinities,” he said. “One thief.”

“Scout,” Serena corrected.

“A historic day.”

They lit lanterns and went down.

The staircase ran far deeper than the building above should have allowed. Its walls were made from the same black stone as Luca's relic, crossed at intervals by channels of dull metal. Whenever they passed one, the relic pulsed and weak light raced ahead for several steps before dying again.

The air grew warmer.

At the foot of the stairs waited a chamber the size of a palace hall. Curved pillars held up a ceiling lost in shadow. Metal conduits entered the walls from every direction. At the center stood a core of stone and metal as large as a small house.

Dark fractures covered its surface.

Some looked natural. Others were far too straight.

Luca stepped closer.

The relic grew hot in his hand.

“Wait,” Lysara said.

Too late.

Golden light leaped from the relic to the core.

A deep sound passed through the chamber—not an explosion, but a single beat so immense Luca felt it inside his ribs.

One by one, lines in the floor awakened.

Light climbed the pillars, spread across the walls, and formed a map of Asteron around them. Roads, towers, waterways, and buildings emerged as a network of shining veins.

Most were gray.

Dead.

A handful flickered weakly around the gate and the northern district. One other line pulsed with steady light, running from the core toward the south before disappearing beyond the edge of the city map.

Zerak approached the wall. “Something is still receiving power.”

“Or draining it,” Lysara said.

Serena studied the cracks across the core. “Can this place be repaired?”

Lysara did not answer at once.

Beneath the map, ancient letters lit one at a time. Their shapes changed when Luca's relic pulsed, as if the system were translating itself for them.

Two words appeared:

**ASTERON CORE.**

Then the southern line pulsed once.

Twice.

Three times.

Like an answer from something that had waited a very long time to be heard.

**End of Chapter 14**
