# Chapter 5: Testimony

Arklune's southern gate looked much larger when someone approached it with a Goblin.

Luca only realized it after several people leaving the city chose to give them a wider berth.

No one said anything.

They only looked.

Then looked again.

Pip pulled his hood a little lower. His ears twitched whenever the footsteps around them slowed.

“You said people would stare.”

“I did say that.”

“You didn't say this many.”

Luca scratched his cheek.

“I've never entered a city with a Goblin before either.”

Pip glanced at him from beneath his hood.

“That doesn't help.”

“Sorry.”

They kept walking.

The basket of medicinal herbs swayed in Luca's hand. There was enough inside to complete the quest, though some of the leaves were bent and one plant looked as if it had been stepped on.

Perhaps it had.

Luca could not remember.

His ribs hurt too much to consider the fate of every leaf.

When they reached the gate, both guards immediately focused on Pip.

One of them lowered his spear. The point was not aimed at Pip, but the shaft came down far enough to block their path.

“Stop.”

Pip stopped.

His shoulders tensed, but his hand did not move toward his dagger.

Luca showed them his Guild plate.

“We're here to make a report.”

The guard looked at Luca's plate, then at Pip.

“Is he a Guild member?”

“No.”

“A merchant?”

“No.”

“A messenger?”

Pip answered for himself.

“A witness.”

The guard raised an eyebrow.

“A witness to what?”

“The attack on the southern farms,” Luca said. “A Hobgoblin-led group stole crops. Pip's tribe was there, but they weren't the attackers.”

“Pip?”

“My name,” Pip replied.

The other guard looked at the dagger on his waist.

“That weapon stays sheathed while you're inside the city.”

Pip looked at the spear in his hands.

“Yours too?”

The air grew a little quieter.

Luca closed his eyes for a moment.

They had not even entered yet.

The guard's expression hardened.

“Pip,” Luca called softly.

Pip exhaled through his nose. One ear twitched in irritation.

“My dagger stays sheathed.”

The first guard held out his hand.

“Guild plate.”

Luca handed it over.

The guard checked the emblem and number on its surface, then took out a small writing board.

“Name?”

“Luca.”

“His name is already on the plate,” Pip said.

The guard looked up.

“I was asking you.”

Pip fell silent.

“Oh.”

Luca pressed his lips together to keep from smiling.

At least he was not the only one asking unnecessary questions today.

The guard wrote something down.

“The Goblin enters as a witness under the responsibility of a Guild member.”

Luca stared at him.

“Responsibility?”

“If he causes trouble, yours will be the first name we look for.”

Pip raised one hand.

“What if he causes trouble?”

The guard looked at Pip.

Pip pointed at Luca.

“I only want to know whether the rule works both ways.”

“No,” the guard answered.

Pip slowly lowered his hand.

“At least he's honest,” he muttered.

The Guild plate was returned.

“Go straight to the Guild. No wandering around.”

Luca took his plate.

“That's where we're going.”

The spear was raised.

They passed through the gate.

Once they were far enough away, Pip looked back. His shoulders only relaxed slightly when the gate was no longer directly behind them.

“Did that go well?” he asked.

Luca considered his answer.

“We got inside.”

“That isn't an answer.”

Luca sighed.

“I hear that a lot.”

***

The Adventurers' Guild was crowded when Luca and Pip arrived.

The conversations did not stop all at once.

They stopped little by little.

Someone near the door noticed Pip first. The person beside him followed his gaze. Then another table turned to look.

The noise in the hall faded like rain gradually easing.

Pip kept walking.

His stride did not change, but his ears moved each time a chair scraped. His fingers tensed several times at his sides before opening again.

Luca walked beside him.

He did not touch Pip or say that everything was all right.

It clearly was not.

The clerk at the counter looked at Luca, the basket in his hand, the tears in his clothes, and then Pip.

Her gaze returned to Luca.

“Medicinal herb quest?”

“Yes.”

The clerk looked over Luca again.

“Did the plants fight back?”

“No.”

“The forest?”

“No.”

The clerk waited.

Luca placed the basket on the counter and winced as his ribs pulled.

“There was a Hobgoblin.”

The clerk looked at the basket.

Then at Luca.

“Among the medicinal herbs?”

“At the farms.”

“That explains nothing.”

Luca held his breath until the pain subsided.

“I know.”

The clerk pulled the basket closer and began inspecting its contents.

“The amount is sufficient. Some are damaged.”

“So am I.”

“That isn't included in the quest assessment.”

Luca stared at her flatly.

“What a shame.”

Pip snorted softly beside him.

Luca turned.

Pip immediately put on a neutral expression, but the corner of his mouth had not quite fallen.

The Guild clerk set aside the unusable leaves, then recorded the rest.

“Quest complete. Full reward.”

She placed several coins on the counter.

Luca looked at them.

“Full?”

“There are still enough usable plants.”

The tension in Luca's face eased.

“Good.”

“Now explain the Hobgoblin.”

The tension returned.

Luca began recounting what had happened at the farm.

He described the overturned cart, the armed Goblins taking crops, and the Hobgoblin leading them.

Then he explained Pip's group.

The clerk wrote quickly.

“A second Goblin group was near the farm when the attack occurred.”

“We weren't the second group,” Pip said.

The tip of her pen stopped.

The clerk looked at him.

Pip stood a little straighter. His hood shifted, revealing both ears.

“We were already living nearby before they came. They arrived later.”

The clerk looked back at her notes.

Then crossed out a section.

“Pip's tribe was in the area before the attackers arrived.”

Pip kept looking at the paper.

“And we didn't take any crops.”

The clerk wrote.

“Did not take any crops.”

“We helped repair the fence.”

The pen moved again.

“Some members of the tribe helped repair the damage after the attack.”

Pip's clenched fingers slowly opened.

Luca said nothing.

That was why Pip had come to Arklune.

He deserved to tell his part himself.

“Who witnessed all of that?” the clerk asked.

“I did,” Luca replied.

“Besides you?”

“The farmers.”

“Names?”

Luca fell silent.

He had not asked a single one of their names.

In his defense, someone had been swinging an axe at him.

“I don't know.”

The clerk sighed.

Luca felt the need to defend himself.

“Things were a little busy at the time.”

Pip glanced at him.

“He nearly got hit by an axe.”

“I dodged it.”

“Twice.”

“That's enough.”

Pip did not answer. His gaze briefly dropped to Luca's ribs.

Luca looked away first.

The clerk partially closed her report book.

“The Guild will need to verify this report.”

“Then verify it,” someone said from behind them.

A large adventurer rose from one of the tables. Three claw marks crossed the leather guard on his chest.

“Make the Goblin wait outside until we know the story is true.”

Several people nodded.

Pip did not move.

His ears lowered, but his chin lifted instead.

The clerk closed her report book completely.

“He is giving testimony.”

“He's a Goblin.”

“My eyesight is still quite good.”

A few people laughed.

The adventurer did not.

“If you let one in, his entire tribe will come tomorrow.”

“If his entire tribe wishes to complete the paperwork, I'll request another desk.”

More people laughed this time.

But the tension did not disappear.

“Auregrad wouldn't allow this,” the adventurer said.

A woman standing at the next counter turned around.

She had been there since Luca entered, a document tube tucked beneath one arm. Her clothing was neat and practical, with Auregrad's insignia fixed to her traveling coat. Unlike some of the adventurers in the hall, every strap and buckle of her equipment was exactly where it belonged.

Even the dust on her boots seemed disciplined.

The woman looked at the adventurer. Her shoulders remained straight, but her jaw tightened slightly when Auregrad's name was invoked.

“Auregrad does not govern this hall,” she said.

The adventurer stared at her.

“You're an Auregrad soldier.”

“Correct.”

“And you're letting an armed Goblin stand behind you?”

The woman looked at Pip.

Her gaze stopped at the dagger on his waist. Her fingers tightened around the document tube for a moment.

Then she looked back at the adventurer.

“He came to make a report. Let him finish.”

“How do you know he's telling the truth?”

The woman paused for a fraction of a second.

“I don't.”

The answer silenced several whispers.

“I wasn't at the farm,” she continued. “So I won't pretend I saw what happened.”

Pip watched her.

The suspicion had not left his eyes.

But his ears were no longer quite as low.

The large adventurer clicked his tongue and sat down again.

Conversation gradually resumed throughout the hall, though many were still listening.

The clerk reopened her book.

“Continue.”

***

Pip's testimony was shorter than Luca's explanation.

It was also more precise.

He stated the number of attackers.

The direction they had come from.

The kinds of weapons they carried.

How many sacks they had managed to take.

And the direction in which they retreated.

“Their leader was wounded in the thigh,” he said.

The clerk wrote it down.

“By Luca?”

Pip glanced at Luca.

“By my dagger.”

The clerk shifted her gaze to Luca.

Luca nodded.

“He attacked from behind.”

“It was only a scratch,” Pip said. He tried to sound indifferent, but his ears stood a little higher.

“Enough to make the Hobgoblin turn around.”

Pip opened his mouth to object.

Then changed his mind and looked away.

The Guild door opened before the clerk finished writing.

A farmer entered, breathing heavily. His clothes were still stained with soil, and one sleeve was torn.

He stopped when he saw Pip.

Pip shifted half a step backward before catching himself. Luca recognized the tension in his shoulders.

The farmer was the man who had nearly been struck by the axe.

He looked around the entire hall, then toward the counter.

“I was told to come and make a report.”

The Guild clerk pointed to the open space before the desk.

“Perfect timing. Were you at the southern farm this morning?”

“Yes.”

“Did this Goblin's group attack the farm?”

The farmer looked at Pip for a long moment.

Pip met his gaze without blinking.

Luca saw the farmer's hand clench, then open again.

“No,” he finally answered.

Some of the tension left Pip's shoulders, though his expression did not change.

“The attackers came from the forest,” the farmer continued. “They were led by a Hobgoblin. This group...”

He stopped and swallowed.

“They were already nearby. One of their elders was injured when the cart overturned.”

“Did they take any crops?”

“No.”

“Did they help after the attack?”

The farmer rubbed the back of his neck. His face showed just how uncomfortable the admission made him.

But he nodded anyway.

“Yes.”

Pip folded his arms. His gaze lowered for a moment, no longer challenging, but not forgiving either.

The clerk recorded every answer.

The farmer looked at Luca.

“And this boy pushed a cart at the Hobgoblin.”

Luca raised his chin slightly.

“It was a good plan.”

“It was a terrible plan.”

Pip immediately nodded.

“That's what I said.”

Luca's smile disappeared.

He looked from one to the other.

“It still worked.”

“You nearly got hit by an axe,” the farmer said.

“And then you got rammed,” Pip added.

Luca's eyebrow twitched.

He wanted to object.

Unfortunately, they were both merely stating what had actually happened.

“The ending could use some improvement,” he said.

The Guild clerk looked at him over her notes.

“How many people have told you your plan was bad today?”

Luca released a long breath.

“It's getting difficult to count.”

***

Footsteps sounded from the stairs.

Rolan descended from the upper floor carrying several reports.

He stopped on the last step when he saw Luca.

Then the basket.

Then the tears in Luca's clothing.

Then Pip.

His gaze returned to Luca.

“Your first quest.”

“Yes, Guildmaster.”

“Medicinal herbs.”

“Yes.”

Rolan looked at the report book on the counter.

“And a Hobgoblin.”

Luca straightened, then immediately regretted it when his ribs hurt again.

“That wasn't on the quest sheet.”

The corner of Rolan's mouth moved ever so slightly.

“I suspected as much.”

He took the clerk's report and read it.

Without hurrying.

Pip followed the movement of his eyes from across the counter. His feet stayed in place, but his body leaned forward slightly as if he wanted to make sure not a single line was overlooked.

When he finished, Rolan looked at the farmer.

“Do you confirm the contents of this report?”

The farmer nodded.

“Yes.”

“Including the statement that Pip's tribe was not the attacking group?”

The farmer hesitated.

Only for a moment.

“Yes.”

Rolan returned the report to the clerk.

“Record them as two separate groups. One nonaggressive tribe temporarily settled near the farms. One band of raiders led by a Hobgoblin.”

Pip lifted his head slightly.

“Not only in that record.”

Rolan looked at him.

“Explain.”

“The notices along the road say a band of Goblins attacked the farms.” Pip's voice remained steady, but his fingers gripped the edge of his cloak. “People will read that and assume it was us.”

Rolan was silent for a few moments.

Then he looked at the clerk.

“Replace the notice. Add a description of the attackers and the direction in which they were last seen.”

The clerk nodded.

“Understood.”

“Send a copy to the southern gate.”

Pip was still looking at Rolan.

“Is that enough?”

“No,” Rolan answered. “But it is what can be corrected today.”

Pip loosened his grip on his cloak.

The answer did not satisfy him completely.

But at least Rolan did not pretend that one corrected notice would fix everything.

Rolan turned to Luca.

“The Guild will grant you emergency-response compensation. It won't be as much as a quest reward because you didn't accept a contract for it.”

“That makes sense.”

“You will be examined by a Healer.”

“I'm fine.”

Rolan looked at the torn side of Luca's clothing and the dirt still clinging to his shoulder.

Then back at his face.

“That wasn't a question.”

Luca closed his mouth.

Rolan shifted his attention to Pip.

“And you.”

Pip pointed at himself.

“Me?”

“You joined the battle and gave testimony. Your name will be recorded as a witness, not a target of the report.”

Pip did not answer immediately.

“Does that mean I can stay in the city?”

“Not automatically.”

Pip's ears lowered slightly before he forced them upright again.

“But you can receive a temporary visitor's permit while this report is investigated,” Rolan continued.

“For how long?”

“Three days.”

“And after that?”

“We'll see what you decide.”

Pip narrowed his eyes.

“What *I* decide?”

Rolan raised an eyebrow.

“That is usually how decisions work.”

Luca lowered his head to hide a smile.

Pip did not seem sure whether the Guildmaster was mocking him.

Perhaps he was.

***

When the report was complete, the clerk gave Luca two shares of compensation.

Luca pushed one toward Pip.

Pip looked at the coins.

“What's this?”

“Your share.”

“I'm not a Guild member.”

“You still helped.”

“I only scratched his thigh.”

Luca closed his eyes for a moment.

“We've had this conversation before.”

Pip did not take the coins.

Luca pushed them a little closer.

“If you don't want them, I can keep them.”

Pip's hand immediately closed over the coins.

“I didn't say I didn't want them.”

“Good.”

“How large was your share?”

“The same.”

Pip counted his coins.

Then Luca's.

“You received the quest reward too.”

“That was for the medicinal herbs.”

“The herbs you abandoned when you ran toward the farm.”

“I still gathered them.”

“After a Hobgoblin hit you.”

Luca felt his eyebrow begin twitching again.

“The order isn't important.”

“I think it is.”

Luca opened his mouth.

Then closed it again.

Pip was not wrong.

That was the most irritating part.

***

The woman from Auregrad was still near the next counter.

The Guild clerk had opened the documents she carried. A map of Arklune's northern and southern routes lay spread across the desk.

Rolan approached her.

“Caravan report?”

“Yes,” the woman replied. “We came from Auregrad by the eastern road, then cut across to the northern route. There were signs of an armed group's movement south of the stone junction.”

“Orcs?”

“The tracks were too small.”

“Goblins?”

“Possibly. There were more than ten.”

Pip stopped counting his coins.

“Were they carrying anything?”

The woman looked at him. There was a brief pause before she answered, as if she were still getting used to speaking directly to a Goblin inside the city.

“Drag marks. Sacks or some other load.”

“The Hobgoblin group,” Pip said. “They may have passed through before attacking the farms.”

Rolan studied the position indicated on the map.

“Why did the Auregrad caravan take that route?”

“The main bridge is under repair. The captain chose a detour.”

“Will you continue your journey?”

“Not yet. One of the wagon axles cracked. Our company is staying in the northern yard until repairs are complete.”

Luca looked at the woman standing alone.

“You came to the Guild by yourself?”

The woman looked at Luca as though the question were unnecessary.

Luca immediately felt that he had received that look before today.

“The captain and the other soldiers are guarding the caravan,” she answered. “I was ordered to deliver the report.”

“Why you?”

“Because I wrote it.”

Luca nodded.

Right.

Another unnecessary question.

Pip stood beside him, studying the insignia on the woman's coat.

“Auregrad,” he said.

The woman nodded.

Pip put his coins into his pouch.

“I've heard stories.”

The woman's fingers tightened around the document tube again.

“I've heard stories about Goblins too.”

Her tone was not harsh.

But neither was it warm.

Luca could feel the distance between them.

Not the distance of a few steps.

Something that had existed long before they met.

Rolan tapped the map with one finger.

“The Guild will inspect this route tomorrow. If the Hobgoblin group is still near the farms, we need to know where they're moving.”

The clerk at the counter checked the contract list.

“A scouting party?”

“Not yet. A route inspection first.”

Rolan looked at Luca.

Luca noticed immediately.

“Why are you looking at me?”

“You've seen their leader.”

“From much too close.”

“Pip knows the area.”

Pip folded his arms.

“I haven't said I'll help the Guild.”

“True.”

Rolan did not argue.

“You haven't refused either.”

Pip frowned. He did not seem pleased that Rolan had found a gap in his answer.

Rolan looked at the Auregrad woman.

“Your caravan needs the route confirmed safe.”

“Correct.”

“The Guild needs someone who can ensure Auregrad's report isn't misinterpreted.”

The woman nodded firmly.

“That is part of my duty.”

“If your captain permits it, join tomorrow's inspection.”

“I'll inform him.”

Luca pointed to himself.

“And me?”

“You make your own decisions.”

Rolan began walking back toward the stairs.

“Shouldn't a Guildmaster give advice?”

Rolan did not look back.

“Don't die.”

Luca stared at his back with raised eyebrows.

“Is that everyone's advice around here?”

The clerk shrugged.

“We have experience.”

***

The Guild Healer confirmed that none of Luca's bones were broken.

That was the good news.

The bad news was that he still needed to rest.

“For how long?” Luca asked.

“At least until tomorrow morning.”

Luca waited.

“That's all?”

The Healer raised an eyebrow.

“Would you prefer longer?”

“No.”

“Then don't go looking for Hobgoblins tonight.”

“I wasn't planning to.”

Pip stood near the door with his arms folded.

“His plans are often bad.”

The Healer nodded.

“I believe you.”

Luca turned sharply toward Pip.

He wanted to object.

Then he remembered the cart he had pushed, the Fireball that had missed, and the Hobgoblin's shoulder sending him tumbling across the ground.

His mouth was already open.

No objection came out.

“I had reasons,” he finally said.

“Having reasons doesn't make the plan good.”

Luca's eyebrow twitched.

It was deeply irritating.

Especially because it was true.

The Healer hid a smile while packing her tools.

“Good. If you can still get annoyed, your condition isn't too serious.”

***

The sun was already descending when Luca and Pip left the Guild.

Pip carried a small wooden token bearing the Guild's seal and a simple cord.

A visitor's permit.

He turned it over in his hand.

“Three days.”

“Longer than you planned for this morning?”

“I didn't have a plan this morning.”

“Then three days is longer.”

Pip slipped the token beneath his clothes.

“After that, they might throw me out.”

“They might.”

Pip turned. His eyes narrowed slightly.

“Aren't you going to say everything will be all right?”

Luca put both hands in his pockets.

“I don't know whether everything will be all right.”

Pip studied him for a few moments.

Then faced the road again. The tension around his eyes eased slightly.

“Good.”

“Good?”

“Promises made too easily are usually lies.”

They walked toward Luca's inn.

Arklune's streets were still crowded. Some people continued to stare at Pip. A mother pulled her child a little farther away. A merchant closed his cash box as they passed.

Pip's jaw tightened each time.

But there were also people who looked only briefly before returning to their work.

Not good.

Not bad.

Only different from what Pip had expected.

When they reached the inn, the owner was standing behind the counter.

He recognized Luca.

Then saw Pip.

His expression changed immediately.

“No.”

“We haven't said anything yet,” Luca said.

“I know what you're going to say.”

“Good. That saves time.”

The innkeeper rubbed his face.

“Luca.”

“He has a Guild permit.”

Pip produced the stamped wooden token.

The innkeeper did not take it.

“The Guild doesn't own this place.”

“True.”

“If something happens—”

“I'll pay for the room.”

Pip placed some of his Guild coins on the counter.

The innkeeper looked at the money.

Pip continued.

“In advance.”

Luca turned toward him. Pip's shoulders remained stiff, but his hand did not tremble as he set down the coins.

“Your weapon?” the innkeeper asked.

“It stays sheathed.”

“Tied down while you're inside.”

Pip looked at Luca.

Luca did not intervene.

The decision belonged to Pip.

Pip looked back at the innkeeper.

“One night. The dagger stays with me, but I'll tie the sheath shut.”

The innkeeper thought for a long moment.

“Don't disturb the other guests.”

“I didn't come to entertain them.”

“One night,” the innkeeper repeated.

He took the coins.

Pip's shoulders remained tense, but he slowly exhaled.

“Anything else?” the innkeeper asked.

Pip looked at the coins that had changed hands.

“Does that include food?”

Luca turned his face away. His smile was still visible from the side.

The innkeeper sighed.

“Evening soup and bread.”

Pip nodded in satisfaction.

“Enough.”

***

Someone opened the inn door just as they were about to climb the stairs.

The woman from Auregrad entered.

Behind her lay a courtyard where several large wagons were parked. Soldiers and workers were lifting one side of the damaged wagon.

So the Auregrad caravan was staying at the same inn.

The woman noticed it too. Her step paused when she saw Pip standing on the stairs.

Her gaze moved from Luca to Pip, then to the innkeeper.

“The captain has granted permission,” she said. “I'll join the route inspection tomorrow morning.”

The innkeeper pointed toward the dining tables.

“If you're done reporting, eat first.”

The woman nodded but did not move yet.

Luca stood on the first stair.

“We haven't introduced ourselves.”

The woman looked at him.

“Serena. Soldier of Auregrad.”

“Luca. New adventurer.”

“I know. Your name came up rather often at the Guild.”

Luca frowned.

He was not sure that was a good thing.

Pip stood one step higher.

“Pip.”

Serena looked at him.

“I heard your testimony.”

“And?”

“I haven't said I trust you.”

Luca held his breath.

Pip narrowed his eyes. His ears lowered slightly, but he did not look away.

Serena drew a short breath.

“But I believe reports should be recorded correctly.”

“Would you let me speak in Auregrad too?”

The question was direct.

Serena's fingers tensed at the side of her coat when Pip mentioned Auregrad.

But she did not avoid his gaze.

“In Auregrad, you would most likely be detained at the gate until your identity and purpose were confirmed.”

Pip gave a short, humorless laugh.

“At least you're honest.”

“I see no use in lying about it.”

“So you don't approve of me being here.”

Serena did not answer immediately.

Her posture straightened slightly, as if her body had first chosen the answer she had been taught.

“I was raised not to ignore a threat,” she said.

She paused.

For a moment, her jaw tightened.

“But I was also raised not to pass judgment before a report is complete.”

Pip studied her.

“Those sound like two rules that contradict each other.”

Serena looked ready to object.

No answer came immediately.

When she finally spoke, her voice was a little lower.

“They often do.”

Luca saw the tension on both their faces.

“Tomorrow might feel rather long.”

“You're supposed to rest tomorrow,” Pip said.

“The Healer said until morning.”

“Morning won't necessarily make you smarter.”

Luca's step stopped.

He stared at the back of Pip's head.

“Why are you starting to sound as if you've known me for years?”

Pip looked back from the stair above him.

“In one day, I saw you stand in front of an axe, push a cart at a Hobgoblin, and cast Fireball while lying on the ground.”

“That sounds worse when you say it all at once.”

“Because it was bad.”

Luca set his jaw.

Once again, he could not find anything to dispute.

Below them, Serena covered her mouth with one hand. Her shoulders moved slightly.

Luca stared at her.

“Are you laughing?”

Serena immediately lowered her hand. Her face became composed far too quickly.

“No.”

Pip resumed climbing the stairs.

“She laughed.”

“I know.”

Serena cleared her throat, then walked toward the dining tables.

Pip stopped halfway up the stairs.

“Will you take the route inspection?” he asked without turning.

Luca looked at the visitor's permit hidden beneath Pip's clothes.

“The Guild needs to make sure the attackers don't return.”

“That isn't an answer.”

Luca rubbed his face.

Pip really was learning him too quickly.

“Yes.”

Pip gave a small nod.

“Then I'm coming.”

“Are you sure?”

“No.”

Luca waited.

Pip continued up the stairs.

“But I know paths that aren't on the Guild's map.”

Below them, Serena opened her document tube and checked the route report again. Her face had already returned to calm, but her eyes occasionally lifted toward the stairs.

Tomorrow, a new adventurer, a Goblin who was not yet an adventurer, and an Auregrad soldier would walk the same road.

Not yet as a party.

Not yet as friends.

Not even fully trusting one another.

But for the first time, their paths led in the same direction.

**End of Chapter 5**
