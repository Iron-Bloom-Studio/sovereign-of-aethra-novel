# Sovereign of Aethra

## Chapter 11: The Hidden Trail

The abandoned Guild post looked even emptier up close.

The sign above the door had cracked. The Guild emblem once painted across it had faded to pale lines beneath the moss. One window hung open, swaying whenever the wind passed along the old road.

Serena stood before the entrance with her shield raised.

“Pip?”

The Goblin remained crouched in the yard, studying three sets of footprints and the hoofprints of a pack animal.

“They came from the eastern road,” he said. “Spent the night here, then left southwest.”

“Three people?” Luca asked.

“Three walking.” Pip pointed to an uneven depression beside the hoofprints. “One donkey. Its load was heavy when it arrived. Heavier when it left.”

Luca frowned. “How can you tell?”

“The hoofprints are deeper.”

“Right.”

Pip glanced at him. “I can make the answer longer if that sounds more convincing.”

“No need.”

Serena pushed the door open with the edge of her shield.

The hinges gave a long groan.

Inside, the room smelled of damp wood, dust, and old ash. A service desk leaned at an angle on the right. The shelves behind it were empty except for broken bottles and stacks of paper that had decayed into brown clumps.

No one was there.

Zerak entered behind Serena. He kept his spear lowered, but his red eyes swept across every corner.

“No breathing,” he said.

Lysara raised her staff. The red crystal at its tip cast a faint reflection across the dusty floor.

“But there is residual Aethra.”

The mark on Luca’s left palm grew warm.

He closed his hand.

The sensation did not hurt. It was only a faint pulse, as though something familiar were calling from another room.

Lysara noticed. “Is your hand reacting?”

“A little.”

“A little by your standards, or a normal person’s?” Pip asked from the doorway.

Luca looked at him. “Since when are my standards different?”

Pip pointed at Luca’s injured ribs. “Since you described cracked bones as ‘still able to walk.’”

“I was still able to walk.”

“That does not answer his question,” Serena said.

Luca sighed. “Warm. No pain. Not as strong as it was in the ruins.”

Lysara took a small measuring crystal from her belt pouch. It was clear, with a thin wire wrapped around its center. She moved it slowly above the floor.

A pale light appeared within the crystal.

“This residue is not natural,” she said. “Someone carried an object containing Aethra into the post.”

“The missing relic?” Serena asked.

“Most likely.”

They spread out to search the building.

Pip went to the firepit in the corner. He stirred the ash with the tip of his dagger and found three small bones, a dried fruit peel, and a scrap of waxed black cloth matching the fiber on the doorframe.

“They carried waterproof wrapping,” he said. “Not cheap equipment.”

Serena examined the desk. “It may have been supplied by whoever hired them.”

“Or stolen with everything else.”

Zerak stood beneath the narrow stairs leading to the watchtower. One step had broken, and the dust above it remained undisturbed.

“No one went up.”

Luca walked behind the service desk. The first drawer was empty. The second refused to open.

He pulled harder.

Crack.

The front came free and nearly struck him in the face.

“Whoa—”

Luca stepped back, still holding the drawer panel.

Pip turned. “You defeated it.”

“The drawer was already rotten.”

“Still a victory.”

Inside the hollow desk, Luca saw the corner of a thin book wedged behind the drawer. Its leather cover was moldy, but some of the pages remained intact.

He carefully pulled it out.

“Lysara.”

The Elf approached. She took the book, cleaned its cover, then opened the pages one by one.

“The post log,” she said. “Most of the ink has faded.”

Serena stood beside her. “Can you read any of it?”

Lysara tilted the book toward the window light.

“Supply reports. Repair requests. Complaints about the trade route growing quieter...”

She turned a page.

“Here.”

The writing at the bottom was almost gone. Lysara followed the line without touching the surface.

“‘The final three families from Larkhill have requested an escort to Arklune.’”

“Larkhill?” Luca asked.

“An old settlement along the southwestern route,” Serena replied. “I have seen the name on Auregrad military maps. It should be abandoned.”

Lysara read the next line.

“‘Monster displacement has been observed in the valley again. The likely source lies toward the old military road.’”

Luca remembered the Giant Boar and the wolf pack that had attacked them.

“How old is that entry?”

“Around two years,” Lysara said. “Perhaps more.”

“Then this did not begin recently.”

“No,” she answered softly. “It has only become worse.”

Pip stood at the rear door of the post.

“If you are finished reading, the trail has not been waiting for us.”

***

The tracks left the main road less than an hour beyond the Guild post.

They turned onto a narrow path between pine trees. Low branches had scraped the cargo, leaving fibers of black cloth against the bark.

Pip walked in front with his dagger drawn. Every few dozen steps, he stopped to examine the earth, a broken twig, or moss scraped from a stone.

Luca began to understand that tracking did not mean staring at the ground.

Pip noticed almost everything.

Rope marks on a tree trunk. Grass that had begun to rise again. Dry earth on the side of a stone that should have been wet.

“They tried to hide their trail,” Luca said.

“After leaving the post,” Pip replied. “Before that, they did not care.”

“Why change?”

Pip stopped beside a large root. “Perhaps they knew someone was following.”

Serena immediately raised her shield.

The forest ahead looked empty.

Pip pointed to a thin cord stretched at ankle height. It was almost invisible among the grass.

One end led into the bushes on the right.

Luca followed it and saw a small wooden shape concealed beneath the leaves.

A crossbow.

It was tied to a tree, loaded, and aimed directly at the path.

“Do not move,” Pip said.

“I’m not moving,” Luca replied.

“Your foot is raised.”

Luca looked down. The toe of his boot hovered only a few fingers above the cord.

“Ah.”

He held it in the air.

“May I put it down?”

“Backward.”

Luca slowly drew his foot back and placed it where it had been.

Pip slid his dagger beneath the cord, held it with his thumb, then cut it near the trigger. The crossbow string trembled, but the bolt did not fire.

He exhaled.

“You can move now.”

Luca lowered his shoulders. “You could have told me you had it under control a little sooner.”

“I wanted to make sure.”

“By leaving me on one foot?”

“Your balance is acceptable.”

The corner of Serena’s mouth moved slightly.

Pip removed the crossbow from the tree and examined it. Its construction was rough, but the mechanism was well maintained.

“This is not an old trap,” he said. “It was placed yesterday at the earliest.”

Zerak looked across the surrounding brush. “Was it meant for pursuers, or for one of their own?”

Pip looked at him.

“Three sets of tracks continue past this point.”

“Then it has not struck anyone.”

“This trap has not.”

They proceeded more carefully.

The path descended into a damper part of the forest. Lysara stopped several times to inspect her measuring crystal. The light within grew brighter as they continued southwest.

The mark on Luca’s hand pulsed in the same direction.

“The relic is still ahead,” Lysara said.

“How far?” Serena asked.

“This crystal measures a trail, not distance.”

“Then we know the direction.”

“And that the residue is growing stronger.”

“I will accept that.”

They found the donkey near midday.

The animal lay beside the trail, still breathing in short, rapid gasps. Its saddle had been removed. Rope marks pressed into the fur along its back, and one rear leg was injured.

Luca immediately knelt beside it.

“Easy.”

The donkey tried to lift its head, then lowered it again.

Lysara examined the injured leg. “This was not a monster attack. It fell, or they forced it to keep walking after it was hurt.”

“They carried the cargo without it,” Serena said.

Pip checked the earth. “Three people left this place. Two carried something between them. The third walked unevenly.”

Zerak knelt on the opposite side of the path. He touched a dark stain on the ground with one fingertip.

“Human blood.”

Luca placed a hand on the donkey’s leg. Soft light gathered in his palm.

“Heal.”

Warmth spread into the wound. The donkey tensed briefly, then its breathing slowly eased.

Luca stopped the flow before the same emptiness he had felt the previous night returned.

“I cannot repair everything,” he said. “But the bleeding has stopped.”

Pip opened a water skin and poured a little into his palm. The donkey moved its muzzle and began to drink.

“We cannot take it with us,” Serena said.

“We cannot leave it in the middle of the trail either,” Luca replied.

Serena looked around, then pointed to a patch of level ground beyond the trees. “We move it there. There is grass and water. On our return, we take it if it can walk.”

Luca nodded.

They used an old cloak from the saddle to help move the donkey without putting pressure on its injured leg. Zerak carried most of the weight alone while Serena kept its hindquarters stable.

Once the animal was safe, Pip found a fragment from a cargo crate.

A burned circle marked its surface.

Lysara held the measuring crystal near it.

The light inside flared.

“The relic was inside this crate.”

Luca studied the burned mark. “Did the crate break when the donkey fell?”

“Perhaps.” Lysara looked toward the path ahead. “Or something inside began to react.”

The mark on Luca’s hand grew warmer.

This time, he did not need to say anything. Everyone saw his fingers tighten.

Serena stood. “We continue.”

***

The blood on the ground became easier to follow.

The trail led them out of the forest and onto a rocky slope. Below, a narrow valley stretched between two ridges. Its grass stood tall and pale. There were no houses or smoke, but the remains of stone fences showed that people had once lived there.

Pip stopped.

“Someone is there.”

Beneath a tree leaning over the slope, a figure rested against the trunk.

A man.

His clothes were dark, with waxed fabric over the shoulders. A crossbow lay beside his right hand. The cloth wrapped around his thigh had turned dark red.

Serena advanced with her shield forward.

“Do not touch the weapon.”

The man opened his eyes.

He tried to reach for the crossbow.

Zerak planted the point of his spear in the earth between the man’s hand and the weapon.

“Don’t.”

The man looked at Zerak’s carapace, then at Serena, Lysara, Luca, and Pip.

“Guild?” His voice was rough.

Luca showed him the emblem on their assignment paper. “Arklune Guild. What is your name?”

The man gave a short laugh, then grimaced when the movement pulled at his wounded thigh.

“Too late.”

“That is not a name,” Pip said.

The man glanced at him. “Harlan.”

Luca knelt, but Serena held his shoulder.

“He set a trap for anyone following them.”

“If we do not close the wound, he will not have time to answer anything.”

Harlan gave a weak grin. “The boy is right.”

“I’m not a boy—” Luca stopped, then looked at Serena. “Can we argue about this after he stops losing blood?”

Serena released him. “Pip, secure his weapon. Zerak, watch him.”

Luca opened the bandage around Harlan’s thigh. The wound was deep, but it had not reached the bone. Another tear marked his side, as though a fragment of wood or stone had struck him.

“This will hurt,” Luca said.

“It already hurts.”

“Then it should not be too surprising.”

He placed both hands over the wound.

“Heal.”

Pale light flowed between his fingers. Harlan growled and clenched the earth as the torn flesh began to close.

“Aargh—you call this healing?”

“I never said it would be comfortable.”

“You should have.”

“I only recently learned this version.”

Harlan opened one eye. “That does not help.”

Luca maintained the spell until the bleeding stopped. Cold sweat appeared on his forehead, and the mark on his palm glimmered more brightly than before.

He withdrew his hands and sat back.

“The wound is not fully healed,” he said, catching his breath. “But you will not die from blood loss.”

“Wonderful news.”

Lysara knelt on the other side. “What were you carrying?”

Harlan looked at the measuring crystal in her hand.

“They did not tell us.”

“You risked your life for something you knew nothing about?” Serena asked.

“I risked it for the payment.”

“And now?”

Harlan looked at the wound Luca had closed. “Now I am reconsidering its value.”

Pip had dismantled the trap crossbow and pocketed its trigger bolt. “Who were the other two?”

“Calder and Venn.”

“They left you.”

Harlan’s jaw tightened. “Venn wanted to stop after the donkey fell. Calder would not allow it.”

“Why?” Luca asked.

“Because the thing inside the crate started making a sound.”

No one spoke immediately.

Wind moved down the slope.

Lysara watched him. “What kind of sound?”

“Not one you hear with your ears.” Harlan pressed a hand to his temple. “A hum inside the bones. It grew stronger as we neared the valley.”

The mark on Luca’s hand pulsed.

Once.

Then stopped.

“Was the relic cracked?” Lysara asked.

Harlan nodded. “I saw it when the crate broke. A pale stone the size of a human head, surrounded by old metal. There was a black crack through its center.”

Lysara exchanged a glance with Serena.

“That is not a minor relic,” she said.

“Where did they take it?” Serena asked.

Harlan raised a hand with some difficulty and pointed across the valley.

“The old bell tower.”

Luca followed his finger.

On the ridge opposite them, the shape of a stone tower emerged through thin mist. Its upper section had broken away. It had no roof, no intact windows, and no bell hanging within it.

“Who hired you?” Serena asked.

“Never gave a name. He paid through Calder.”

“Human?”

“Yes.”

“From Arklune?”

“I don’t know.”

Pip crossed his arms. “You know very little for someone who builds a decent trap.”

“Knowing too much makes the price higher.”

“And life shorter,” Zerak said.

Harlan looked at him, then laughed dryly. “It seems so.”

Luca stood slowly. His legs felt light after using Heal, but he could still walk.

“Harlan cannot come with us.”

“I had no intention of doing so,” the man said.

Serena checked the sky. The sun had fallen near the ridge.

“We do not have enough time to carry him back to the post and catch them before dark.”

“I can hide,” Harlan said. “There is a hollow beneath the slope. Calder will not return for me.”

“That is not a guarantee,” Luca said.

“No. But if the hum is growing stronger, you do not have time either.”

Lysara looked at her crystal.

The light within no longer remained still.

It turned slowly, like smoke trapped behind glass.

“He is right.”

Serena did not like the decision. It showed in the tightening of her jaw.

But she did not deny the reality of it.

“We move him to the hollow,” she said. “Give him water and one food packet. Then we head for the tower.”

Pip raised an eyebrow. “One packet?”

“You count the food.”

“I am only making sure you are learning.”

***

They reached the valley floor as the sun touched the horizon.

The closer they came to the tower, the fewer sounds they heard.

No birds.

No insects.

Even the wind seemed to hold its breath among the tall grass.

Pip walked with his small crossbow raised. His dagger remained within easy reach.

Serena led from the front with her shield.

Zerak guarded the rear, his spear occasionally turning toward something unseen in the grass.

Lysara held the measuring crystal away from her body. The light inside now spun rapidly.

“The flow is spreading,” she said.

“From the tower?” Luca asked.

“From beneath it. The tower may have been built over an old Aethra channel.”

The mark on Luca’s palm shone beneath his glove.

He could feel something traveling beneath the ground.

It was not a calm current like water.

It felt more like a pull.

Something in the tower was drawing Aethra from its surroundings—and sending its pulse back through the earth.

Luca stopped.

“Something is moving.”

Serena turned. “Where?”

“I don’t know.” Luca closed his hand. “Below us. Or around us.”

Zerak looked toward the northern slope.

The grass there swayed.

Not with the wind.

A large shadow moved behind it, then vanished.

Pip swallowed. “Tell me that was only grass.”

“Grass does not have shoulders,” Zerak said.

“I preferred my answer.”

They quickened their pace.

The old tower stood within a circle of collapsed stone. Its wooden door was gone. Inside, a stairway spiraled along the wall, though several steps had broken away.

Fresh drag marks crossed the ground at its base.

Two people had carried something inside.

Lysara approached the threshold and abruptly stopped.

Her measuring crystal made a faint cracking sound.

She immediately pulled it back.

“The Aethra inside is too dense.”

Serena raised her shield higher. “Are Calder and Venn still in there?”

Pip crouched and touched one of the tracks.

“They entered. They have not come out.”

A low toll sounded from within the tower.

Doong.

All five froze.

Luca looked up.

There was no bell at the top of the tower.

A second toll followed.

This time, it did not travel through the air.

The vibration rose through the earth, passed through their boots, and settled inside their bones.

Doong.

The mark on Luca’s hand flared.

Something roared in the distance.

Another roar answered from the eastern ridge.

Then a growl came from the grass behind them.

Serena turned and raised her shield.

A pair of eyes appeared in the darkness.

Then another.

And another.

Across the valley, shadows began moving toward the tower.

Luca drew his sword.

“We did not come alone.”

The third toll shook the ground.

And for the first time, the entire valley answered the tower’s call.

**End of Chapter 11**
