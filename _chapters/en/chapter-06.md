# Chapter 6: Resonance

The next morning, Luca discovered that a night's sleep had not stopped his ribs from hurting.

It had only made the pain more regular.

It hurt when he woke.

When he dressed.

When he walked down the stairs.

And especially when Pip tapped his side to check whether he had truly recovered.

Luca immediately pulled away.

“Why did you do that?”

Pip withdrew his hand.

“Checking.”

“You could have asked.”

“If I asked, you'd say you were fine.”

Luca opened his mouth.

Then closed it again.

Pip gave a small nod.

“That's why I checked.”

At a table near the window, Serena lowered her cup. Her expression remained calm, but the corner of her mouth moved slightly.

Luca stared at her.

“Are you laughing again?”

“No.”

Her answer came too quickly.

Pip took a piece of bread from the table.

“She laughed.”

“I know.”

Serena stood and straightened her traveling coat.

“If you're finished, the caravan will depart shortly.”

“We're finished,” Luca said.

“He hasn't eaten breakfast,” Pip said.

Luca looked at the bread Pip had just taken.

“That was mine.”

Pip bit into it.

“Not anymore.”

For the second time that morning, Luca could find no useful objection.

***

The Auregrad caravan had filled the road outside the inn by the time they emerged.

Three large wagons stood in line between soldiers and workers. Wooden crates were tied down in their beds, while thick cloth covered cargo that could not be allowed to get wet. The axle that had cracked yesterday had been replaced, though its wood still looked much newer than everything around it.

A soldier bearing a captain's rank stood beside the lead wagon. He read the letter from the Guild, then looked at Luca.

His gaze dropped to the Guild plate on Luca's chest.

Then to Pip.

It remained there longer.

Pip met it without lowering his head.

“The Guild vouches for him?” the captain asked.

“The Guild issued him a visitor's permit,” Serena answered.

“That was not my question.”

Serena's jaw tightened slightly.

“He is a witness who knows the paths around the farms. The Guildmaster requested that he join the inspection.”

The captain looked again at the letter in his hand.

“The dagger remains sheathed unless there is a threat.”

Pip folded his arms.

“That is generally what a sheath is for.”

Luca closed his eyes for a moment.

The day had barely begun.

The captain stared at Pip long enough for two nearby soldiers to take notice.

Serena stepped half a pace forward.

“I am responsible for the route inspection, Captain.”

His gaze shifted to her.

“Then make sure there are no problems.”

“Yes, Captain.”

He handed the Guild letter back and signaled the caravan.

The wheels began to move.

Pip waited until the captain had walked away.

“He likes you,” Luca said.

Pip looked at him flatly.

“You're bad at lying.”

“I was trying to lighten the mood.”

“It weighs exactly the same.”

Serena slipped the letter into her document tube.

“Do you two always talk like this?”

“We only met yesterday,” Luca said.

“It feels longer,” Pip said.

Luca's eyebrow twitched.

Serena turned away before her smile became too obvious.

***

They left Arklune through the southern gate.

At first, the journey passed without incident.

Luca walked beside the second wagon. Serena stayed several steps ahead, occasionally speaking with the driver and inspecting the road. Pip chose the edge of the company, close enough to follow them but far enough from the other soldiers.

No one directly drove him away.

No one spoke to him either.

Pip seemed to prefer it that way.

The sun was still low when they reached the stone junction marked in Serena's report.

One road led toward the southern farms. The other bent east through low hills.

Serena raised a hand.

The caravan stopped.

Pip immediately crouched beside the road.

“The tracks are still here,” he said.

Luca approached.

The ground in front of Pip looked like ordinary earth that had been trampled by many feet.

“Which ones?”

Pip pointed at several small depressions between the wheel tracks.

“These.”

Luca leaned closer.

“I still only see dirt.”

“That's because you're standing on one.”

Luca looked down.

His boot was directly over a track.

He moved his foot.

“You could have said so earlier.”

“I wanted to see how long it took you to notice.”

Serena crouched on the other side. She studied the direction of the tracks, then a long scrape across the ground.

“Something was dragged,” she said.

Pip nodded.

“They came from the hills, went toward the farms, and returned the same way.”

“How many?” Luca asked.

Pip studied the overlapping tracks.

“More than ten. Perhaps fifteen.”

Serena looked toward the distant trees.

“They didn't use the main road.”

“They didn't want to be seen.”

“Or there is another path.”

Pip stood. One ear turned toward the eastern slope.

“There is.”

The captain approached from the front of the caravan.

“Findings?”

Serena explained the direction of the tracks. The captain looked at the narrow trail between the bushes, then at the wagons behind him.

“We're not taking the caravan in there.”

“We don't need to,” Serena replied. “The main road remains passable. We only need to inspect where the tracks meet it again.”

The captain nodded.

“The caravan will move slowly to the next bend. Stay within sight.”

Pip looked toward the slope.

“If we stay within sight, we won't find anything.”

The captain turned toward him.

“Do you have a suggestion?”

“There's an old waterway behind those bushes. It returns to the main road farther ahead.”

“How do you know?”

“I live in this forest.”

The captain did not look satisfied.

Serena checked the map in her hands.

“That waterway isn't marked.”

“Many things aren't on Auregrad's maps.”

Pip said it without mockery.

Somehow, that made it sound sharper.

Serena inhaled slowly.

“How far?”

“Less than half a bell.”

The captain looked from the main road to the slope.

“Inspect the entrance. Do not go too far inside. If you find signs of an armed group, return.”

“Understood,” Serena replied.

They left the caravan and followed Pip into the bushes.

***

The old waterway was almost buried beneath roots.

An arch of fitted stone remained visible beneath soil and moss. Water ran only ankle-deep, clear enough to reveal pebbles along the bottom.

Beside its entrance lay mud that had recently been disturbed.

Pip touched it with two fingers.

“They passed here.”

Serena looked into the dark opening beneath the arch.

“The captain ordered us not to go too far inside.”

“We don't need to enter,” Pip said. “The tracks come out again on this side.”

He pointed toward the slope above the waterway.

Luca followed his hand. Broken branches formed a trail toward the main road.

“So they used the waterway as a marker?”

“Or as a place to hide something.”

Pip stepped toward the stone wall.

Serena raised a hand.

“Stop.”

Pip halted before his right foot touched the ground.

A quiet sound came from beneath them.

Not a snapping branch.

Not flowing water.

Cracking stone.

Serena looked down.

Her expression changed at once.

“Back.”

The ground collapsed.

Luca grabbed Pip just as the Goblin lost his footing. He caught the back of Pip's cloak, but the sudden pull sent his own feet sliding.

Serena drove her palm into the earth.

A layer of stone rose beneath them.

For a moment, it held.

Then cracks spread through it.

“Jump!” Serena shouted.

There was nowhere to jump.

The ground beneath all three gave way.

Luca felt himself falling among rock, roots, and dust. He was still gripping Pip's cloak when something struck his shoulder.

Below them, Serena extended both hands.

Falling stones gathered into a sloped surface just before they reached the bottom.

They struck it and slid.

Luca tried to protect his head. Pip slipped from his grasp and rolled past him. Serena landed harder than either of them, and then the stone slope beneath them broke apart.

Dust filled the air.

For several moments, no one moved.

Luca lay on his back, staring into darkness.

His ribs throbbed.

So did his shoulder.

He decided not to count the rest.

“Pip?”

Coughing came from his left.

“Alive.”

“Serena?”

“Here.”

Luca breathed out.

Then a small stone fell and struck his forehead.

He closed his eyes.

“I'm beginning to dislike route inspections.”

“Only now?” Pip asked.

***

Light entered through the opening far above them.

It was not enough to illuminate the chamber, but enough to show that they had not fallen into the waterway.

They were inside a stone hall.

Low pillars stood on both sides. Some had broken and lay covered in earth and roots piercing the ceiling. The walls were too precisely shaped to belong to a natural cave.

Serena rose slowly. She held her left arm close to her body.

Luca noticed.

“Injured?”

“Bruised.”

“That isn't an answer.”

Serena looked at him.

Luca only then realized he had repeated the words used against him several times since yesterday.

Pip noticed too. One ear lifted.

Serena carefully moved her arm.

“Not broken.”

“Your mana?” Luca asked.

“Enough.”

A crack sounded from the ceiling.

All three looked up.

Several roots snapped. More earth fell, sealing most of the opening above.

The soldiers' voices came faintly from the surface.

“Serena!”

Serena approached the pile of stone.

“We're alive!” she called. “The chamber below is unstable!”

The captain's answer came broken by distance and rock.

“Stay there! We'll dig you out!”

Serena placed her palm against the collapsed wall. Her eyes closed for several seconds.

Then she pulled her hand away.

“Don't!” she shouted. “The ground to the north is cracking too. Move the wagons away from the slope!”

There was a pause before an answer came.

“Is there another way out?”

Pip was already moving along the wall.

He raised one hand, asking for silence.

Then pointed toward a dark passage at the end of the hall.

“There's air.”

Serena looked from the passage to the opening above.

“We'll find another exit!” she shouted. “Give us one bell!”

The captain said something they could not clearly hear.

The earth shifted again.

Serena stepped back from the rubble.

“We move.”

Luca raised a hand and formed a small flame above his palm.

It was not as large as a Fireball. Only a weak, flickering light, enough to help them see several steps ahead.

Pip looked at it.

“Can you maintain that?”

“For a while.”

“What if it goes out?”

“You were the one who said there was air.”

“Air isn't light.”

“Thank you. I know the difference.”

Pip went first.

“Just making sure.”

***

The passage was built from black stone Luca did not recognize.

There was no moss inside. No water dripped from above. The air felt cold and dry, as though the place had been sealed away from the world for a very long time.

Reliefs lined the walls, some worn almost smooth.

Luca raised his flame higher.

A human figure stood in one relief. Beside it were a long-eared figure, a short bearded one, and a smaller figure with broad ears.

Their faces had been destroyed.

But their hands remained.

All of them reached toward the same circle.

Pip stopped before the smaller figure.

“Is that a Goblin?”

Serena came closer.

“Perhaps.”

“You don't sound certain.”

“These ruins aren't on Auregrad's maps.”

“Many things aren't on Auregrad's maps.”

Serena looked at him from the side.

Pip kept staring at the relief.

“I'm only stating what has already been proven.”

Luca closed his mouth before his smile could show.

Serena breathed in through her nose and resumed walking.

“Don't touch the reliefs.”

Pip pulled back the hand that had almost reached the wall.

“I wasn't going to touch it.”

“Your hand was raised.”

“I was looking more closely.”

“With your fingers?”

Pip lowered his hand.

“You're beginning to sound like Luca.”

Luca turned.

“Why did that sound like an insult?”

“Because it was,” both answered at nearly the same time.

Luca stopped walking.

Serena and Pip looked at one another.

For a moment, the old tension between them eased.

Only slightly.

But Luca saw it.

He sighed and followed them.

“Good. You're already starting to work together.”

***

They found the first trap several dozen steps later.

Pip stopped Luca by pressing one hand against his chest.

“Don't move.”

Luca froze.

A narrow line crossed between two floor stones beneath the tip of his boot.

Pip crouched, drew his dagger, and slipped its point into the gap.

“Pressure plate,” he said.

Serena examined both walls.

“What happens if someone steps on it?”

“I can step on it if you want to know.”

“Don't.”

“Then we won't know.”

Luca looked at the small holes in the walls.

“Arrows.”

Pip followed his gaze.

“Perhaps.”

“You don't see the holes?”

“I see them. They could also be spears.”

Luca moved his foot very slowly.

“That doesn't make me feel better.”

Pip sheathed his dagger again.

He marked the stone with a small piece of chalk from his pouch, then stepped across by a safe route.

“Step where I step.”

Serena studied the chalk.

“You carry chalk?”

“I carry useful things.”

His gaze shifted to Serena's document tube.

“You carry reports.”

“That report is why we're here.”

“The ground collapsing is why we're here.”

Luca walked between them.

“We can argue after we get out.”

Pip looked at him.

“You just said we were starting to work together.”

Luca began to regret saying it.

***

The deeper they went, the clearer it became that the ruins were not merely a waterway or storehouse.

They passed through a circular chamber with six doors, all collapsed except one. Lines patterned the floor and met at its center, but the central emblem had split apart.

In the next room, they found the remains of stone tables and empty shelves.

There were no bodies.

No weapons.

No sign that the place had been abandoned in haste.

That was what made it more unsettling.

Pip stopped several times to listen.

On the third occasion, he drew his dagger.

“Something is following us.”

Serena immediately turned.

The passage behind them was empty.

Luca enlarged the flame in his hand. The shadows of the pillars stretched and shifted across the walls.

There was nothing.

“Footsteps?” Serena asked.

Pip shook his head.

“Scraping.”

The sound came again.

Stone grinding against stone.

One of the statues between the pillars moved its head.

Luca dropped the small flame and drew his sword.

The statue's eyes lit faintly.

Its body was smaller than the gate-guarding golems of old stories—only twice a man's height—but its shoulders nearly touched both sides of the passage. One arm formed a stone shield. The other ended in a massive, cracked fist.

“A guardian,” Serena said.

The golem stepped forward.

The corridor trembled.

“Back to the circular chamber!”

They ran.

The golem pursued with heavy steps. Each footfall shook dust from the ceiling.

Pip reached the circular room first and veered aside. Serena stopped in the doorway and stamped her foot against the floor.

A low stone wall rose between them and the golem.

The creature struck it.

Serena's wall shattered in a single blow.

She was driven backward. Her face tightened as she resisted the pain in her arm.

Luca raised his hand.

“Fireball!”

The sphere struck the golem's chest.

The blast filled the doorway with red light.

When the smoke thinned, the golem was still walking.

Its chest had blackened.

Nothing more.

“That wasn't enough,” Serena said.

“I noticed.”

The golem swung its fist.

Luca pulled Serena aside. The stone fist struck where they had stood and left a deep hollow in the floor.

Pip moved behind the golem.

His dagger traced the stone joints along its back, searching for a gap.

He stabbed.

The point entered less than the width of one finger.

The golem turned faster than Pip expected.

Its shield arm swept across the room.

Pip leaped back, but the shield's edge still struck his shoulder. His body was thrown aside and rolled across the floor.

“Pip!”

Luca ran toward him.

Pip was already trying to rise.

“I'm still alive.”

“You always say that as if it's enough.”

“For now, it is.”

The golem approached again.

Serena raised two layers of stone from the floor. The first shattered. The second held the golem's fist for a moment before cracks spread across it.

Serena's breathing grew heavy.

“We can't fight it here.”

Pip looked toward the only open passage.

“There's air coming from there.”

“An exit?” Luca asked.

“Or another hole.”

“I'll take either.”

They retreated into the passage.

Serena was the last to leave the chamber. She collapsed part of the doorway to slow the guardian.

The golem struck the barrier from the other side.

Once.

Twice.

On the third impact, the entire corridor shook.

“That won't last,” Serena said.

Luca saw how pale her face had become.

“How much mana do you have left?”

“Enough.”

“We've discussed that answer.”

Serena stared straight ahead.

“If I say very little, will the exit become closer?”

Luca wanted to object.

But this time Serena was right.

He did not like it.

“Very little,” Serena finally said. “Try not to make me build another wall.”

“I'll ask the golem to cooperate.”

Pip looked back while running.

“You're bad at negotiating.”

“I've only just learned that too.”

***

The passage ended in a vast hall.

Fresh air entered through a high gap in the far wall. Beneath it stood a sealed stone door, but roots and thin daylight showed through its edges.

The way out.

A circular altar stood in the center of the hall.

A piece of pale metal floated a few fingers above its surface. It curved like part of a circle—or a broken crown. Golden light traveled through its engravings, so faint it almost resembled reflected fire.

Luca stopped.

For a moment, the pain in his body left his thoughts.

There was something about that object.

Not a voice.

Nor a pull he could truly feel.

Yet he knew it was waiting for him.

“Don't go closer,” Serena said.

Luca came back to himself.

“I didn't move.”

Pip pointed at his feet.

Luca stood one step closer to the altar than before.

He looked at his boots.

“Fine. Perhaps a little.”

An impact sounded from the passage behind them.

The stone barrier broke.

The golem entered the hall.

The light in its eyes burned brighter now. Black cracks from the Fireball remained on its chest, but its movements had not slowed.

Serena looked toward the exit.

“I'll hold it. You two open the door.”

“No,” Luca said.

“We don't have time to argue.”

“Good. Then we don't need to discuss that terrible plan.”

Serena turned to him. Her jaw tightened.

“This is my duty.”

“Your duty isn't to die in ruins that aren't even on your map.”

“My duty is to ensure both of you return.”

“And mine?”

“You're a Guild adventurer. Take the witness back to Arklune.”

Pip narrowed his eyes.

“The witness can hear you.”

The golem stepped closer.

Serena took position between the guardian and the others.

“Take Pip and go.”

“No.”

“Luca.”

“Stop deciding by yourself who gets left behind.”

For the first time, Luca truly raised his voice at her.

Serena fell silent.

Anger surfaced on her face—not wild, but sharp and controlled.

“If no one holds that creature back, all of us will die.”

“If you stay behind, then we aren't getting out together.”

“You aren't responsible for me.”

Luca tightened his hand around his sword.

“I choose that for myself.”

Pip looked from the golem to the exit.

“Human arguments are remarkably long.”

He drew his dagger.

“If we're going to do something, do it now.”

The golem raised its fist.

Serena struck the floor.

Her final stone wall rose.

The golem's fist shattered it.

Serena fell to one knee.

Luca caught her shoulder before she struck the floor.

That was when the altar's light flared.

There was no explosion.

No thunderous sound.

Only a low note felt more within their chests than heard by their ears.

The metal fragment above the altar began to turn.

The engravings across its surface lit one after another.

Luca looked toward it.

For a fraction of a second, the hall disappeared.

He saw a space without walls.

Shadows stood around a broken circle. They had no faces. No names. Only outstretched hands—not lowered in submission, but reaching toward one another.

A sentence entered his mind.

So ancient that he should not have understood it.

Yet he did.

*One who stands alone has no right to be called sovereign.*

Light stretched from the fragment.

One line reached Luca.

A second reached Serena.

A third reached Pip.

Pip stepped back and raised his dagger.

“Luca.”

“I know.”

“What are you doing?”

“I don't know.”

“That isn't a reassuring answer.”

Then the light touched them.

Luca drew a sharp breath.

He could still see the hall.

But at the same time, he felt Serena's footing against the floor. The pain running through her arm. The remnants of mana spiraling unsteadily beneath her palms.

On the other side, he felt Pip.

Light.

Alert.

His eyes searched for openings between the plates of stone covering the golem. His feet had already chosen a path before his thoughts finished forming it.

Luca could not read their minds.

He could not hear their voices.

He simply knew where they were going to move.

And somehow, they knew the same of him.

The golem attacked.

“Serena!”

Serena did not wait for another instruction.

Both palms struck the floor.

Cracks raced beneath the golem's feet.

Not wide.

Not deep enough to bring it down.

But Luca saw their path as clearly as the flame in his own hand.

He raised his palm.

“Fireball!”

The sphere flew.

Serena tensed when she saw its path. The attack was not aimed at the golem's chest.

It struck the floor.

Straight into the cracks she had created.

The fire did not explode outward.

Red flame flowed inside, following the Earth magic still moving between the stones.

Lines of heat spread beneath the guardian's feet.

The floor turned red.

Then white at its center.

The stone softened.

Not the entire hall.

Only a small circle beneath the golem's feet.

But the guardian's body was too heavy.

Its right foot sank first. It tried to pull free, causing the half-liquid surface to cling to its stone joints. Its left foot shifted and lost purchase.

With a deafening impact, one of the golem's knees struck the floor.

A wave of heat washed over Luca's face.

Serena stared at the molten stone with widened eyes.

“Fire and Earth...”

Luca looked no less confused.

“I only cast Fireball.”

“Later!” Pip shouted.

He was already running.

The golem raised its shield arm to strike him.

Luca moved from the opposite direction.

He had not seen Pip signal.

He did not need to.

His sword struck the blackened crack in the golem's chest. The first impact numbed both his arms. He changed the angle and slashed again.

The outer layer of stone broke.

The golem turned its attack toward Luca.

Before the stone shield could hit him, a foothold rose beneath Luca's boot.

Serena.

Luca pushed himself upward. The golem's shield swept through the empty space below him.

He landed on the guardian's arm, ran two steps, then drove his sword into the gap in its chest armor.

“Now!”

Serena clenched her fist.

The molten stone began to harden around the golem's feet. New cracks climbed from its knee to its waist, locking its body in place for only a few seconds.

On the other side, three blocks of stone rose from the floor.

One.

Two.

Three.

Pip stepped onto the first.

He did not ask why the stones had appeared.

He already knew.

His small body leaped from one foothold to the next. He passed beyond the reach of the golem's arm, landed on its shoulder, then ducked when its stone head tried to knock him away.

Luca twisted his sword.

The armor over the golem's chest opened wider.

Behind the stone lay a pulsing red light.

But the core was too deep to reach from the front.

Pip saw the same joint from behind.

He slipped his dagger into a narrow gap at the base of the golem's neck.

The blade did not pierce the core.

Pip changed his grip and used the dagger as a lever.

The golem roared without sound. Its entire body shuddered as it tried to tear its feet from the hardening stone.

Serena kept both hands against the floor.

Blood seeped between her fingers.

“Hurry!”

Luca forced his sword deeper from the front.

The crack opened.

Pip's dagger slid beneath the red crystal.

Pip clenched his teeth.

Then pulled with all his strength.

The crystal came free.

The light within the golem went out.

For a moment, the creature remained kneeling.

Then its entire body collapsed forward.

Luca pulled out his sword and jumped. Pip released the golem's shoulder just before its stone body struck the floor.

Serena raised one hand.

A small stone platform appeared beneath Pip, turning his fall into a rough roll that did not break any bones.

The golem hit the floor.

The hall shook.

Then fell silent.

***

The light connecting them broke.

Everything Luca had felt vanished at once.

His balance went with it.

He dropped to one knee.

His sword slipped from his hand.

“Luca!”

Serena tried to stand, but nearly fell herself.

Pip reached Luca first. His hand stopped just before touching Luca's shoulder, as though he was not certain the light was no longer dangerous.

“Are you still alive?”

Luca fought down the nausea.

“That's my line.”

“No, it isn't.”

“It should be.”

Pip exhaled, somewhere between relief and irritation.

Behind them, the metal fragment above the altar began turning again.

A single point of light separated from its surface.

It drifted toward Luca before any of them could move.

It touched the back of his right hand.

Heat traveled all the way into his chest.

Luca gritted his teeth.

A faint mark appeared on his skin—an incomplete circle with three lines leading toward its center.

Then the light faded.

The mark remained, pale as an old scar.

The fragment above the altar lost its glow and fell onto the stone surface.

Pip immediately pointed his dagger at it.

“Did that thing just enter your hand?”

Luca stared at the mark.

“A small part of it.”

“That doesn't make it better.”

Serena approached while holding her injured arm.

Her gaze moved from the mark on Luca's hand to the floor that still glowed red.

“What did you do?” she asked.

“I cast Fireball.”

“Fireball doesn't turn the floor into magma.”

“You had already cracked it.”

“Earth magic doesn't direct fire.”

Luca rubbed his forehead.

“If I knew the answer, I would have explained it already.”

Serena studied the remains of the magma for several more moments.

“Dual Art,” she said quietly.

Pip placed the golem's core inside a small pouch.

“What's that?”

“Two spell patterns combined to produce a single result.”

“That sounds simple.”

Serena stared at him.

“It isn't.”

Her answer was so firm that Pip stopped tying the pouch.

“Two casters must understand each other's spell patterns,” Serena continued. “Their mana flows must align. Their timing must be exact. Soldiers who train for years can still fail.”

Luca looked at the floor.

The molten section was no wider than a dining table. It had already begun to harden, leaving uneven black stone around the golem's feet.

“We didn't plan it,” he said.

“That is the problem.”

Serena looked toward the darkened altar.

Pip shifted his gaze between them.

“That light told us how to move.”

“I didn't tell you to,” Luca said.

“I know.”

“Then why are you looking at me like that?”

Pip sheathed his dagger.

“Because that is the worrying part.”

***

The stone door had no handle.

Fortunately, the fallen golem had broken part of the mechanism beneath the floor. Pip found a gap along the wall, while Serena used the remnants of her Earth magic to shift the stone holding it in place.

Luca helped without using magic.

He pushed.

The door moved less than a finger's width.

Luca pushed harder.

His ribs protested.

“Are you certain you're helping?” Pip asked from beside the mechanism.

“I'm pushing.”

“The door doesn't appear impressed.”

“If you have another idea—”

The stone beneath the door shifted under Serena's magic.

The door suddenly opened.

Luca lost his balance and nearly fell outside.

Pip caught the back of his clothes.

“Careful.”

Luca looked back.

“Now you're the one pulling me out of a hole.”

Pip let go.

“Don't make it a habit.”

Beyond the door was a short upward passage. Roots filled its sides, and daylight showed at the end.

They emerged one by one through a gap among the hillside rocks.

The outside air felt painfully bright after the darkness of the ruins.

Luca shielded his eyes with one arm.

Voices called in the distance.

Serena took out a small whistle and blew three times.

An answer came from the direction of the main road.

Before long, two soldiers appeared between the trees. The tension on their faces changed to relief when they saw Serena.

Then confusion when they saw Pip.

Then concern when they saw Luca's condition.

“The captain is waiting on the road,” one of them said. “We thought you had been buried.”

“Almost,” Luca answered.

The soldier looked at the dirt on his clothes, the wound on Serena's arm, and the stone fragments still caught in Pip's cloak.

“What is down there?”

Serena looked toward the cave behind them.

“Ruins. And something that must be reported.”

Pip raised the pouch containing the golem's core.

“Also this.”

Luca looked at his right hand.

The faint mark on his skin was almost invisible beneath the sunlight.

He closed his fist before either soldier could notice it.

They began walking back toward the main road.

Serena led the way, though her steps were slower than they had been that morning. Pip walked in the middle, repeatedly checking that the small pouch at his waist remained closed. Luca followed behind, still trying to understand the emptiness the light had left behind.

None of them realized that they were being watched.

On the hill above, among the shadows of the trees, a woman stood without moving.

A traveling cloak concealed most of her body. The wind shifted the hair beside her face and revealed the tip of a long ear.

The Elf's eyes followed the three figures who had emerged from the ruins.

An Auregrad soldier.

A Goblin with a dagger at his waist.

And a young man who kept glancing at his right hand.

The woman did not call out to them.

She merely stood there and watched until all three disappeared behind the trees.

**End of Chapter 6**
