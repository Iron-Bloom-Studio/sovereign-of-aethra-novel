# Chapter 2: The City Without a Crown

Aven was no longer visible.

Luca knew that because he had already looked back four times.

Maybe five.

All right.

Six.

At first, he could still see the rooftops between the hills.

Then only a thin trace of chimney smoke.

After that, nothing.

Just a dirt road stretching north.

Luca stopped.

The pack on his back seemed to get heavier every hour.

Strange.

It had not felt this heavy last night.

He lowered it beneath a large tree.

Finally.

Luca sat down.

Then opened the pack.

Two sets of clothes.

A blanket.

Bread.

Salted meat.

Bandages.

Flint.

A water flask.

Three apples.

Cheese from Uncle Daren.

Luca stared at the block of cheese.

Why was it this big?

He did not even remember packing it.

Uncle Daren.

Definitely.

Luca lifted the cheese.

Heavy.

Very heavy.

“Is this food or a weapon?”

No one answered.

Of course.

Luca was alone.

Actually alone.

It only felt real now.

In Aven, even when no one else was inside his house, he had never felt completely alone.

There was always some sound.

Roosters.

Children.

Bram’s hammer at the forge.

Someone shouting about grain prices.

Or Daren calling Luca’s name for a job that would “only take a minute.”

Here?

Wind.

Birds.

Leaves.

That was all.

Luca took out his water flask.

Drank.

Then stopped.

He stared at it.

A little more than half remained.

He had only been walking for a few hours.

...

Good.

“First lesson.”

Luca closed the flask.

“Don’t drink like there’s a well beside the house.”

He put it back.

Then looked at the pack.

Then the cheese.

Pack.

Cheese.

No.

He was not giving up.

Not on the first day.

Luca put the cheese back inside.

The pack felt heavier just from looking at it.

A problem for Luca a few hours from now.

***

Footsteps came from the road.

Luca looked up.

Someone was approaching from the north.

A man.

Not particularly old.

Late twenties, maybe.

Maybe thirties.

Hard to tell.

He wore a simple dark travel cloak and boots that looked as though they had seen far more roads than Luca’s.

A sword hung at his waist.

Not a large sword.

No decoration.

Nothing eye-catching.

The man looked at Luca.

Then at his pack.

Then back at Luca.

“Moving house?”

Luca frowned.

“What?”

The man pointed at the pack.

“You’re carrying enough for someone who claims to be traveling.”

Luca looked down at it.

...

Fair.

“Does it look that bad?”

“A little.”

“Good.”

The man smiled faintly.

“Heading to Arklune?”

“Yes.”

“First time?”

Luca looked at him.

“Can everyone tell just by looking?”

“Not everyone.”

“Thank goodness.”

“Only the ones with eyes.”

...

Luca had not decided whether this man was annoying or funny.

Possibly both.

The man walked to the side of the road and sat on a stone.

“New adventurer?”

Luca shook his head.

“Not yet.”

The man raised an eyebrow.

“I’m planning to register when I reach Arklune.”

“Ah.”

He nodded as though Luca had revealed something important.

“So you’re newer than a newbie.”

Luca fell silent.

...

Was there actually a rank below newbie?

“Is that an official category?”

“It is now.”

Yes.

Annoying.

A little funny.

The man took something from a small pouch.

Dried fruit.

He ate it casually.

Luca remained seated a short distance away.

A few seconds passed.

Then Luca asked,

“Been to Arklune?”

“A few times.”

“What’s it like?”

The man thought about it.

“Loud.”

Luca waited.

Nothing else came.

“That’s it?”

“Crowded.”

“Very helpful.”

“And expensive.”

“That sounds more like a city.”

The man chuckled.

Luca pointed toward the sword at his waist.

“You an adventurer?”

“Sometimes.”

“Sometimes?”

“Sometimes a traveler.”

“What’s the difference?”

The man bit into the dried fruit.

“If someone is paying me, usually adventurer.”

Luca thought about that.

Reasonable.

Very reasonable, actually.

“What’s your name?”

The man paused.

Only for a moment.

“Rae.”

“Luca.”

They shook hands.

Rae’s hand was warm.

Strong.

Nothing strange about it.

Just the hand of someone who looked like he had spent a long time on the road.

Rae looked at Luca’s sword.

“Can you use it?”

“A little.”

“Magic?”

“A little of that too.”

“A lot of little.”

“It’s a safe way to explain it.”

Rae smiled.

“Where are you from?”

“Aven.”

Rae tilted his head.

“Farming village in southern Eldoria?”

Luca looked slightly surprised.

“You know it?”

“I’ve passed through many places.”

“Ever been to Aven?”

“No.”

Rae stood.

“But I know the road.”

He looked north.

“Keep following the main road. Before nightfall, you’ll find a roadside lodge near a crossroads.”

Luca stood as well.

“From there to Arklune?”

“Still far.”

“Oh.”

“But caravans usually pass through.”

“Safer to travel with one?”

“More crowded.”

“That wasn’t an answer.”

“Sometimes they’re the same thing.”

Luca was beginning to suspect travelers simply enjoyed talking like that.

Rae lifted his small bag.

Then pointed at Luca’s pack.

“Throw something away.”

Luca stared.

“What?”

“If you don’t, tomorrow you’ll throw it away while crying.”

Luca looked at the pack.

Then at the three apples.

“The apples?”

Rae looked mildly offended.

“Don’t blame the apples.”

Fair.

Luca did not know why, but he suddenly felt the need to apologize to the apples.

Rae started walking south.

“Rae.”

The man looked back.

Luca remembered something.

“If I meet a Dragon on the road, what should I do?”

Rae went quiet.

One second.

Two.

Then he smiled faintly.

“Run.”

Luca nodded seriously.

“That was my answer too.”

“A healthy decision.”

Rae lifted one hand.

Then continued on his way.

A short while later, he disappeared around the bend.

Luca stared after him.

Strange man.

Then he looked at the pack.

Took out an apple.

Bit into it.

“Not your fault.”

***

Rae had been right about the roadside lodge.

By late afternoon, Luca found it at a large crossroads.

The building itself was simple.

The front yard was not.

Carts.

Horses.

Merchants.

Crates.

People.

A lot of people.

Luca stopped just outside the yard.

A Dwarf was arguing with a Human merchant over the price of iron.

Near the stable, an Elf woman knelt beside the wheel of her own wagon.

Two Beastfolk carried a large crate while speaking too quickly for Luca to follow.

On the other side of the yard, three Human travelers sat together.

Not far from them, two Orcs ate in silence.

No one was fighting.

But no one was really mixing either.

Luca watched that a little longer than he should have.

An Elf passed the Humans’ table without looking at them.

One of the Orcs stood to let a Human merchant pass, and the merchant thanked him from a distance that felt slightly too far.

Strange.

Arklune was not even in sight yet.

But the road to Arklune already felt different from Aven.

Busier.

More diverse.

And somehow, more distant.

Luca stood there too long.

Someone bumped his shoulder.

“If you want to sightsee, stand to the side.”

“Oh. Sorry.”

The person kept walking.

Did not look back.

Did not know Luca.

Did not care.

Luca moved aside.

In Aven, someone who bumped into him would probably say:

Luca, help me with something.

Or:

Luca, come by the house later.

Or at least:

Luca, why are you standing in the road like an idiot?

Here?

He was just a stranger.

Odd.

Luca smiled faintly.

Kind of nice.

***

He eventually found a caravan heading for Arklune.

Its leader was an older merchant named Halven.

Large belly.

Larger voice.

“You can fight?”

“A little.”

Halven sighed.

Luca was beginning to think he should stop saying “a little.”

“Sword?”

“Yes.”

“Magic?”

“Yes.”

Halven looked at him.

“Good.”

Luca was slightly surprised.

Finally.

Praise.

“If something happens, don’t hide under the wagon.”

...

Not praise after all.

“What if it’s something really bad?”

Halven thought for a moment.

“Find the strongest wagon.”

Luca was not sure if that was a joke.

Better not to test it.

***

The caravan left the next morning.

Seven wagons.

Fourteen merchants and workers.

Five guards.

And Luca.

For the first hour, Luca walked quickly.

Back straight.

Pack neat.

Like a real adventurer.

Two hours later?

Not so much.

Three hours later?

Luca was beginning to hate Uncle Daren’s cheese.

One of the guards walked beside him.

A lean man carrying a spear.

His name was Corvin.

“First long journey?”

Luca glanced over.

“The boots too?”

Corvin looked down.

“Your boots still look too good.”

“I’m starting to dislike comments about my boots.”

“Give it three days.”

Luca looked at them.

Already covered in dust.

If this still counted as good, he did not want to know what three days would look like.

Corvin pointed at his sword.

“You joining the Guild?”

“Yes.”

“Rank?”

“I’m not even registered yet.”

Corvin nodded.

“Good.”

“Why is that good?”

“No bad habits yet.”

Luca looked at him.

“All adventurers have bad habits?”

“The ones who live long enough.”

...

Again.

An answer that sounded wise without actually being helpful.

Luca was beginning to suspect this was part of adventurer training.

***

By late afternoon, the road entered a wooded area.

The trees grew denser.

The shadows grew longer.

The wagons slowed.

The guards also began watching the brush more often.

Luca did the same.

“Monsters common here?”

Corvin shrugged.

“Sometimes.”

“Forest Boar?”

“Wolves more often.”

Luca almost laughed.

Of course.

Why not three boars and a dragon while they were at it?

The brush moved.

Corvin immediately raised a hand.

The caravan stopped.

The mood changed.

Fast.

Merchants moved closer to the wagons.

The guards took position.

Luca drew his sword.

Three shapes emerged between the trees.

Forest Wolves.

Larger than ordinary dogs.

Dark gray fur.

Long jaws.

Eyes that did not look afraid of humans at all.

One stood in the middle of the road.

The other two spread out.

Corvin looked at Luca.

“Don’t chase.”

“I know.”

“All newbies say that.”

“I’m not a newbie yet.”

Corvin thought about it.

“True.”

Luca nodded.

“Worse.”

...

Thank you.

The first wolf attacked.

One guard caught it with a shield.

Corvin thrust his spear.

The wolf backed away.

The second appeared from the right side of the wagon.

A horse whinnied.

Luca saw it.

Too late.

No.

Not this time.

He raised a hand.

Draw Aethra.

Shape it.

“Fireball!”

Flame shot forward.

Not at the wolf.

At the ground directly in front of it.

The wolf jumped back.

Good.

At least this time it had not flown over the monster’s head.

Luca moved forward.

Sword raised.

The wolf growled.

Luca did not attack.

Did not chase.

He simply forced it away from the horse.

The monster moved left.

Luca followed.

Right.

Luca followed again.

Then something flashed past Luca.

Corvin’s spear.

One quick thrust.

The wolf yelped and retreated into the woods.

Luca exhaled.

“Good,” Corvin said.

Luca looked over.

Really?

Finally?

Then another guard dropped the third wolf in only two movements.

Two.

Luca barely saw it happen.

Oh.

Right.

Still a long way to go.

The fight ended in less than a minute.

No cheering.

No one raised a sword in triumph.

The guards simply checked the area.

Made sure there were no more wolves.

Then told the caravan to move.

Just like that.

Luca sheathed his sword.

Corvin walked beside him.

“Fire affinity?”

“Yes.”

“Only Fire?”

“Light too.”

Corvin looked over.

“Light?”

“I can use Lesser Heal.”

“Hm.”

Luca waited.

“Hm what?”

“Not bad.”

“That sounded like a very small compliment.”

“It was.”

Luca sighed.

But he took it.

Corvin pointed behind them, toward where the wolf had vanished.

“The good part wasn’t your Fireball.”

“Oh.”

Luca was not sure he wanted the rest.

“You stopped.”

Luca frowned.

“Stopped?”

“You didn’t chase.”

Luca looked toward the woods.

“Why would I?”

Corvin smiled.

“Exactly.”

He patted Luca’s shoulder.

“Newbies die because they think a retreating monster is a defeated monster.”

Luca remembered the Forest Boars in Aven.

No one had chased them either.

He had never thought of that as anything special.

Maybe Aven had taught him more than he realized.

***

That night, they stopped at a small roadside post.

A long wooden building.

A stable behind it.

A large fire in the yard.

Luca sat with several travelers holding a bowl of soup.

Warm.

Not too salty.

Already better than Aven’s inn.

Don’t tell the innkeeper.

A loud voice came from the doorway.

“No.”

Luca looked over.

The owner stood with his arms crossed.

In front of him were two small travelers.

Goblin.

Luca had seen Goblins before.

Not often.

But enough to know these two were not wild monsters that had wandered into a settlement.

They wore travel clothes.

Carried bags.

One held coins.

“We pay,” said the older Goblin.

“I said no.”

“There are rooms.”

“Not for you.”

Luca watched.

Several others did too.

No one moved.

The Goblin raised his coins.

“Why?”

The owner snorted.

“Find somewhere else.”

Luca set down his bowl.

Corvin looked at him.

“Don’t.”

Luca turned.

“What?”

“Not your problem.”

Luca looked back toward the door.

The Goblin was still holding the money.

“They’re paying.”

“Luca.”

“If it was full, I’d understand.”

Corvin rubbed his forehead.

Luca stood.

He walked over.

The owner looked at him.

“What?”

Luca pointed at the coins in the Goblin’s hand.

“They want to pay.”

“And?”

“You said there are still rooms.”

“So?”

Luca was becoming confused.

Wasn’t this fairly simple?

“If there’s room, why not?”

The owner narrowed his eyes.

“You know them?”

“No.”

“Then why interfere?”

Luca stopped.

Good question.

He thought about it.

Found no complicated answer.

“Because I don’t understand the problem.”

The owner gave a short laugh.

“They’re Goblins.”

Luca looked at the two Goblins.

Yes.

Obviously.

He looked back at the man.

“Right.”

The owner waited.

Luca waited too.

...

Was he missing something?

“So?”

“So what?”

Someone behind Luca tried not to laugh.

The owner was starting to look irritated.

“Village boy.”

“Maybe.”

“If you don’t like my rules, you can sleep outside too.”

Luca considered it.

Not too cold.

He had a blanket.

“Fine.”

Corvin covered his face with one hand.

The owner blinked.

“What?”

“If they have to sleep outside just because they’re Goblins, I can sleep outside too.”

Luca picked up his bowl again.

“There’s a fire, at least.”

Silence.

Then a Dwarf from the caravan laughed loudly.

“Idiot.”

He stood.

“But I like him.”

Others began to murmur.

Not many.

But enough.

The owner looked across the yard.

Then at the number of customers.

Then at the two Goblins.

He clicked his tongue.

“One room in the back.”

The older Goblin nodded.

“Enough.”

Coins changed hands.

Problem solved.

Luca returned to his seat.

Corvin stared at him for a long moment.

“What?”

“You always like this?”

“Like what?”

“Walking into problems that aren’t yours.”

Luca took a spoonful of soup.

“He was paying.”

“That wasn’t my question.”

“That was my answer.”

Corvin went quiet.

Then laughed softly.

“The Guild is going to love you.”

Luca looked up.

“Really?”

“No.”

Right.

Should have known.

***

The next day, the road grew busier.

Wagons came from the north.

Caravans from the east.

Adventurers.

Merchants.

Craftsmen.

Travelers.

The closer they came to Arklune, the more races Luca saw.

But the same pattern remained.

Humans mostly walked with Humans.

Elves more often traveled with other Elves or alone.

Orcs usually stayed with their own groups.

Dwarves seemed far more willing to speak with anyone as long as business was involved.

There were no obvious laws keeping them apart.

No guards dividing them.

But there was an invisible line almost everyone seemed to know.

Luca did not know who had drawn that line.

Maybe no one person had.

Maybe it had been there for so long that people had simply stopped asking.

Then the road began to climb.

Halven, walking ahead, pointed.

“Look.”

Luca moved to the side of the wagon.

Reached the crest.

And stopped.

“Oh.”

That was all that came out.

Arklune.

The city spread across the distance.

Stone walls surrounded it.

Towers rose at intervals along the fortifications.

A great road led toward the main gate.

Wagons and travelers flowed toward the city in a long stream.

Beyond the walls?

Roofs.

So many roofs.

Multi-story buildings.

Forge smoke.

A clock tower.

Banners.

And a large building near the center of the city bearing an emblem of a sword and wings.

Luca pointed.

“That...?”

Corvin followed his finger.

“Adventurers’ Guild.”

Luca fell silent.

His father’s stories.

At last, one of them stood in front of him.

Real.

Not a story.

Not something built in his imagination.

Real.

And far larger than he had expected.

“Still want to be an adventurer?” Corvin asked.

Luca looked at the city.

“Yes.”

The answer came faster than the thought.

Corvin smiled faintly.

“Good.”

This time, Luca chose not to ask whether that was praise.

***

Arklune’s gate was chaos.

That was Luca’s conclusion after less than a minute.

Guards inspected cargo.

Merchants shouted.

Horses whinnied.

People argued.

A wagon nearly ran him over.

Luca moved aside.

Then nearly walked into someone else.

“Sorry.”

No answer.

Once through the gate, everything became worse.

Or better.

Luca had not decided.

Weapon shops.

Apothecaries.

Inns.

Forges.

Food stalls.

Guild boards.

Adventurers.

People of different races walked the same streets.

Not together.

But close enough to see each other.

Close enough to trade.

Close enough not to trust each other.

Luca saw a Human merchant speaking with a Dwarf about metal crates.

Across the street, two Elves passed a group of Orcs without greeting them.

No shouting.

No drawn weapons.

But the space between them felt different.

Luca had no word for it.

So he kept walking.

Bread.

Metal.

Horses.

Spices.

Smoke.

Too much at once.

Luca stopped.

No.

Do not stop in the middle of the road again.

Lesson learned.

He moved aside.

Halven pointed toward the market.

“The caravan stops east.”

Luca nodded.

“Thanks for letting me travel with you.”

Halven grunted.

“You weren’t too troublesome.”

Luca smiled.

“I’ll take that as a compliment.”

“Don’t.”

Of course.

Corvin patted his shoulder.

“Guild’s two streets from here.”

“Which way?”

“Follow the blue banners.”

“And if I get lost?”

Corvin considered it.

“Don’t become an adventurer.”

Luca laughed.

They parted ways.

***

The Adventurers’ Guild was larger than Luca had imagined.

Its wooden doors stood wide open.

People moved in and out constantly.

Adventurers in full armor.

Mages carrying staffs.

Archers.

Parties made up mostly of the same race.

There were a few mixed groups, but usually only two races, and they were rare enough to draw glances.

Luca saw a Human and Dwarf walking side by side without issue.

Nearby, an Elf stood alone reading the quest board.

An Orc submitted a report at another counter while two Human adventurers behind him kept a little more distance than necessary.

No one was forbidden.

But the past clearly had not disappeared.

A man carried a monster head Luca preferred not to identify.

A large board covered in papers hung outside.

Quests?

Probably.

Luca stood before the entrance.

Took a breath.

Then stepped inside.

Sound hit him immediately.

Conversation.

Laughter.

Mugs.

Coins.

People arguing about rewards.

Someone on the far side of the hall was shouting that the monster he had killed was “obviously larger” than the Guild record claimed.

Luca had no idea where to go.

He stood there too long.

Again.

“If you’re registering, counter three.”

An adventurer passed beside him.

Luca turned.

“Thanks.”

The man was already gone.

Counter three.

Right.

Luca had just started forward when a short laugh came from the upper floor.

Not loud.

But close enough.

Luca looked up.

A man with graying hair stood on an interior balcony speaking with two staff members.

His posture was still straight like a former soldier.

His expression was flat.

Far too flat for someone who had apparently just made one of the staff laugh.

“Guildmaster, that wasn’t funny,” said the other staff member.

The man nodded.

“I know.”

“Then why say it?”

He thought for a moment.

“Garling says I should relax more.”

The two staff members exchanged a look.

One tried not to smile.

The man let out a small sigh and walked away.

Luca watched him for a few seconds.

Guildmaster?

For some reason, Luca had imagined the Guildmaster would look much more frightening.

That man looked...

Stiff.

But not frightening.

At least, not yet.

“Counter three,” someone reminded him from behind.

“Oh. Right.”

Luca moved quickly.

A young woman sat behind the desk.

Her hair was tied neatly.

A stack of forms lay in front of her.

She did not look up.

“Name.”

“Luca.”

“Family name?”

Luca paused.

“None.”

The pen moved.

“Origin?”

“Aven Village. Eldoria.”

The pen stopped briefly.

“Aven?”

“Yes.”

“South?”

“Yes.”

The pen moved again.

“Age?”

“Eighteen.”

“Guild experience?”

Luca opened his mouth.

Forest Boar?

No.

Not a quest.

Forest Wolf?

Also not a quest.

...

This was starting to sound bad.

“None.”

“Monster subjugation contract?”

“None.”

“Escort contract?”

“No.”

“Dungeon?”

“No.”

“Party experience?”

Luca liked the direction of this less and less.

“No.”

The clerk finally looked up.

Luca felt the need to defend himself.

“I helped a caravan fight Forest Wolves yesterday.”

“Under Guild contract?”

“No.”

“Then it doesn’t count.”

“Oh.”

The pen moved again.

The scratching across the paper sounded strangely judgmental.

“Weapon?”

“Short sword.”

“Magic?”

“Fire and Light.”

The clerk paused.

This time, she actually looked at him.

“Spells?”

“Fireball. Lesser Heal.”

She wrote something.

“Formal training?”

“No.”

“Teacher?”

“My father taught me basic swordsmanship. Then a former soldier in Aven.”

“Magic training?”

“Only the basics.”

The pen stopped.

The clerk took another sheet.

“Practical test tomorrow morning.”

Luca blinked.

“Tomorrow?”

“Yes.”

“I’m not registered immediately?”

“No.”

“Oh.”

The woman looked at him.

“Disappointed?”

“A little.”

“Good.”

Luca was becoming suspicious.

“Why?”

“Means you still have expectations left to crush.”

...

Right.

Everyone outside Aven really did talk like this.

The clerk pushed a sheet toward him.

“Bring this tomorrow.”

Luca took it.

“If I pass?”

“Official registration.”

“And then?”

“Starting rank.”

“And then?”

“Quests.”

Luca looked at the paper.

Simple.

No light.

No magical symbol appearing.

Just paper.

Still, his fingers tightened around it.

Tomorrow.

The clerk looked past Luca.

“Next.”

“Oh. Right.”

Luca stepped aside.

He stood in the middle of the hall.

Adventurers moved past him.

Quests were discussed.

Coins changed hands.

Monster parts were placed on tables.

Someone laughed far too loudly.

From the upper floor, Luca caught another glimpse of the Guildmaster crossing the balcony.

Same expression.

Same measured steps.

Stiff.

But there was something in the way the staff around him acted that made Luca feel the man had been here for a very long time.

Luca did not think much more about it.

There was no reason to.

Not yet.

No one knew who Luca was.

No one cared that he had just left a small village called Aven.

No one knew about the Fireball that had almost missed.

The pack that was too heavy.

His first journey.

Good.

Luca looked at the paper in his hand.

Tomorrow, he would be tested.

If he passed, he would become an adventurer.

A real adventurer.

Not a legend.

Not yet.

Not anyone important.

Not even close.

Just Luca.

An eighteen-year-old from Aven who had just stepped into a city without a crown.

And for now?

That was enough.

**End of Chapter 2**
