# Chapter 7: Strangers at the Ruins

Luca had already used Lesser Heal on himself.

Once, after leaving the ruins.

Faint light wrapped around his palm, closing the scratches on his arm and easing the swelling in his shoulder.

His ribs still hurt.

He had considered using it again.

The Guild Healer had forbidden it.

“Lesser Heal helps with minor wounds and speeds up natural recovery,” the Healer said. “It does not fix everything beneath the skin.”

“The pain has already gone down.”

“Less pain does not mean the injury is gone.”

The Healer pressed Luca’s side.

Pain shot all the way through his back.

Luca’s body went rigid. His eyes widened. His mouth opened, but for several seconds no sound came out.

The Healer removed his hand.

“Like that.”

Luca took a short breath.

“I understand.”

“Good. Because I have not pressed the worst part yet.”

The Healer’s hand moved toward his ribs again.

Luca immediately caught his wrist.

The Healer raised an eyebrow.

“What are you doing?”

“Protecting the patient.”

“You are the patient.”

“That is why I am taking this very seriously.”

The Healer looked at the hand still gripping his wrist.

Luca released him at once.

“Sorry.”

“Lie down.”

“I feel much better already.”

“Your expression just said otherwise.”

Luca lay down without arguing.

After that, he was examined again.

Twice.

In his opinion, once should have been enough.

According to Rolan, the medical judgment of someone who returned from a herb-gathering quest injured by a Hobgoblin, fell into a ruin, and was nearly crushed by a golem could not be trusted.

It was difficult to argue with him.

Especially because all of it was true.

Now Luca sat on a bench near the Guild entrance with fresh bandages wrapped around his ribs. Lesser Heal had taken care of the minor wounds, but his body still remembered every impact it had suffered since yesterday.

His right hand rested on his knee.

The mark shaped like an incomplete circle was still visible on the back of it.

Faint.

Almost like an old scar.

He rubbed it with his thumb.

Nothing happened.

No light.

No voice.

No sense of knowing where someone else was about to move.

Just skin.

“If you keep rubbing it, the mark is not going to feel guilty and leave.”

Pip sat at the other end of the bench, turning the small pouch containing the golem core in his hands.

Luca stopped rubbing his hand.

“I wasn’t expecting that.”

“You have been doing it for a while.”

“I was thinking.”

“With your hand?”

Luca looked at Pip.

Pip returned the look with a flat expression.

“Why is everyone starting to say that?”

“Because you often do things in strange ways.”

Luca was about to answer when the Guild door opened.

Serena entered with the captain of the Auregrad caravan.

Her arm had been bandaged, but her posture remained straight. The travel coat bearing Auregrad’s insignia still sat neatly on her shoulders.

Luca noticed the part of the coat torn inside the ruins.

Even the damage looked more orderly than Luca’s clothes.

The captain walked directly to Rolan’s desk. Serena followed.

Pip slipped the pouch containing the golem core beneath his cloak.

“This is probably about us.”

“Why do you think that immediately?”

Pip pointed with his chin.

“He looked at you.”

The captain had indeed looked at Luca.

Not for long.

But long enough.

Luca sighed and stood.

His ribs immediately reminded him that standing too quickly was a bad decision.

His face tightened. His hand almost moved to clutch his side, but he stopped it halfway.

Pip noticed.

Of course he did.

“Feeling much better?” he asked.

Luca stared at him.

Pip put on an innocent expression.

Luca did not answer.

They approached the Guildmaster’s desk.

Rolan was reading Serena’s report. Three sheets already lay in front of him.

Serena’s handwriting was small, straight, and neatly arranged.

Luca could not imagine how anyone could write that much after returning from the ruins.

He only wanted to sleep.

Rolan finished one section before looking up.

“How is the caravan?”

“The replacement axle has been installed,” the captain answered. “We will continue before midday.”

Luca looked at Serena.

“And her?”

Serena turned toward him.

“I am standing here.”

“I noticed.”

“Then why did you ask?”

Luca paused.

Good question.

The captain hid something close to a smile behind a cough.

“Serena will not continue with us.”

Serena did not look surprised.

Luca felt the opposite.

“Why?”

Rolan shifted the report across the desk.

“Because she is the only member of the Auregrad caravan who entered the ruins.”

“And the only one who witnessed what happened inside,” the captain continued. “The discovery of an unrecorded structure, a magical guardian, an unidentified relic, and an unprepared Dual Art must be investigated.”

Luca raised his right hand slightly.

“The Dual Art part was an accident.”

The captain looked at him.

“That is precisely why it must be investigated.”

Luca lowered his hand.

Right.

Reasonable.

He was beginning to dislike reasonable things.

The captain faced Serena.

“Until further orders, you are assigned to Arklune as Auregrad’s liaison. Cooperate with the Guild. Record every finding and send your reports through the military post.”

Serena straightened.

“Understood.”

There was no hesitation in her answer.

But when the captain handed her a new document tube, Serena’s fingers stopped for a moment before accepting it.

Only a moment.

Her caravan would leave.

She would remain alone in a foreign city with a new adventurer and a Goblin who had only just received a visitor’s permit.

Luca began to understand why her expression seemed slightly stiffer than usual.

The captain lowered his voice.

“This is not a punishment.”

“I do not consider it one.”

“I know.”

Serena accepted the tube.

The captain gave Rolan a brief salute and turned toward the door. Before leaving, he stopped in front of Serena.

“Send your first report before the end of the week.”

“Understood.”

“And do not try to hold back another golem by yourself.”

Serena’s jaw tightened.

“That was not part of my plan.”

One of Pip’s ears rose.

“I think that was exactly the plan.”

Serena stared at him.

The captain finally smiled faintly.

Then he left.

Serena watched his back until the door closed.

Luca did not speak immediately.

Neither did Pip.

After a moment, Serena looked at them.

“What?”

“Nothing,” Luca answered.

“You look as if you want to say something.”

“I’m only thinking.”

Pip looked at Luca’s hand.

“But his hand is still this time.”

Luca ignored him.

“So you’re staying in Arklune?”

“Temporarily.”

“And working with us?”

“I am working with the Guild.”

Pip nodded.

“So, with us.”

“Those are not the same thing.”

“Will you return to the ruins?”

“Yes.”

“With us?”

Serena fell silent.

Pip waited.

Luca decided not to interfere.

Serena took a slow breath.

“Yes.”

Pip turned to Luca.

“The same thing.”

The corner of Serena’s eye twitched.

Rolan tapped one finger against the desk.

“If you are finished establishing a command structure without my permission, there are several matters we need to discuss.”

They immediately fell silent.

***

The Guild prohibited anyone from entering the ruins until the surrounding route had been declared safe.

That was the good news.

The bad news was that confirming the route’s safety was their job.

Rolan was not sending them back inside. They were only meant to mark the exit, inspect the slope’s stability, and put up boundary ropes so no traveler would fall through the same hole.

A simple task.

At least, that was what Rolan called it.

Luca was beginning to distrust tasks described as simple.

They left the following morning.

Serena carried a map, a document tube, two coils of rope, and equipment arranged so neatly that Luca felt his own pack had done something shameful.

Pip carried chalk, his dagger, several strips of cloth, and the pouch containing the golem core.

Luca carried his sword, food, and more bandages than he believed necessary.

Rolan had packed those.

They followed the same route as the day before. The caravan’s wheel tracks remained visible, but the Auregrad convoy was gone.

Serena looked at the tracks several times.

She said nothing.

Neither did Luca.

Pip walked in front.

When they reached the stone crossroads, he crouched and examined the ground.

“No new tracks from the Hobgoblin group.”

“The old ones?” Luca asked.

“Still here, but they’re fading.”

“So they didn’t return?”

“It means they did not return by this road.”

Luca nodded.

The difference mattered.

He was learning.

The journey to the hill passed without trouble. No Hobgoblins. No collapsing ground. No golems emerging from the trees.

Luca almost felt relieved that everything was proceeding normally.

Then an explosion sounded from beyond the hill.

Birds burst from the trees.

Pip drew his dagger immediately.

Serena dropped the coils of rope and placed one hand on the ground.

Luca drew his sword.

“Smoke?” he asked.

Pip raised his head.

“No.”

A second sound followed.

Sharper.

Like something cutting through the air.

A small tree beyond the slope shook violently even though the morning wind was almost still.

Serena was already running.

Luca and Pip followed.

They pushed through the brush, descended a root-covered slope, and reached an open patch of ground on the northern side of the ruins.

Two figures stood there.

The first was an Elf woman.

Pale-blond hair flowed past her back, moving with the wind circling around her. White-and-green traveling clothes covered her body, fitted with belts and small pouches that looked more suited to research tools than weapons.

But the staff in her hands was clearly more than a walking stick.

Its shaft was formed from twisted wood. Branches curved around a red crystal at its crown.

She was the woman Luca had seen on the hill yesterday.

The woman who had watched them emerge from the ruins.

Before her stood a tall male figure covered in black plates.

At first, Luca thought he was wearing armor.

But there were no straps.

No buckles.

No seams where armor met clothing.

The dark plates moved with his breathing.

It was not armor.

It was his body.

Black carapace covered his arms, shoulders, legs, and most of his head. Lighter plates formed a pattern across his chest and abdomen, while a pair of red eyes watched the Elf woman before him.

His hands were empty.

No sword.

No spear.

No traveling equipment.

Serena stopped abruptly.

“Hiveborn.”

Her voice held no hatred.

But her entire body immediately became alert.

The Hiveborn stood sideways, his body positioned between the Elf and a formation of half-buried stones behind them.

Thin darkness flowed around one of his hands.

The crystal on the Elf’s staff glowed faintly.

“I do not intend to hurt you,” she said.

Her voice was gentle and calm.

She did not aim the staff directly at him, but neither did she lower it.

“But I need to make sure you do not approach the ruins.”

“I was not trying to enter,” the Hiveborn replied.

“Then remain there until we understand the situation.”

“I do not understand the situation.”

“That is why I asked you to stop.”

Though her voice remained soft, the wind around her staff strengthened.

The Hiveborn lowered his center of gravity.

His hands took position as if gripping something long.

But nothing was there.

His body had assumed a fighting stance before his mind had decided to.

“Stop!” Luca shouted.

Both of them glanced over.

Only slightly.

It was enough for something behind the stones to move.

One of the slabs Luca had mistaken for part of a wall rose from the ground. Mud and roots fell from its surface.

Red light flared behind a crack in the stone.

Another guardian.

It was smaller than the golem they had fought inside, but one arm ended in a long stone blade.

The blade rose behind the Elf woman.

The Hiveborn moved first.

“Down.”

He ran toward her with one hand raised.

Darkness gathered between his carapaced fingers.

“Dark Bolt.”

From the Elf’s position, an unknown Hiveborn had suddenly charged her with Dark magic in his hand.

She turned her staff by reflex.

“Gust.”

The red crystal flashed.

Wind struck the ground between them.

The Hiveborn was thrown aside. His Dark Bolt missed its intended target and struck only the stone guardian’s shoulder.

He rolled, clawed the ground with his fingers, and rose again.

“I was not attacking you.”

The Elf finally noticed the stone figure behind her.

Her eyes widened for a moment.

But her staff remained raised.

“Then lower your hand.”

“That thing is still moving.”

“I can see it now.”

“You did not see it before.”

A trace of guilt appeared on the Elf’s face.

Not enough to make her lower her guard.

The stone guardian raised its blade again.

The Hiveborn stepped forward.

The Elf turned her staff.

A blade of wind formed around the crystal.

Neither seemed certain whether to face the guardian or each other.

Serena stamped her foot.

A stone wall rose between them.

The Wind Blade struck one side.

The Dark Bolt struck the other.

Cracks spread across the wall.

Serena clenched her hand.

The wall thickened before either spell broke through.

“Enough!” she shouted.

The Elf lowered her staff immediately.

The Hiveborn did not.

His gaze moved past Serena toward the guardian raising its blade once more.

“Behind you.”

Serena turned.

The guardian struck the wall from the side.

Stone shattered.

Most of it collapsed toward Serena and the Elf woman.

Serena raised both hands, but her footing shifted when the ground cracked beneath her.

Luca ran toward Serena.

At the same time, the Hiveborn moved toward the Elf.

He did not raise Dark magic.

Nor did he try to attack her.

He turned and placed his back between her and the falling stone.

Several fragments bounced off his carapace.

One larger piece struck his shoulder.

His body lurched half a step.

But he remained standing.

The Elf stared at his back with widened eyes.

“You—”

“Later.”

He raised his still-empty hands.

Once again, his stance resembled that of someone preparing to use a long weapon that was not there.

“The guardian has not stopped.”

The stone guardian pulled its blade free from the broken wall.

Pip was already circling to the right.

“The rear joint is exposed!”

Luca saw the spot he meant.

A gap behind the guardian’s knee.

Not the core.

Only a joint that had not been completely covered by stone and earth.

Serena saw it too.

A stone platform rose in front of Pip.

He stepped onto it without asking.

His body shot upward.

The Elf moved her staff by reflex.

Wind caught Pip’s back and sent him higher than Serena had expected.

Pip’s ears flattened at once.

“A little warning!”

“Sorry!”

Pip twisted in the air.

His dagger entered the joint behind the guardian’s knee.

The blade was too short to sever the entire mechanism, but it was enough to jam the leg when the guardian tried to step.

The guardian lost its balance.

Its stone blade turned toward Pip.

The Hiveborn raised one hand.

Darkness formed a thin layer around the guardian’s arm joint.

The red light there dimmed.

The blade slowed.

It did not stop.

But Pip had enough time to release his dagger and drop.

Luca moved to catch him.

At least, that was the plan.

Pip landed with both feet on Luca’s shoulders.

His knee struck the side of Luca’s head.

Both of them fell.

Pip rolled first.

Luca landed on his back.

The morning sky looked very peaceful.

It did not fit the situation.

“Sorry,” Pip said as he stood.

Luca held his head.

“A little warning.”

“I was falling.”

“That is not an excuse.”

“That is an extremely clear excuse.”

The stone guardian tried to pull its leg free.

Pip’s dagger remained lodged in the joint.

Serena clenched her hand.

Earth closed around the guardian’s leg from both sides.

The Elf lowered the tip of her staff.

The red crystal gave off a gentle glow.

Water flowed from a small pouch at her waist and spiraled with the staff’s movement. The stream wrapped around the exposed leg joint.

It was not strong enough to break stone.

But it washed the mud and grit from the gap.

A red light became visible behind the cracked plates.

Not the main core.

Only one of the magical channels controlling the leg.

“There,” she said.

Her voice remained calm even as her breathing grew heavier.

Luca rose and raised his hand.

“Fireball!”

The fireball struck the exposed joint.

The explosion was not large.

But the heat shattered the already cracked stone from within.

The guardian’s leg collapsed.

Its body tilted.

The stone blade swung wildly as the creature struggled to balance.

Luca saw its path.

Serena was too close.

The Elf had not lowered her staff.

Pip was pulling at his dagger.

The Hiveborn stood between them all.

Heat rose from the mark on Luca’s hand.

Only for a moment.

The world did not vanish as it had inside the ruins.

There was no golden light.

No ancient voice.

But suddenly Luca knew where they were.

Serena was about to raise the ground.

Pip would move left.

The Elf was shaping wind around her staff.

And the Hiveborn was preparing to take the guardian’s blade with his own body.

Luca had no time to wonder how he knew.

“Don’t block it!” he shouted.

The Hiveborn looked toward him.

“Down!”

Serena struck the ground.

The earth beneath the Hiveborn dropped half a step.

The guardian’s blade passed over his head.

At the same moment, the Elf swung her staff. Wind struck the side of the weapon and changed its path.

Pip jumped left.

He pulled his dagger from the guardian’s joint as he turned.

The guardian’s side opened.

Luca drew his sword.

He ran toward the stone platform Serena had raised.

One step.

Two.

He jumped.

His sword struck the red channel in the guardian’s side.

Not deep enough.

Luca twisted the hilt and pushed with both hands.

Cracks spread.

From the other side, a Dark Bolt struck the same point.

The red light went out.

The stone guardian stopped moving.

Its body collapsed.

Luca pulled his sword free and jumped back.

This time, there was no stone platform waiting beneath his feet.

He landed badly, lost his balance, and fell onto the ground.

The impact ran through his ribs.

Luca’s expression changed at once.

One hand clutched his side. His eyes squeezed shut as he waited for the pain to fade.

Pip approached.

“Are you still alive?”

Luca opened one eye.

“That is my line.”

“I am asking it now.”

“I’m alive.”

Pip nodded.

“Your expression says otherwise.”

Luca was beginning to regret ever meeting the Guild Healer.

Dust drifted around them.

Silence.

Serena still held one palm against the ground.

The Elf remained with her staff raised.

The Hiveborn stood before her despite the stone that had struck his shoulder.

No one moved for several seconds.

Pip was the first to speak again.

“Do all ruins have guardians?”

Serena exhaled.

“No.”

“So far, two out of two.”

“That is not a large enough sample to reach a conclusion.”

The Elf slowly lowered her staff.

“Technically, he is right.”

Serena turned toward her.

Pip lifted his chin slightly.

Luca was still sitting on the ground.

“Could we discuss conclusions after confirming there isn’t a third one?”

No one argued.

That was concerning enough.

***

They searched the stone formation and the surrounding ground.

There were no other guardians.

Pip found three similar slabs, but all of them were damaged and showed no sign of moving.

Once the area was reasonably safe, Serena stood between the two strangers again.

“Names.”

Her tone made the word sound like a military command.

The Elf placed one hand against her chest.

“Lysara.”

Her voice remained gentle.

She slowly reached into a pouch beneath her cloak so the movement would not be misunderstood, then removed a thin wooden tube.

“I came from Sylvaris under orders from Eldren, head of the scholars. My task is to locate and document ruins mentioned in the old archives.”

She handed a letter to Serena.

Serena opened and read it.

Luca tried to look from the side.

The writing was even smaller than Serena’s.

He could not read a single word.

Pip tried to look as well.

“Can you read it?” Luca whispered.

“No.”

“Then why are you looking?”

“There might be a picture.”

There was no picture.

Serena inspected the seal at the bottom of the letter.

“The document appears genuine.”

“It is genuine,” Lysara replied.

“You were watching us yesterday.”

Lysara looked at Luca.

So she knew he had seen her.

“I saw you emerge from a gap on the southern side of the hill. You were injured and soldiers were coming to meet you. I chose not to approach.”

Luca stood slowly.

This time, he did it very carefully.

Pip watched him.

Luca stared back.

Pip said nothing.

That was almost worse.

“Why didn’t you come to the Guild?” Luca asked.

“I intended to inspect the ruins’ exterior first.”

Lysara looked toward the fallen guardian.

“The inspection became slightly more complicated than I expected.”

Pip pointed at the Hiveborn with his chin.

“Because of him?”

Lysara hesitated.

“Partly.”

The Hiveborn did not react.

Serena returned the letter to Lysara, then faced him.

“Now you.”

He looked at Serena.

“Zerak.”

Serena waited.

Zerak did not continue.

“Zerak what?”

“That is my name.”

“Origin?”

Zerak’s red eyes moved toward the trees.

For the first time since the battle ended, his body seemed uncertain.

His hands remained empty, but his fingers repeatedly formed a grip around something that was not there.

As if his body remembered a weapon his mind did not carry.

“I don’t know,” he answered.

“What do you mean, you don’t know?”

“I woke in the forest.”

“When?”

“I don’t know.”

“How did you get there?”

“I don’t remember.”

There was little change on his carapaced face.

But Luca saw his hand close into a fist.

Not in anger.

More like someone trying to hold on to the only thing he still possessed.

Lysara spoke before Serena asked another question.

“I found him approaching this area. I asked him to stop.”

Pip looked at the fallen guardian.

“Then you attacked each other.”

“I used Gust to keep him at a safe distance,” Lysara said.

Zerak looked at her.

“You threw me into a tree.”

Lysara lowered her eyes for a moment.

“I misjudged its strength.”

“You watched me stand, then used Wind Blade.”

“Because you ran toward me again.”

“There was a guardian behind you.”

Lysara paused.

“Yes.”

Her voice grew softer.

“That is also true.”

She turned fully toward Zerak.

“I misunderstood what you were doing.”

Zerak seemed unsure where the conversation was going.

Lysara bowed her head slightly.

“I’m sorry.”

Zerak stared at her.

Then at Serena.

Then at Luca, as if expecting one of them to explain what he was supposed to do when someone apologized.

No one helped.

“I am not badly hurt,” he finally said.

“That does not change my mistake.”

Zerak looked at the shoulder struck by stone.

“You aren’t hurt either?”

Lysara shook her head.

“No.”

Some of the tension left Zerak’s shoulders.

It was a tiny movement.

But Luca noticed.

Zerak had just fought Lysara.

Yet the first thing he wanted to confirm was whether she had been injured.

Pip walked to the first impact mark near the stone formation.

He crouched and touched the ground.

“The first Dark attack hit the guardian.”

Lysara approached.

“How do you know it was the first?”

“The mark is covered by dirt from the later impacts.”

Pip pointed to Zerak’s tracks.

“He stood there. Then moved toward you when the guardian woke.”

Lysara followed the direction he indicated.

“So he truly wasn’t attacking me.”

“I already told you that,” Zerak said.

“I know.”

“More than once.”

Lysara released a small breath.

Not annoyed.

Almost embarrassed.

“I have also apologized.”

“You can continue later,” Serena said. “We still don’t know whether he is connected to the ruins.”

Zerak looked at her.

“I am not.”

“You were found near them.”

“So were you.”

“We have an assignment.”

“I have a reason.”

“What reason?”

Zerak fell silent again.

Luca watched him.

Zerak clearly knew how to fight. The way he stood, measured distance, and moved to protect Lysara was not an accident.

But when asked about himself, he looked like someone standing on a road without a signpost.

Luca recognized part of that feeling.

It was not the same as his first day away from Aven.

But it was close enough.

“What is the last thing you remember?” Luca asked.

Serena turned toward him.

Luca’s voice did not sound like an interrogation.

He was only asking.

Zerak watched him for a long moment.

“Walking.”

“In the forest?”

“I’m not sure.”

“Alone?”

Zerak’s fingers tightened.

“I’m not sure about that either.”

Luca nodded.

There was no point forcing him to provide an answer he did not have.

Serena did not look satisfied.

But she asked no further questions.

Lysara looked at Luca’s right hand.

Her gaze stopped on the faint mark.

“What is that?”

Luca closed his hand by reflex.

“A mark from the relic.”

Lysara stepped closer.

Then stopped before Luca needed to move back.

“May I see it?”

Luca hesitated.

At least she had asked.

He opened his hand.

Lysara did not touch it. She only leaned closer to study the incomplete circle.

Her expression changed.

Not surprise.

More like someone who had finally discovered the missing part of a sentence.

“The same pattern,” she said.

“As what?” Serena asked.

“The records Eldren brought from the old archives. A circle with lines leading toward a single center.”

Pip stepped closer.

“The relief inside the ruins had a circle too.”

Lysara looked at him.

“A relief?”

“There was a Human, an Elf, a Dwarf, and a Goblin.”

“Together?”

Pip nodded.

Lysara looked ready to ask several questions at once.

She restrained herself.

Almost.

“How many figures? Was the circle complete? Was there writing? How were their hands positioned? Did—”

Pip raised one hand.

“One at a time.”

Lysara closed her mouth.

“Sorry.”

Luca was beginning to understand something.

Lysara was gentle.

Her curiosity was not.

Zerak was still looking at Luca’s hand.

Not with Lysara’s fascination.

His gaze was more cautious.

“When the guardian fell,” he said, “what did you do?”

Luca looked at the mark.

“I’m not sure.”

“You knew where we were going to move.”

Serena and Pip turned toward Luca.

Luca had hoped no one noticed.

That hope had been too ambitious.

“Only for a moment,” he said.

“You told me to lower myself before the ground moved beneath me,” Zerak continued.

“That was Serena.”

“I know.”

“Then what are you asking?”

“How did you know she would do it?”

Luca opened his mouth.

Then closed it.

He had no answer.

Lysara studied the traces of battle across the ground.

Serena’s stone platform.

The wind marks.

The scorch from Dark Bolt.

The place where Pip had landed.

Then she looked at Luca again.

“The five of you moved as though you had trained together.”

“The three of us have,” Pip said.

“Once,” Serena added.

“And those two had not even introduced themselves,” Luca said.

Zerak did not look away.

“That is not an answer.”

Luca sighed.

Everyone truly was beginning to say that.

“I wasn’t controlling you.”

“I know,” Zerak said.

The answer came too quickly.

Luca raised an eyebrow.

“You know?”

“If you controlled me, I would not have been aware of it after everything ended.”

“How can you be sure?”

Zerak looked at the fallen guardian.

“I chose to strike that point.”

He looked at Luca again.

“But I knew you would strike it too.”

No one spoke for several moments.

The morning wind moved between the trees.

Lysara finally broke the silence.

“I want to inspect the inside of the ruins.”

“Not today,” Serena said.

“I have research authorization.”

“The Guild has closed the entrance until the slope is inspected.”

“I can assist with the inspection.”

Serena looked at the letter in Lysara’s hand.

Then at the ruins.

“That decision belongs to the Guildmaster.”

Lysara did not argue.

“Very well.”

The simple answer seemed to surprise Serena slightly.

Lysara put the letter away.

“I will follow the procedure.”

Pip walked around Zerak.

Zerak allowed it, though his gaze followed Pip’s every movement.

“And him?” Pip asked.

Serena looked at Zerak.

“He is coming back to Arklune.”

“As a prisoner?” Zerak asked.

“As someone found near a dangerous location who does not know how he arrived there.”

“That sounds like a prisoner.”

“If you were a prisoner, your hands would already be bound.”

Zerak looked at his hands.

“A narrow difference.”

“Still a difference.”

Luca sheathed his sword.

“You don’t have to come if you don’t want to.”

Serena turned immediately.

“We did not agree to that.”

“That is why I said it first.”

“Luca.”

“He hasn’t committed a crime.”

“He is a Hiveborn found near the ruins with no clear memory.”

“And he protected Lysara.”

Zerak looked at Luca.

So did Serena.

Luca pointed to the stone that had struck Zerak’s shoulder.

“If he wanted to attack her, he could have let that hit her head.”

“That does not prove he is safe.”

“No.”

Luca agreed too quickly, robbing Serena of part of her argument.

“But it doesn’t prove he is guilty either.”

Serena tightened her jaw.

She did not like the conclusion.

But she could not deny it.

Luca recognized the expression.

He wore it often himself.

“If you come with us,” Luca told Zerak, “the Guild may be able to help discover where you came from.”

“And if they cannot?”

“At least you will have somewhere to begin looking.”

Zerak looked at the ruins.

Then the forest.

Neither path looked more familiar than the other.

“I will come.”

Serena picked up one coil of rope she had dropped.

Zerak walked over and took the other.

Serena looked at him.

“What are you doing?”

“Carrying this.”

“I can carry it.”

“I know.”

Zerak lifted the coil.

His manner did not suggest he was trying to prove Serena weak.

He had simply seen someone with a bandaged arm and taken a burden he could carry.

Serena watched him for a moment.

Then let him.

Zerak walked ahead toward the path back.

Lysara followed him.

Before leaving, she stopped beside Luca.

“Thank you.”

“For what?”

“For stopping us.”

Luca looked at Serena’s broken wall.

“I’m not sure I was the one who did that.”

“No,” Pip said. “That was Serena.”

Luca looked at him.

Pip sheathed his dagger.

“I am only keeping the record accurate.”

Lysara smiled slightly.

It was the first time Luca had seen her smile.

The expression made her face look far gentler than it had while surrounded by Wind magic.

They left the hill together.

Five people.

Not yet a party.

Not yet trusting one another.

But for the moment, at least no one was trying to attack anyone else.

Luca decided that was good enough.

***

They encountered trouble at Arklune’s gate.

Of course they did.

The two guards on duty immediately lowered their spears when they saw Zerak.

The people waiting to enter moved away without being asked.

Several even abandoned the line.

Zerak stopped.

His body did not take a fighting stance, but both hands slowly opened at his sides.

Serena stepped forward first.

“He is under the supervision of the Guild and Auregrad.”

The guard looked at the insignia on her coat.

“He’s Hiveborn.”

Serena looked at him.

“My eyesight is still good.”

Luca felt he had heard a similar answer before.

The second guard looked Zerak over from head to foot.

“No travel marks. No documents.”

“He was just found in the forest,” Serena said.

“And you’re bringing him into the city?”

“To be examined and reported to the Guildmaster.”

Pip raised a hand.

“He also helped carry the rope.”

Everyone looked at the coil in Zerak’s hands.

The first guard fell silent.

“That changes nothing.”

Pip lowered his hand.

“I was only providing complete information.”

Luca closed his eyes for a moment.

They were back at the same gate.

With a larger problem.

“Pip,” he called quietly.

“What?”

“Don’t help.”

Pip looked offended.

Lysara stepped forward with Eldren’s letter.

“I found him near my research site. He has shown no aggression except when attacked or while protecting someone.”

The guard accepted the letter.

He read the top.

Then the bottom.

His gaze moved between the seal of Sylvaris, Auregrad’s insignia, and Luca’s Guild plate.

“What kind of group is this?”

Luca looked at the people around him.

An Auregrad soldier.

A Goblin.

An Elf carrying a staff.

An unarmed Hiveborn.

And himself.

“We don’t know yet,” Luca answered honestly.

The guard rubbed the bridge of his nose.

Serena produced a copy of her orders.

“Escort us directly to the Guildmaster. Record that the Hiveborn entered the city under our supervision.”

The guard still hesitated.

But eventually raised his spear.

“Straight to the Guild.”

“That is where we are going,” Luca said.

Pip walked through the gate.

“He said the same thing yesterday.”

The guard stared at his back.

Luca quickened his pace before Pip added anything else.

***

The Adventurers’ Guild fell silent faster than it had when Pip first entered.

This time, it was immediate.

Conversations stopped almost at once.

Several hands moved toward weapons.

Zerak noticed.

His red eyes moved from table to table. He did not retreat or take a fighting stance.

Instead, he stepped slightly closer to Lysara’s side.

Not to hide.

His position placed Lysara farther from the nearest standing adventurer.

Lysara noticed.

But said nothing.

The Guild clerk at the counter looked at Luca.

Then Pip.

Then Serena.

Then Lysara.

His gaze finally stopped on Zerak.

The clerk took a breath.

“Another herb-gathering quest?”

Luca stared at him.

“No.”

“Good.”

The clerk looked at Zerak again.

“Because I was beginning to fear asking what grows in the southern forest.”

Pip gave a small snort.

Serena walked directly toward Rolan’s desk.

The Guildmaster stood near the quest board. When he saw them, his gaze stopped on Zerak.

Then moved to Luca.

“Route inspection,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Do not enter the ruins.”

“We didn’t.”

“Do not encounter a guardian.”

“Technically, the guardian was outside.”

Rolan closed his eyes.

Pip raised one hand slightly.

“He is right.”

“Thank you, Pip.”

“That wasn’t praise.”

“I know.”

Rolan exhaled and pointed toward a small meeting room beside the hall.

“Inside.”

No one argued.

***

Rolan read Eldren’s letter twice.

Then he read Serena’s brief report about the battle outside the ruins.

After that, he looked at the five people sitting or standing around the table.

Lysara sat with her staff resting against her chair.

Pip sat at the end of the table, inspecting his dagger.

Serena stood because she considered sitting while delivering a report improper.

Luca sat because his ribs considered standing too long improper.

Zerak chose to stand near the wall.

Rolan looked at Lysara.

“Eldren truly sent you?”

Lysara nodded.

“He found three records mentioning an ancient structure in this region. None gave an exact location.”

“Now you’ve found it.”

“Yes.”

“And you want to enter.”

“After the route is declared safe.”

Serena glanced at Lysara.

The answer seemed to earn a little of her approval.

“What is the purpose of your research?” Rolan asked.

“To document the structure, translate any surviving symbols, and determine whether the ruins are connected to other sites known to Sylvaris.”

“The relic?”

“I will only observe it.”

Rolan raised an eyebrow.

“Without touching it?”

Lysara paused.

“If possible.”

“That is not a reassuring answer.”

“I will not touch it without permission.”

“Better.”

Rolan turned to Zerak.

“And you do not remember how you reached the forest.”

“No.”

“You don’t remember where you came from?”

“Not clearly.”

“But you remember your name.”

“Yes.”

“Your abilities?”

Zerak raised one hand slightly. Thin darkness appeared at his fingertips, then immediately faded.

“Some of them.”

“Do you recognize the ruins?”

“No.”

“The mark on Luca’s hand?”

“No.”

“Your purpose?”

Zerak did not answer.

Rolan waited.

“I don’t have one yet,” Zerak finally said.

Luca watched the Guildmaster’s face.

Rolan showed no open fear or suspicion.

But his eyes sharpened slightly.

“Then we begin with something simple,” he said. “Until your origin is clear, you will receive a temporary permit under Guild supervision.”

Pip crossed his arms.

“Why was my permit only three days?”

“Because you argued with the gate guards.”

“That wasn’t written in the reason.”

“It is now.”

Pip stared at Rolan.

Rolan stared back.

Pip looked away first.

Zerak looked at Pip.

“How long is my permit?”

“Three days for now,” Rolan answered.

“Do I also need to argue with a guard?”

Pip turned toward him.

Luca pressed his lips together to hide a smile.

Rolan kept a straight face.

“No.”

“Good.”

Lysara covered her mouth with one hand.

Serena’s shoulder moved slightly.

Perhaps she had almost laughed.

Perhaps not.

Rolan took out a blank contract.

“The ruins remain closed. In two days, the Guild will send a team to inspect the interior.”

Lysara leaned forward slightly.

“I’m going.”

“I assumed so.”

Serena placed one hand on her document tube.

“I have been assigned to record all findings.”

“I assumed that too.”

Pip touched the pouch containing the golem core.

“I know the trapped route.”

Rolan nodded.

Then every eye turned toward Luca.

Luca looked at them one by one.

Serena.

Pip.

Lysara.

Zerak.

“I don’t think I have a reason not to go.”

“You have several reasons,” Serena said. “Most of them are still bandaged.”

“That is a reason to be more careful.”

“Is that what you said before pushing a cart at the Hobgoblin?” Pip asked.

“More or less,” Serena added.

Luca began to feel irritated.

It was even more irritating because they were stating facts again.

Rolan looked at Zerak.

“What about you?”

Zerak looked at the blank contract.

“I know nothing about the ruins.”

“True.”

“I also do not know whether that place is connected to me.”

“Also true.”

“Then why do I need to go?”

Rolan leaned back in his chair.

“You don’t.”

The room fell silent.

Zerak did not seem to expect that answer.

“You may remain here for the duration of your permit,” Rolan continued. “The Guild will search for reports of Hiveborn groups passing through this region. If you do not want to return to the ruins, no one will force you.”

Zerak looked toward the door.

For a moment, no one spoke to him.

No command.

No demand.

The decision was truly left in his hands.

“I will go,” he said.

Luca raised an eyebrow.

“Certain?”

“No.”

“An honest answer.”

“The ruins are the only place that might be connected to my condition.”

Zerak looked at the mark on Luca’s hand.

“And I want to know what happened when the guardian fell.”

Lysara nodded slightly.

For a different reason, she appeared to be thinking the same thing.

Rolan began writing.

“Good. Then it is settled.”

He wrote several lines, then turned the sheet so everyone could see it.

“Temporary investigation team. Five members.”

Pip pointed at Serena.

“She isn’t a Guild member.”

“Auregrad representative.”

Pip pointed at Lysara.

“She isn’t a Guild member either.”

“An authorized scholar.”

Pip pointed at Zerak.

“He only just entered the city.”

“A person under supervision.”

Pip looked at Luca.

Luca raised both hands.

“Don’t point at me. I have a plate.”

Pip lowered his hand.

“This party does not look convincing.”

Rolan continued as though he had not heard him.

“No one enters the ruins alone. No one touches the relic without permission.”

His gaze stopped on Lysara.

Lysara nodded obediently.

“No one activates a mechanism that has not been inspected.”

This time he looked at Pip.

“I usually inspect them first.”

“Usually?”

Pip did not answer.

“And no one starts a fight with another member of the team.”

Lysara glanced at Zerak.

“I can agree to that.”

Zerak nodded.

“So can I.”

Pip read the contract.

“Did that last part truly need to be written?”

Rolan put down the pen.

“After meeting all of you, yes.”

Serena read every line before taking the pen and signing her name.

Lysara wrote next.

Her handwriting was smaller than Serena’s, but just as neat.

Pip wrote only his name in short letters.

Luca signed the next line.

Zerak received the pen last.

He looked at the empty space beneath the other four names.

His hand did not move immediately.

“This is not an oath,” Rolan said. “Only a contract for one mission.”

Zerak looked at the Guildmaster.

“And after the mission?”

“You choose what you want to do next.”

Choose.

Zerak looked at the sheet again.

Then wrote his name.

Only one word.

Zerak.

Rolan took the contract and allowed the ink to dry.

“Two days from now, at sunrise.”

He looked at all five of them.

“Come prepared.”

One of Pip’s ears rose.

“Prepared for the ruins, or prepared for one another?”

Rolan put away the contract.

“Both.”

No one looked certain which would be more difficult.

Outside the meeting room, the Guild’s noise returned.

Conversation.

Mugs placed on tables.

Chairs scraping across the floor.

But when the door opened and the five of them emerged together, the noise faded again.

Luca walked first.

Pip was beside him.

Serena followed with the document tube beneath one arm.

Lysara walked while holding her staff.

Zerak came last, empty-handed, his red eyes watching the entire room.

Several adventurers still looked at him with suspicion.

Zerak did not react.

When a Guild server passed too close carrying a tray, Zerak shifted aside so his body would not block the way.

It was a simple movement.

But Luca noticed something in it.

Zerak was always watching the positions of others.

Always making sure he stood between them and whatever danger might come.

Perhaps he had not noticed it himself.

Pip looked at the five shadows stretching across the floor.

“One mission,” he said.

Serena nodded.

“One mission.”

Lysara smiled gently.

“That sounds simple enough.”

Luca looked at her.

Then at Pip.

Then Serena.

None of them appeared to recognize the mistake in that sentence.

Luca sighed.

He had heard the word simple too many times already.

And so far, it had never meant what it was supposed to mean.

**End of Chapter 7**
