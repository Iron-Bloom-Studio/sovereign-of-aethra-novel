# Chapter 1: The Road Beyond Home

Forty-three years had passed since the Battle of Fort Asteron.

For most people, it was simply history.

A name in a book.

A story told by veterans.

Or an old song sometimes sung too softly in frontier taverns.

In Aven, nothing had changed because of a war fought forty-three years ago.

Roosters still crowed too early.

Farmers still complained about the weather.

And someone still called Luca’s name before he could finish whatever he was doing.

“Luca!”

Luca stopped hammering.

Of course.

He had almost finished.

Almost.

He looked across the fence.

Uncle Daren stood in the middle of the field with both hands on his hips. Nearly all of the old man’s hair had turned white, but he was still broad enough to make people think twice before calling him old to his face.

“When you’re done over there, help me carry these to the storehouse.”

Luca looked beside Daren.

Three sacks of grain.

He frowned.

“Weren’t there only two yesterday?”

“One of them multiplied overnight.”

Luca paused.

Then looked at the sacks again.

“Grain sacks can reproduce now?”

“Keep talking and there’ll be four.”

Right.

Luca tucked the hammer into his belt.

Winning an argument against Uncle Daren had long ago joined Luca’s list of impossible things.

Along with:

Stopping the rain.

Making roosters shut up.

And getting the owner of Aven’s inn to admit his soup was too salty.

Luca walked over and lifted one of the sacks onto his shoulder.

Heavy.

“North storehouse?”

“All of them.”

Luca stared at him.

“It’s already full.”

“That’s why you get to find space.”

“Uncle, that sounds like your problem.”

Daren smiled.

“Now it’s yours.”

Yeah.

Of course.

That was life in Aven.

Aven was not a large town.

Calling it a small town might even make people from actual towns laugh.

There were no walls.

No palace.

No tall towers.

Only simple houses of timber and stone, fields surrounding the village, a river to the east, an old bridge, a forge, an inn, and a road that eventually led to the world beyond.

For Luca, that was enough.

Aven was home.

***

Luca did not really have a permanent job.

Today he was helping Daren.

Yesterday he had repaired the Merel family’s fence.

Two days before that, he had carried timber to Uncle Bram’s forge.

Tomorrow?

No idea.

Someone would probably need help.

Someone always did.

Sometimes Luca was paid in coins.

Sometimes in food.

Sometimes someone simply put a plate of dinner in front of him and said:

“Consider the village’s debt to you reduced a little.”

Luca had never understood that part.

What debt?

If anyone bothered to count properly, Luca was probably the one who owed Aven more.

He had lost his parents when he was young.

No single family had ever officially taken him in.

There had been no need.

The whole village had done it.

When Luca was too young to cook, food appeared at his door.

When his roof leaked, three people came to repair it before he had the chance to ask.

When he became old enough to work, the villagers stopped simply feeding him.

They gave him jobs instead.

Luca preferred that.

Far better than being pitied.

As for his mother...

Luca remembered almost nothing.

Sometimes there was a voice.

A woman singing softly near a window.

At least, he thought he remembered it.

Was it really a memory?

He had no idea.

He had tried to remember for so long that he was no longer sure which parts were real and which parts his mind had built for itself.

His father was different.

Luca still remembered a few things.

A sword at his waist.

Boots covered in mud.

A large travel bag near the door.

And stories.

So many stories.

Cities with walls taller than the highest trees around Aven.

Forests filled with monsters.

Dwarves who could shape metal as if it were clay.

Elves who remembered events that had already become legends to Humans.

Luca’s father had once been an adventurer.

That was the part Luca remembered best.

And perhaps that was why a travel pack had already been waiting in Luca’s house for nearly a month.

Nearly.

He simply had not told anyone when he planned to leave.

Not yet.

***

By midday, Luca had already changed jobs three times.

Three.

He was only eighteen and already felt like a sixty-year-old day laborer.

The grain sacks were done.

Two fences were repaired.

A loose wagon wheel had been delivered to the forge.

Now he only had to carry a coil of rope.

Easy.

For once, an easy job.

“BOAR!”

Luca stopped.

...

Of course.

A second shout came from the southern side of the fields.

“FOREST BOAR! TWO OF THEM!”

Wood cracked.

Luca dropped the rope.

A fence collapsed.

Then something burst through the crops.

Large.

Dark brown.

And not friendly in the slightest.

A Forest Boar.

Luca had seen one from a distance before.

From a distance, they were not especially frightening.

Up close?

Much bigger.

Its body was dense and heavy. Its shoulders nearly reached Luca’s waist. Coarse bristles covered its back, and two curved tusks rose from its lower jaw.

Another one appeared behind it.

“Get the children away!”

People started moving.

One boar crashed into a basket.

The basket went flying.

The second boar spun wildly through the crops, panicked by the villagers’ shouting.

Luca was already moving before he had time to think.

Then he saw a child fall.

Not far in front of the first boar.

Bad.

Very bad.

Luca drew his short sword.

It was not a good sword.

No engravings.

No jewels.

No legendary story behind it.

Just an iron blade sharp enough to do its job.

Hopefully.

“Get up!”

The child tried to move.

Couldn’t.

His leg was trapped beneath a wooden basket.

The boar lowered its head.

Luca raised his left hand.

Stay calm.

Draw Aethra.

Shape it.

Heat gathered in his palm.

“Fireball!”

A ball of flame shot forward.

And passed over the boar’s back.

Luca stared.

...

Too high.

Great.

Perfect day.

Fortunately, the fireball struck the ground behind the monster and burst.

The boar startled.

Turned.

Enough.

Luca rushed forward.

He pulled the child free just as the boar thundered past.

“Run!”

The child ran without answering.

Smart.

Now Luca only had one small problem.

The Forest Boar had turned around.

And it was looking at him.

“Oh.”

The creature scraped at the ground.

Luca gripped his sword with both hands.

He had trained before.

His father had taught him the basics of swordsmanship when he was young.

Later, a former soldier in Aven had occasionally corrected his stance and footwork.

So, in theory...

He knew what to do.

In theory.

The boar charged.

Luca stepped aside.

Too late.

“Ghk—!”

Its shoulder slammed into him.

The ground struck his back.

His sword flew from his hand.

Right.

Theory was not proving especially useful.

“Luca!”

Someone shouted.

The boar turned again.

Not good.

Luca raised his hand.

Heat gathered once more.

This time, don’t be stupid.

Don’t aim at the monster.

Aim at the ground.

“Fireball!”

Flame struck the earth directly in front of the boar.

The creature stopped sharply.

Backed away.

Two farmers rushed in from the side carrying hunting spears.

“Now!”

They attacked together.

The boar retreated.

One step.

Two.

Then it turned and fled through the broken fence.

The second boar followed after the villagers drove it off with shovels, sticks, and torches.

No one chased them.

Luca remained seated.

He held his shoulder.

It hurt.

But he could still move it.

Good.

Probably.

Uncle Daren stood in front of him.

“Brilliant.”

His tone suggested the opposite.

Luca picked up his sword.

“I know.”

“Did you plan on letting it hit you?”

“That was part of the strategy.”

“Stupid strategy.”

“It worked.”

“Because we came.”

...

Luca decided not to answer.

Then someone groaned.

One of the farmers who had helped was sitting on the ground.

A boar’s tusk had torn his calf.

Blood was already soaking through his trousers.

Luca stood immediately.

Or tried to.

His shoulder protested.

He ignored it.

“Let me see.”

“It’s only a scratch.”

Luca looked at the blood.

“Your scratch seems to disagree.”

He knelt.

Held one hand above the wound.

Fireball was easy.

Or it was supposed to be.

Lesser Heal was different.

This spell required more calm.

Draw Aethra.

Slowly.

Don’t force it.

A soft light appeared between Luca’s fingers.

“Lesser Heal.”

The light settled over the wound.

The bleeding began to slow.

The torn skin did not close completely, but its edges gradually drew together.

Luca maintained the spell for several seconds.

Then exhaled.

Done.

“That’s all I can do.”

The farmer moved his leg.

“Already much better.”

“It still needs to be cleaned and bandaged.”

“Yes, Mother.”

Luca stared at him.

Several people laughed.

Good.

If he could still joke, then it probably was not too bad.

A broken fence.

Several ruined crops.

One minor injury.

His shoulder hurt.

But everyone was alive.

For Aven, that counted as a decent victory.

***

That afternoon, Luca sat outside the blacksmith’s forge.

His shoulder had been bandaged.

Uncle Bram held Luca’s sword.

He examined the blade with an expression far more serious than the one he had used to examine Luca himself.

Luca watched him.

“The sword’s all right?”

“Yes.”

Luca exhaled in relief.

Bram looked at him.

“More worried about the sword?”

“The sword costs more than the shoulder.”

“Good thinking.”

“Thank you.”

“That wasn’t praise.”

Of course.

Bram turned the sword slightly.

“You rely too much on your front hand.”

Luca frowned.

“You saw that?”

“I saw you nearly get flattened.”

“That wasn’t an answer.”

“It was enough of one.”

Hard to argue with that.

Bram was a large man whose arms were covered in small burn scars from the forge.

Ever since Luca was young, the man had often repaired things around his house without asking for payment.

Luca had sometimes suspected Bram felt he owed something to Luca’s father.

He had never asked.

Maybe one day.

Bram handed the sword back.

“Still good.”

“Thank goodness.”

“Your shoulder?”

“Still there.”

“That’s good too.”

They sat in silence for a while.

The sound of a hammer came from inside the forge.

Clang.

Clang.

Clang.

Then Bram said,

“You moved a little like your father.”

Luca looked at him.

A simple sentence.

But hearing about his father always made something in his chest feel a little different.

“Did he get hit by boars a lot too?”

“No.”

“Oh.”

“He was smarter.”

Luca snorted.

That did not sound like praise.

Bram smiled faintly.

“Your father always moved before he had time to think about whether he himself was safe.”

Luca looked down at his sword.

“Doesn’t that sound stupid?”

“Yes.”

Bram looked at him.

“Very much like someone else I know.”

...

All right.

Luca had no answer to that.

The sun was beginning to sink behind the fields.

Luca watched the orange light spread across the roofs of Aven.

Tomorrow.

Yes.

Tomorrow.

“Uncle.”

“Hm?”

“I’m leaving tomorrow.”

Bram did not look surprised.

At all.

“I thought so.”

Luca blinked.

“I haven’t told anyone.”

“Everyone knows.”

...

What?

“Everyone?”

“You’ve been saving money for nearly two years.”

True.

“You bought a travel pack last month.”

True.

“For the last three days, you’ve been asking merchants about the road to Arklune.”

Also true.

Bram looked at him.

“You’re terrible at keeping secrets.”

Luca thought about it.

“It sounds pretty obvious now.”

“It was.”

Bram stood.

“Go.”

Luca looked up.

“That’s it?”

“Would you rather we tie you to a tree?”

“No.”

“Then go.”

Bram walked toward the forge door.

Then stopped.

“Aven will still be here if you want to come home.”

Luca did not answer.

He looked across the village.

Small houses.

Smoke from kitchens.

Children playing near the well.

People were already repairing the fence that had broken only hours earlier.

As if two Forest Boars charging into the fields had been nothing more than a small inconvenience in an ordinary day.

Aven.

Home.

Luca looked at both of his hands.

His first Fireball had almost missed completely.

He had failed to avoid the boar’s charge.

His Lesser Heal could only handle a minor wound.

If an experienced adventurer had been there today, perhaps everything would have ended much faster.

Luca was not strong.

That much was obvious.

Strange.

A few years ago, that thought might have made him afraid to leave Aven.

Now?

It did the opposite.

He wanted to learn.

He wanted to see the cities from his father’s stories.

The forests.

The monsters.

The people.

The world beyond the hills that had always marked the edge of his view.

And maybe one day he could come home as someone more useful than a young man who could only make a Forest Boar turn because his Fireball had almost missed.

Yes.

Tomorrow.

***

That night, Luca packed his things.

Again.

He had already done it twice that week.

Still, it could not hurt to check.

Two sets of clothes.

A blanket.

Flint and steel.

A water flask.

Dry bread.

Salted meat.

Bandages.

His short sword.

A pouch of coins saved from nearly two years of work.

Enough.

Probably.

He looked at the pack.

It looked heavy.

Tomorrow Luca’s problem.

His house felt quieter than usual.

A table.

Two chairs.

A wooden shelf.

A bed.

And an old chest that had been there since he was a child.

Luca opened it.

There was nothing impressive inside.

Of course there wasn’t.

No legendary sword.

No glowing stone.

No mysterious letter that suddenly explained every secret about his parents.

Only old things.

His father’s leather belt.

A metal button from a coat that had long since fallen apart.

And a small piece of cloth Luca was not even sure had belonged to anyone he remembered.

He picked up the belt.

It was old.

There were several scratches in the leather.

Who knew how many roads his father had traveled while wearing it?

Luca held it for a moment.

Then put it back.

“Not yet.”

Even he did not know why he said it.

Maybe because that belonged to his father’s journey.

Tomorrow, Luca would begin his own.

***

The next morning, Luca stepped outside.

Then stopped.

Almost half the village was already standing along the main road.

...

No.

Impossible.

He stared at them.

They stared back.

“You really didn’t all have to come.”

“We know,” someone answered.

“Then why are you here?”

“To make sure you actually leave.”

Laughter broke out.

Luca sighed.

So his secret really had been terrible.

Uncle Daren stepped forward and handed him a wrapped bundle of food.

“For two days.”

“I already packed food.”

“Now it’s enough for three.”

Luca accepted the bundle.

His pack felt heavier just looking at it.

Then the innkeeper handed him a small pouch of coins.

Luca immediately shook his head.

“No. I can’t take this.”

“It’s not a gift.”

“Then what?”

“Payment.”

“For what?”

“For the work you’ll do after you come back.”

Luca opened his mouth.

Closed it.

No.

Pointless.

He put the pouch into his pack.

A small child tugged at the edge of his mantle.

“Luca.”

“Hm?”

“If you meet a dragon, bring back one of its scales.”

Luca looked at the child.

Then considered the request very seriously.

“If I meet a dragon, I’ll probably run.”

The child looked disappointed.

“Adventurers aren’t supposed to run.”

“Wrong.”

Luca crouched.

“Adventurers who stay alive know when to run.”

Several people laughed.

The child still did not look convinced.

That was fine.

He would understand when he was older.

Probably.

Bram stood a little apart from the others.

He said nothing.

Luca nodded to him.

Bram returned the nod.

That was enough.

Luca turned around.

The road from Aven stretched north.

Beyond those hills was Arklune.

A city Luca had known only from merchants’ stories.

And his father’s.

He started walking.

One step.

Two.

Ten.

Then Luca stopped.

Don’t look back.

...

He looked back.

Aven was still there.

Small houses.

Green fields.

The old bridge.

Smoke rising from chimneys.

The people who had raised him standing along the road.

For a moment, his legs felt heavier than the pack on his back.

This was his home.

Truly his home.

Then someone shouted,

“If you want to come back after three days, we’ll still take you!”

Luca laughed.

“At least give me a week!”

He raised a hand.

Then turned again.

This time, he did not stop.

Luca did not know how long the journey would last.

He did not know who he would meet.

He did not know what kind of monsters were waiting for him.

He did not know whether he would even become a decent adventurer.

And he certainly did not know how far the road he had just chosen would one day carry him.

At that moment, he was only an eighteen-year-old young man leaving a small village called Aven.

Nothing more.

For the first time in his life, the road ahead felt longer than the road home he had always known.

Luca smiled.

Good.

He wanted to see where it would lead.

**End of Chapter 1**
