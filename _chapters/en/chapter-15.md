# Sovereign of Aethra

## Chapter 15: The Heart Beneath Stone

The third pulse vanished into the wall.

The chamber fell silent again, but the silence felt different now. The Asteron Core knew they were there. Whatever waited at the end of the southern channel might know as well.

“Do not touch anything,” Lysara said.

Pip, who was extending one finger toward a dusty lever, withdrew his hand. “I am offended that you said that while looking at me.”

“I looked at you because your hand was about to touch something.”

“An unfortunate coincidence for my reputation.”

Lysara knelt beside the metal rings surrounding the base of the Core. A thin glow of Water Aethra flowed from her fingertips, washing away dust without shifting any of the components. Beneath it, a series of parallel scratches appeared.

“This is not damage from age,” she said.

Serena moved closer without crossing the boundary Lysara had marked. “Tool marks?”

“A chisel or pry bar. Someone tried to remove the regulator ring.”

Pip examined the other side. He produced a tiny magnifying lens and tapped two bolts with his nail. “These are new.”

“New?” Luca asked.

“Not made yesterday, but not forty years old either.” Pip pointed to the brighter metal beneath a thin coat of dust. “A few months, perhaps. The threads don't match the originals. Quick work.”

Serena looked at the southern channel still pulsing on the wall map. “The relic theft was not the beginning. Someone mapped this place before we arrived.”

Zerak stood beside one of the black fractures without touching it. A thread of Dark Aethra moved around his glove, drawn toward the gap like smoke.

“Pressure is rising from below,” he said. “Not only Dark. Something has collected in the channels. If the Core is activated now, the pressure will rise with the flow.”

Lysara turned to Luca. “Your relic might be able to force the system open.”

Luca closed his fingers around the black stone. The part of him that saw every locked door as a challenge wanted to try. The shining map, the dead city above them, and the southern channel all seemed to be waiting for one more push.

He remembered the tower collapsing. He also remembered Harlan lying in a stone hollow because another man had chosen payment over turning back.

“Then we do not activate it,” he said.

Lysara raised an eyebrow, mildly surprised.

“I can learn,” Luca added.

“Record the date,” Pip said.

They began examining the chamber without waking the Core. Pip found a stone panel behind one of the pillars. It concealed a maintenance map etched onto a thin copper sheet. Six corridors radiated from the central chamber, but only three remained accessible. Two had collapsed. The final passage was sealed by a door marked with a warning Lysara could not fully translate.

“This means pressure,” she said. “Or explosion. The rest is gone.”

“Either one is enough reason not to open it,” Serena decided.

They chose the first corridor.

The room at its end was filled with stone shelves and metal cylinders. Most were empty. Six rod-shaped recesses had been cut into one wall, but one held nothing.

Pip brushed a finger across its interior. “Less dust here.”

“The old key,” Luca said.

“Or a component that looked like one.” Lysara copied the shape into her notebook. “Whatever it was, someone removed it recently.”

Beneath the shelves, they found a leather bag that had not decayed. It clearly did not belong to ancient Asteron. Inside were fresh rope, three climbing spikes, a bottle of oil, and a piece of bread gone green with mold.

“They did not come only once,” Serena said. “They were working here.”

The second corridor was blocked by rubble as high as Serena's chest. She planted her feet and fed Earth Aethra into her shield. Instead of striking the stones, she pushed slowly while Lysara bound the dust with Water to keep it out of the air. Luca and Pip removed loose fragments. Zerak watched the passage behind them.

When they had opened enough space, they slipped through one at a time.

A valve room waited on the other side. Large conduits crossed the walls, each marked with an affinity symbol. Several joints had split. A black mass clung to the Dark valve like dried blood.

Zerak extended one hand.

“Wait,” Luca said.

Zerak looked at him.

“You said there was pressure.”

“I can also learn,” Zerak replied flatly.

He did not touch the mass. Lysara sent a thread of Water Aethra through the conduit from a safe distance. Water emerged from three fractures, dripping onto the floor, then stopped at the Dark valve as if it had struck a wall.

“The channel is blocked,” she said.

Zerak opened his palm. Dark Aethra formed a small spiral above it. The mass on the valve lifted little by little, drawn toward him, but he did not absorb it directly. He held it in the air while Luca shone Light into the pipe.

A sliver of metal sat at the center of the clot.

Pip removed it with a pair of tongs. “Part of a drill bit.”

“Then the blockage was no accident,” Serena said.

Zerak compressed the contaminated Dark Aethra into a stone bottle. Lysara sealed it with a layer of ice before Pip fixed the stopper in place.

“Our first piece of evidence,” Pip said as he put the bottle into his bag. “Extremely dangerous, possibly explosive, and of course I am carrying it.”

“If it explodes, we will remember your sacrifice,” Zerak said.

“I feel much safer.”

The sound of falling metal came from the third corridor.

They fell silent at once.

A human groan followed.

Serena took the lead. Footprints were clear in the dust: several people had run inward, but only one set had returned toward the main chamber. Dried blood stained the wall.

The corridor ended in a storage room whose ceiling had partially collapsed.

Three people were inside.

Two were dead.

The third was trapped beneath a metal beam and broken stone. He was still breathing, but only barely. Sweat had pasted his hair across his face. Near his right hand lay a knife like those carried by the looters at the tower.

Pip recognized it first. “Calder's group.”

The man opened his eyes. When he saw Pip, he tried to reach for the knife. His hand moved the width of one finger before dropping again.

“Don't,” he rasped. “Don't leave me.”

Pip looked at the two bodies behind him. One had a terrible wound across his back, not his front. “You left your partner at the tower.”

The man closed his eyes.

“And we are not him,” Luca said.

Pip watched Luca for several seconds, then sheathed his dagger. “I know. I only wanted him to know it too.”

Serena inspected the beam. “I can lift it, but the floor is cracked. When the weight shifts, the rest of the ceiling may come down.”

Lysara prepared Wind to hold back dust and smaller fragments. Zerak positioned himself beneath the weakest section of ceiling. Shadow hardened over his shoulders like a second support.

“Now,” Serena said.

Earth Aethra flowed from her feet. The stone beneath her boots hardened, joining her to the floor. She drove her shield under the beam and lifted.

The metal moved a hand's breadth.

Luca crawled into the gap. Heat and the smell of blood filled the narrow space. He seized the man's coat and pulled him free just before Serena lowered the beam again.

The ceiling trembled but held.

Luca pressed both hands to the man's chest.

“Heal.”

Light Aethra spread like golden thread. External wounds closed. Broken bone returned to place as far as Luca could sense, but something inside the man's body remained wrong. His breathing steadied, though his skin was still pale.

“I stopped the bleeding,” Luca said. “He still needs a healer.”

“Marrek,” the man whispered.

“What?”

“My name... is Marrek.”

Serena knelt beside him. “Who paid you?”

Marrek swallowed with difficulty. “Calder gathered the crew. Said it was Guild work. Take the old key. Open the flow. Prove the city still had something that could be controlled.”

“For whom?”

“Never knew his name. Never saw his face.” Marrek lifted a trembling hand and touched his ring finger. “A ring. A sun... divided through the middle.”

Lysara drew the symbol: a circle split by a broken line into two uneven halves.

“No house of Arklune or Auregrad that I know,” she said.

“Not Morvane either,” Zerak added.

Marrek suddenly clutched Luca's arm.

“We turned the Core ring. Only a little. Then the southern channel lit.” His eyes shifted toward the wall, afraid of something they could not see. “The thing below... woke when we turned the Core.”

A heavy grinding sound came from the floor.

A crack raced between Serena's feet.

“Get him out,” she said.

Pip dragged Marrek into the corridor. Luca had only just stood when the floor behind them rose. Stones joined into two legs, a massive body, and arms ending in square fists. Red light burned behind a faceless head.

A Ruin Golem rose from the floor.

It was larger than the Stone Golem at the tower. Its back nearly touched the ceiling, and the metal channels across its body were connected directly to the Core conduits.

“Do not break the channels!” Lysara shouted. “If the flow returns into the Core—”

The golem swung one fist.

Serena met it with her shield.

The impact shook the entire room. Serena's knees dropped the width of one finger, but she did not fall.

“Lysara, right joint!” Luca shouted. “Zerak, weaken its center! Pip, find the lock!”

“Luca?” Pip called as he ran between the golem's feet.

“I will do the less intelligent part!”

Luca hurled a Fireball into the golem's chest.

The explosion did little damage, but it was enough to turn the red light in its face toward him. The golem pivoted. Its fist struck the place where Luca had been an instant earlier and punched a crater into the floor.

“Yes,” Pip shouted, “that is definitely your part!”

Luca ran around the chamber, firing small Firebolts to hold the golem's attention without striking the conduits. Serena slammed the side of its knee, forcing it to put its weight on the right leg. Lysara sent Water into that joint, then used Wind to cool it rapidly. Metal groaned under the sudden change in temperature.

Zerak drove the point of his spear into the golem's shadow.

“Dark Bind.”

The shadow beneath the creature climbed its body like chains. The light in its chest dimmed. Its movements slowed, but contaminated Dark Aethra began creeping back toward Zerak.

“Quickly,” he said through clenched teeth.

Pip climbed the golem's back using gaps between the stone plates. A rotating panel sat in its left shoulder, protected by three locks.

“I found the controls!”

“Can you stop it?” Serena asked.

“Yes. Probably. Do not make it sway!”

The golem immediately swayed.

Pip was nearly thrown. He drove his dagger into a gap in the stone—not the mechanism—and held on with one hand. The other worked quickly on the first lock.

Click.

The golem raised one arm to seize him. Serena leaped and struck the creature's elbow with the rim of her shield. The stone arm swung aside and destroyed an empty shelf.

Second click.

Zerak's Dark Bind shattered. The golem stepped forward, carrying Pip with it.

“Luca!”

Luca aimed Light Aethra into the metal lines across the golem's back. The light flowed too brightly, freezing the system for one instant as the two currents collided.

Pip opened the final lock.

He pulled the lever behind the panel.

The red light in the golem's face died.

The creature froze with one foot raised. Serena pushed its lower leg toward a clear section of floor. The Ruin Golem fell away from the conduits and struck the ground in a burst of dust.

No one moved until the final echo faded.

Pip emerged from the dust with one hand pressed to his lower back. “I want the record to show that the plan succeeded because of the intelligent part.”

Luca coughed. “The less intelligent part was also very busy.”

Lysara inspected the conduit joints one by one. The fallen golem had not severed the main channel, but two valves were shaking from the impact. Serena held them in place with stone supports while Pip fashioned temporary wedges from pieces of copper plate.

“This will not last,” Lysara said.

“How long?” Luca asked.

“If no one touches the Core, perhaps several weeks. If someone tries to activate it again, perhaps several minutes.”

Pip tapped one of his wedges. “I vote we write ‘do not touch.’”

“In what language?”

“Every language that has a symbol for a skull.”

They left a warning on the main door and concealed the mechanical panel beneath a layer of dust. It would not stop anyone who had entered before, but at least they would see fresh disturbance when they returned.

They tied Marrek to an improvised stretcher. Pip took the maintenance map, the drill fragment, and a copy of the divided-sun symbol. Lysara recorded the condition of every channel they had inspected. Before leaving, Serena stood before the Core and studied the dead lines on the walls.

“We cannot leave this,” she said.

“We also cannot repair it with five people in one night,” Luca replied.

“What if the saboteur returns?” Lysara asked.

Zerak looked toward the sealed southern corridor. “They will return. No one does all this only to stop after failing once.”

Luca studied the shining map. Asteron was no longer a quest destination or a name written on a relic. Above them was a well that still held water, homes that had once held families, and a gate that could open a new road for Larkhill. Beneath it was the broken heart of a city—and someone wanted to own it.

“We return to Arklune,” he said. “We take Marrek, the evidence, and the map. Then we find people who can help us guard this place.”

Serena nodded. “The Guild may refuse.”

“The kingdoms may fight over it,” Lysara said.

“The enemy may already be waiting,” Zerak added.

Pip lifted one end of the stretcher. “All of you are remarkably good at making decisions sound appealing.”

For the first time, their next step was not about completing a quest.

They had to decide whether Asteron was worth guarding.

And when Luca looked at the weak light still holding against the dark wall, he realized he had made that decision before speaking it aloud.
