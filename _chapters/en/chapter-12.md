# Sovereign of Aethra

## Chapter 12: The Tower's Call

The fourth toll never came.

Instead, the grass behind them split apart.

The first Dire Wolf burst from the darkness with its jaws open and eyes that did not seem fully aware of what it was hunting.

“Serena!”

Serena was already moving before Luca finished calling her name.

She lowered her center of gravity, planted her left foot, and met the monster with the face of her shield. The impact sounded like a tree trunk breaking. Claws scraped across steel and the Dire Wolf was thrown sideways, but two more shadows immediately rose from the grass.

“There are more!” Pip shouted.

“I can see them!”

Serena swept her sword low, forcing the second wolf to leap. Zerak stepped into the space beneath it. His spear moved once—quick, straight, without a wasted motion—striking the creature's shoulder and turning it away from Luca.

Zerak did not pursue.

His red eyes remained on the slope.

More pairs of eyes shone among the grass.

“They are not hunting as one pack,” he said. “Something is drawing them to the same place.”

A heavy growl came from the east. A much larger shadow moved behind a collapsed stone fence.

Lysara raised her measuring crystal. The light inside spun so quickly that it became a pale ring.

“The tower's call is overriding their instincts,” she said. “They are not attacking from hunger.”

The first Dire Wolf rose again. Saliva hung from its jaws, but its ears lay flat and its tail remained low. It looked more frightened than angry.

Luca raised his left hand.

Fire gathered, and he forced himself to hold it.

The valley grass was dry. If he threw a Fireball directly into the pack, they might survive the wolves only to become trapped in a wildfire.

“I'll open a path,” he said. “Don't stand in front of me.”

“Very late advice,” Serena replied.

Luca aimed his palm at the ground.

“Fireball!”

The sphere did not fly toward the monsters. It struck the earth several paces ahead and broke into a low line of flame across the grass. The sudden heat forced the Dire Wolves back. Luca severed the spell before the fire could spread.

A gap opened toward the tower.

“Now!” Serena shouted.

They ran.

Pip reached the threshold first. His small body slipped between Serena and the wall, and he vanished inside without slowing.

Lysara followed with a sweep of her staff.

“Gust!”

A narrow wind scattered the embers behind them and drove smoke into the monsters' faces. Luca went in after her. Zerak retreated last, his spear holding back the wolves still trying to cross the line of fire.

Serena stepped through, turned, and struck the remains of the door with the edge of her shield.

The rotting slab fell at an angle. It did not seal the entrance, but it narrowed it.

A Dire Wolf crashed into the wood from outside.

The entire frame shook.

“That won't hold for long,” Luca said.

“It isn't meant to.”

The tower's main chamber was circular. Empty recesses covered the walls where lamps or crystals had once been mounted. A stone staircase followed the curve upward toward the shattered upper level. In the center of the chamber stood an old metal cradle, chest-high, with several toothed rings surrounding an open core.

The pale stone relic had been forced into it.

A black fracture divided its surface.

Whenever light pulsed within that fracture, thin lines across the floor answered and vanished into the walls.

Calder knelt before the cradle. One hand gripped a lever while the other pressed against his side. Blood had soaked the bottom of his coat.

Venn lay near the stairs.

He was still breathing, but only barely.

Pip stood on a cracked crate with his small crossbow trained on Calder.

“Release the lever,” he said.

Calder turned sharply.

His eyes were red from exhaustion. Dried blood marked his temple, and anger did little to conceal the fear beneath it.

“Don't come closer!”

“You're surrounded,” Serena said.

Another impact shook the door behind her.

Calder gave a short laugh. “We all are.”

“That object is calling them,” Lysara said. “Remove the relic from the cradle.”

“I was paid to open it.”

“You aren't opening it,” Lysara replied. “You are forcing a regulator to operate in reverse.”

Calder looked at the pale stone as if seeing it for the first time.

“The man who hired us said this was a vault.”

“He lied.”

“He paid half in advance.”

Pip tilted his head. “Will the other half feel important after you're dead?”

Calder's jaw tightened.

Luca stepped toward Venn. “He needs help.”

“Don't touch him!” Calder pulled the lever a little farther. “No one moves until the door opens.”

“What door?” Serena asked.

“I don't know!”

The answer echoed through the tower.

Calder looked from one of them to the next, and his voice lowered.

“The tolling started after the crate broke. Venn heard whispers. Harlan wanted to turn back. We had already come too far.”

“So you left Harlan to die,” Luca said.

“I tried to finish the job.”

“That isn't a better answer.”

A claw pushed through the gap in the doorway. Serena kicked it back without taking her eyes from Calder.

Lysara crouched and held her palm a few fingers above the floor.

“The Aethra flow is below us,” she said. “This tower stands over an old junction. The relic is a key, but the cradle is turning it in the wrong direction.”

“Can you stop it?” Serena asked.

“If he releases the lever.”

Calder tightened his grip.

“Whenever I let go, the rings close again. We tried. The tower called even louder.”

Lysara studied the mechanism.

“Because its lock is damaged.”

“Then repair it!”

“I cannot repair a decades-old mechanism while you pull it apart.”

For a moment, there was only their breathing and the monsters battering the door.

Then Venn groaned.

His hand moved weakly across the floor.

“Calder...”

Calder turned.

Pip fired.

The bolt did not strike Calder. It passed above his shoulder and cut the rope holding the cracked crate against the stairs. The crate fell and smashed into the floor between Calder and Venn. Calder flinched and released the lever.

The first ring turned.

Then the second.

The third caught.

A toll with no bell struck them from below.

Doong.

Luca felt the vibration pass through his feet, climb his spine, and rattle his teeth. The mark on his left palm shone brightly enough to pierce his glove.

Stones lifted from the floor.

The first fragments formed legs. More pieces assembled bodies and arms. Within seconds, two Stone Golems stood between them and the relic cradle. Neither was taller than Serena, but both were made from thick foundation stones.

One immediately swung an arm.

Serena blocked it.

Her shield rang, and her boots slid half a step.

“Heavy,” she said through clenched teeth.

“Brilliant analysis!” Pip shouted.

The second golem moved toward Venn.

Zerak intercepted it. His spear struck its stone chest and bounced aside. He shifted before the counterattack reached his head, then drove the spearpoint into the gap at its knee.

“Dark Lance.”

Dense darkness entered the joint. The stone did not break, but the light driving it weakened. The golem's knee folded and its body fell sideways.

“Now,” Zerak said.

Luca swung at the shoulder joint. His blade left a thin crack, but not a deep one.

Pip jumped from the crate onto the golem's back. He pushed his dagger into a seam and pulled with both hands.

“This thing doesn't have a soft part!”

“That is generally a property of stone,” Lysara replied.

She aimed her staff at the crack Luca had made.

“Gust.”

The wind compressed into a narrow thrust. Pressure entered the crack and burst the stone from within. The golem's arm broke free, spun through the air, and struck the wall.

Luca ducked.

“That was close!”

“I accounted for it,” Lysara said.

Pip was still clinging to the golem's back. “Was I included in that calculation?”

Lysara did not answer.

“I will take that as a no.”

Across the chamber, Serena allowed the other golem to strike her shield once more. When its stone arm rose, she turned and swept her sword across its waist—not to cut it, but to change the direction of its weight.

The golem lost its balance.

Serena kicked the back of its knee.

It fell directly on top of the golem facing Luca and Zerak.

The floor shook.

“Two at once,” Serena said. “More efficient.”

Pip crawled out from beneath the fragments. “Warn me before the next efficient thing.”

Wood broke at the entrance.

The first Dire Wolf pushed its head through the gap.

More monsters moved behind it.

Calder seized his knife and charged the cradle.

“I have to take the stone!”

Luca cut him off. Calder's knife struck his sword. The first impact sent a tremor up Luca's arm. The man was wounded, but fear gave him what strength remained.

“Move!”

“You don't even know what it does!”

“I know what it's worth!”

Calder attacked again. Luca parried outward, stepped in, and struck the man's wrist with the hilt of his sword.

The knife fell.

Calder swung with his left hand. Luca ducked, caught the arm, and threw him to the floor with more luck than technique.

“Pip!”

“Already here.”

The goblin appeared beside Calder and placed his dagger close to the man's ear.

“Stay down.”

Luca turned toward the relic.

The third ring still trembled between two metal teeth. Every movement widened the black fracture across the stone.

“Lysara?”

“We must remove it before the channel beneath the tower fully opens.”

“And after that?”

The elf watched the light patterns across the floor.

“I don't know yet.”

“I was hoping for a longer answer.”

“Length would not improve it.”

Luca gripped the pale stone with both hands.

Cold bit his skin.

Heat followed.

He pulled.

The relic did not move.

The metal rings tightened like jaws.

Serena stepped beside him and pushed the point of her sword beneath one ring.

“Together.”

They forced in opposite directions. Old metal screamed. Pip left Calder to Zerak, climbed onto the cradle, and drove his dagger into the jammed teeth.

“If my blade breaks, the Guild is replacing it.”

“The Guild did not order you to use a dagger as a lever,” Serena said.

“They don't need that detail.”

Pip twisted the dagger.

The metal tooth shifted.

The relic broke free.

Luca caught it.

The entire world became silent.

He could still see Serena beside him, Pip atop the cradle, Lysara with her staff raised, and Zerak guarding Calder and Venn. He saw the Dire Wolf breaking through the entrance. He saw dust falling from above.

But every sound had vanished.

Beneath that silence, he felt the Aethra channels.

Serena's Earth moved like roots searching for stone to hold.

Lysara's Wind spiraled, narrow and swift, finding paths that eyes could not see.

Zerak's Dark did not feel like an absence. It had weight—a space capable of receiving an overflow without immediately breaking.

The Fire within Luca moved too quickly, eager to burn a straight road through anything in its way.

And beneath the fire was something smaller.

Light.

Not a blinding radiance. Only a pale thread that appeared whenever he chose to heal instead of destroy.

All those currents collided inside the relic.

Luca tried to hold them.

Pain drove through both arms.

The black fracture crawled toward his fingers.

“Luca!”

Serena's voice returned as if breaking through the surface of deep water.

“Do not fight it alone.”

She planted her shield into the floor.

“Earth Anchor!”

Golden-brown lines spread beneath it. The fractured floor stopped shifting. The tower still trembled, but now it had something to hold.

Lysara closed her eyes and moved her staff in a small circle.

“Gust.”

This time, the wind was not an attack. It entered the luminous channels in the floor and redirected the wild flow so that it no longer struck the relic all at once.

Zerak shifted his spear to his left hand. His right palm pressed against the floor beside Venn.

Shadow spread beneath his fingers.

“I will receive the excess,” he said. “Not all of it.”

“How much can you hold?” Lysara asked.

“We will learn.”

“That isn't a comforting answer,” Luca said.

“It was not intended to be.”

Pip lay across the metal cradle, both legs braced against one ring.

“The third ring is moving again!”

“Turn it in the direction of the lines!” Lysara called.

“There are many lines!”

“The glowing one!”

“They are all glowing!”

A crack opened across the wall.

Pip looked down, then at the ring.

He chose a direction and pushed with his entire body.

The ring moved by one tooth.

The pattern on the floor changed.

For the first time, the Aethra channels no longer collided inside Luca's hands. They flowed through him—still wild, but no longer without direction.

He did not try to control his companions' power.

He could not.

Instead, Luca searched for the empty place between them.

Earth became the foundation.

Wind opened the path.

Dark received the overflow.

Fire kept the current moving.

Light bound them without forcing them to become the same.

The threads met.

The mark in his palm opened like an eye.

“Now!” Lysara shouted.

Luca turned both hands along a pattern no one had ever taught him.

“Stop.”

There was no explosion.

Only a single pulse of light moving from the relic into the floor.

A second pulse spread through the walls.

The third passed beyond the tower and flowed beneath the entire valley.

The bell-less toll ceased.

The Dire Wolf that had broken through the entrance stumbled. Its eyes blinked, as if it had awakened in a strange place. It saw Serena, gave a weak growl, then backed away.

Outside, the howling changed into confused cries.

The shadows among the grass stopped approaching.

One by one, the monsters turned toward the forest and the slopes.

Silence returned to the valley.

This time, it felt natural.

Luca fell to his knees.

The relic slipped from his hands, but Serena caught it before it struck the floor.

“Luca.”

“Still conscious.”

“That is not a measure of health.”

“I learned from the wrong person.”

Serena watched him for a second, then the corner of her mouth lifted slightly.

“Can you stand?”

Luca tried.

His legs refused.

“In a moment.”

Zerak raised his hand from the floor. The shadows around his arm did not immediately fade. A red line across his carapace pulsed once, then dimmed.

Lysara knelt beside him. “You absorbed too much.”

“Not all of it.”

“That is not a defense.”

“It is accurate.”

Pip was still on top of the cradle.

A pebble fell beside his head.

He looked up.

A second pebble fell.

“I vote we leave before this building changes its mind.”

As if in agreement, the tower released a long cracking sound.

Serena pulled Luca to his feet. Zerak lifted Venn without difficulty, though his movements were slower than usual. Pip climbed down, collected his crossbow, then bound Calder's hands with rope from the crate.

“I can walk on my own,” Calder said.

“Good,” Pip replied. “Then I won't have to drag you.”

They left just as a section of the stone staircase collapsed into the chamber.

Outside, Luca's line of fire had gone out. Only blackened earth remained. The last Dire Wolf disappeared into the tall grass without looking back toward the tower.

Night had fully fallen.

But faint patterns of light still moved beneath the ground.

Lysara placed the relic on a flat stone. Its black fracture remained, but it was no longer spreading. The small metal ring around it now turned slowly on its own.

“What exactly did we do?” Serena asked.

Lysara examined her measuring crystal. A hairline crack had appeared across its surface, but the light within was calm again.

“We stopped one regulator tower from drawing Aethra out of the entire valley.”

“Only one tower?”

“Look.”

She pointed toward the floor near the cradle, where the collapse had exposed something beneath the dust and stone. A large engraving lay below the mechanism.

A map.

Its lines did not mark borders. They connected several small circles. The tower where they stood was only one circle in the east. Most of the names had worn away.

Pip wiped one section clean with his sleeve.

“A road?”

“A flow network,” Lysara answered. “It may also follow the old roads. These towers regulated Aethra distribution across the region.”

Serena studied a line leading southwest.

“They all gather there.”

At the end of that route was a circle much larger than the others.

The writing beneath it remained legible.

**ASTERON.**

Luca moved closer, though he had to lean against the wall.

The name felt familiar in a way he could not explain. Not like a memory, but like hearing the end of a sentence that had begun long ago.

“Asteron,” he said.

The mark on his palm pulsed softly.

The stone relic answered.

One line of light appeared on the map. It began at the tower and traveled southwest, crossing two circles that had gone dark and a nearly erased name—Larkhill—before stopping at the great circle of Asteron.

Calder sat with bound hands, staring at it with a pale face.

“The man who paid us...” he said. “He never said there was a city.”

Serena turned toward him. “What exactly did he tell you?”

“Take the stone to the tower. Open the vault. Bring back whatever was inside.”

Pip looked at the map.

“There is no vault.”

“No,” Lysara said. “But someone knew this tower was still connected to Asteron.”

“And wanted us to wake it?” Luca asked.

“Or wanted to know whether the network could still be awakened.”

Night wind moved through the valley. For the first time since their arrival, insects began to sing among the grass.

Serena looked at the luminous route leading southwest.

“We go back for Harlan and the donkey. Then Calder goes to the Guild.”

Calder raised his head. “And the stone?”

Serena took the relic.

“It isn't yours.”

“The Guild will take it.”

“Perhaps.”

“Then where are you going?”

Luca looked at the name on the map once more.

Exhaustion still hung through his entire body. His left hand felt burned and frozen at once. They had two wounded men, one prisoner, a donkey that might not be able to walk, and a long return journey ahead.

But the line of light continued to point southwest.

The road before them was no longer hidden.

“Asteron,” Luca answered.

The old network pulsed once beneath his feet.

Far beyond the hills, something answered.

**End of Chapter 12**
