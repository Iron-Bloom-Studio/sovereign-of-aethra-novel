# Sovereign of Aethra

## Chapter 10: The Old Road

The old road looked better from a distance.

After following it for half a day, Luca realized that most of its surface had vanished beneath grass, roots, and rain-washed soil. Stone markers leaned among the trees. Some had cracked, while others were almost completely covered in moss.

To the right of the road, the fence around an old field had collapsed. There were no crops, no livestock, and no smoke rising from any homes.

Only rotten wooden posts remained, creaking whenever the wind passed.

“Can this still be called a road?” Luca asked.

Pip walked several steps ahead of him. “If we are not lost, it is still a road.”

“That is a very low standard.”

“Low standards are easier to meet.”

Serena checked the assignment map. “The route still leads southwest. We have not left it.”

“Yet,” Luca said.

“You sound disappointed.”

“I’m beginning to understand why no wagons use it.”

Behind them, Lysara stopped beside one of the stone markers. She wiped the moss from its surface with the edge of a cloth, then copied the remaining symbol onto a small sheet of paper.

Zerak waited for her without speaking.

Pip suddenly raised one hand.

“Stop.”

They immediately fell silent.

The Goblin crouched at the edge of the road. His fingertips brushed aside flattened grass, revealing a broad cloven hoofprint.

“Tracks,” he said.

Serena folded the map at once.

Luca approached. The earth around the print had been pushed upward, as though something immensely heavy had recently passed.

“Forest Boar?” he asked.

Pip placed his palm beside the impression.

The print was nearly twice as wide as his hand.

“If that is a Forest Boar,” he said, “this forest feeds them far too well.”

Lysara joined them. She examined the track, then looked at a nearby tree. The bark had been scraped away from the lower trunk. The mark reached almost as high as Luca’s chest.

“Giant Boar,” she said.

Luca looked at the track again. “That sounds like a larger Forest Boar.”

“Essentially, it is.”

Pip stood and cleaned his hands. “I appreciate honest monster names.”

“A Giant Boar is an evolved form of the Forest Boar,” Lysara continued. “It is larger, its hide is thicker, and it is far more territorial.”

“How territorial?” Luca asked.

Crack.

A branch snapped beyond the trees.

Everyone froze.

Pip slowly turned toward the sound.

“I think it is going to explain that itself.”

The bushes beside the road shook violently.

A massive boar burst through them with a thunderous crash.

“Whoa—!”

Luca instinctively stepped back.

The monster stood almost as tall as Serena. Coarse dark-brown hair covered thick skin marked by old scars. Two curving tusks rose from its lower jaw, each long enough to pierce an ordinary wooden shield.

The ground trembled beneath its hooves.

Serena pulled the shield from her back.

“Spread out!”

The Giant Boar lowered its head and charged.

Serena did not move aside.

She stepped forward.

“Serena!” Luca shouted.

A dim brown light traveled from her feet into the ground. A block of stone rose behind her heels, forming a low brace.

Serena lowered her body and met the charge with her shield.

The impact sounded like a tree trunk splitting.

“Ngh!”

The stone brace cracked. Serena was driven back several steps, her boots carving long lines through the earth.

Her arm shook violently.

But she did not fall.

“Now!” she shouted.

Luca rushed in from the left with his sword drawn. He slashed the Giant Boar’s shoulder, but the blade cut through only part of its coarse hair and thick hide.

“Seriously?”

The monster growled and swung its head.

One tusk swept toward him.

“Whoa—!”

Luca threw himself down. The tusk passed barely a finger’s width from his chest.

He rolled and landed on his injured side.

“Aargh!”

Pain shot from his ribs into his back. The unhealed fracture felt as though it had been torn open again.

“Luca!” Serena shouted.

“Still alive!” he answered as he forced himself up. “My ribs are just unhappy!”

The Giant Boar turned to pursue him.

A Dark Bolt struck its front leg.

Black light briefly spread around the joint. The monster stumbled, but the sheer weight of its body kept it moving forward.

Zerak was already standing between Luca and the next charge.

He lowered his spear, drove the butt into the ground, and aimed its point at the monster’s shoulder.

“Do not take it alone!” Serena called.

“I do not intend to.”

The Giant Boar closed in.

Lysara swung her staff.

“Gust!”

Wind struck the side of the monster’s head. It was not enough to stop a body that large, but dust and dry leaves whirled directly across its eyes.

The Giant Boar shook its head and lost direction for a single breath.

That was enough for Zerak to shift aside.

The monster’s tusk passed his waist while the spearhead cut the inside of its weakened foreleg.

The Giant Boar roared.

A bolt flew from the roadside and struck behind its ear.

Pip was kneeling atop one of the stone markers, his small crossbow raised.

“That part is softer!” he shouted.

“You could have said that sooner!” Luca called back.

“I only just confirmed it!”

The Giant Boar stamped the ground. Mud and soil scattered as it turned toward Pip.

Pip looked at the monster.

Then he looked at the empty crossbow in his hands.

“Oh.”

The Giant Boar lowered its head.

Pip jumped from the stone.

“The bad part! This is the bad part!”

He rolled behind a fallen tree just as the monster charged.

The Giant Boar’s tusks smashed through the wood.

“Pip!” Luca raised a hand.

Heat gathered above his palm.

“Fireball!”

The ball of flame flew, but not toward the monster.

It struck the ground to the Giant Boar’s right. A small explosion of fire and dirt forced it to turn away from Pip.

Serena was waiting.

With a short cry, she drove the edge of her shield into the side of its head.

The wound behind its ear opened wider.

“Luca!”

Luca moved.

His ribs flared with pain again, but he forced his legs to keep running. He slipped between Serena and the Giant Boar, turned his sword, and thrust it into the softer flesh behind its front leg.

The blade sank in.

The Giant Boar thrashed.

“Ah—wait!”

Luca nearly lost his grip as the huge body spun. One of its hooves rose directly above him.

Something caught the back of his clothes.

Luca was dragged away an instant before the hoof slammed into the ground.

Zerak released him.

“You were too close.”

Luca panted. “I use a sword.”

“Your sword is not very long.”

“That is a common weakness among swords.”

The Giant Boar tried to charge again.

Its foreleg buckled.

Zerak’s Dark Bolt, the spear wound, and Luca’s thrust had finally broken its balance. Its massive body struck the ground with a heavy crash.

The monster tried to rise one final time.

Serena moved beside its head. With one precise thrust of her sword, she ended the battle.

The forest fell silent again.

No one spoke immediately.

Serena breathed heavily. Her left hand still gripped her shield, but the entire arm shook from the impact.

Luca sat on the ground, clutching his side.

From behind the broken tree trunk, a pair of green ears slowly appeared.

Pip raised his crossbow.

“My crossbow survived.”

Serena stared at him.

Pip paused.

“So did I.”

“Thank goodness,” Luca said. “I was worried about your crossbow.”

Pip nodded solemnly. “I know.”

Lysara approached Luca. “May I examine your ribs?”

“There is no need. They are still in the same place.”

“That is not an examination.”

Pip came closer as well.

Before Luca realized what he intended, Pip extended one finger and pressed his injured side.

“Agh—ow, ow! Don’t press there!”

Luca immediately doubled over and slapped Pip’s hand away.

The Goblin withdrew his finger.

“Not healed yet.”

“I already knew that!”

“Now we both know.”

Luca glared at him through the pain. “That knowledge was not worth the way you obtained it.”

The corner of Serena’s mouth moved slightly.

Even Lysara’s shoulders trembled as she held back a laugh.

Zerak looked at Pip, then at Luca’s ribs.

“Should I confirm it as well?”

“No,” Luca answered quickly.

***

After making certain no other monsters were nearby, Lysara examined the Giant Boar.

She found several old bite wounds on the back of its body. The spacing between the fangs suggested a predator of considerable size.

“Wolves?” Serena asked.

“Possibly,” Lysara replied. “But one Forest Wolf would not attack an adult Giant Boar.”

“What if there were many?” Luca asked.

“That would be different.”

Pip had drawn his dagger and was inspecting which parts of the Giant Boar they could take.

“We cannot carry all of it.”

“We are not leaving it in the middle of the road either,” Serena said.

“We take enough meat for ourselves and drag the rest away from the path.” Pip pointed toward the forest. “And we do not camp anywhere near this place.”

Luca tried to stand.

Pain stabbed through his ribs again.

“Agh…”

He stopped, half bent over.

Lysara immediately moved closer. “Do not force yourself.”

“We cannot remain here.”

“We cannot travel either if the fracture worsens.”

Luca placed his left hand over the injury. Pale light gathered in his palm.

“Lesser Heal.”

The light spread.

This time, however, the warmth did not stop at the skin and muscle surrounding the injury.

Luca felt the magic flow deeper. Heat wrapped around the fractured rib, calming the sharp pulse that had followed every breath.

The faint lines on his left palm glowed.

“Huh?”

Luca stared at his hand.

Lysara immediately knelt beside him. “Do not interrupt the flow.”

“I’m not doing anything differently.”

“Which is why I am telling you not to interrupt it.”

The light remained for several seconds longer than Lesser Heal usually did, then slowly faded.

Luca drew a deep breath.

He waited for the pain.

The sharp stab did not come.

Only a dull ache remained in his side.

He slowly turned his body.

“Much better.”

Pip raised one finger again.

“Do not even think about it.”

Pip lowered it.

Lysara studied the last traces of light in Luca’s palm.

“That was not Lesser Heal.”

Luca frowned. “I said Lesser Heal.”

“The name you say does not determine the spell’s form if the flow of Aethra has already changed.”

“Then what is it called now?”

“Heal.”

Pip nodded. “They removed one word.”

Luca stared at him. “Thank you for that profound explanation.”

Serena tried to move her left arm. Her fingers were still trembling.

Luca stood in front of her.

“Your arm.”

“It is only numb.”

“That still counts as an injury.”

“You just used Fireball and a healing spell you do not understand.”

“I can still do it.”

“Luca.”

He extended his hand. “If I start falling over, you may tell me you were right.”

“I can tell you that now.”

“True. But this way you will have evidence.”

Serena sighed, then held out her arm.

Luca placed his hand over the injury.

“Heal.”

Soft light surrounded Serena’s arm from elbow to wrist. It was brighter than Lesser Heal, yet remained calm and gentle.

The trembling in her fingers gradually stopped.

When the spell faded, Luca pulled his hand away.

The ground seemed to tilt beneath him.

“Whoa…”

His body leaned sideways.

Lysara caught his arm before he truly fell.

“Twice in succession is enough.”

“I’m fine.”

“You nearly fell,” Serena said.

“I only stood up too quickly.”

“You were already standing.”

Luca looked at them. “I am beginning to regret teaching all of you to notice small details.”

“You never taught us that,” Pip said.

Serena moved her fingers. Most of the stiffness in her arm had disappeared.

“It is stronger.”

“But it also consumes more Aethra,” Lysara explained. “Heal accelerates the recovery of deeper injuries, including small fractures. It cannot instantly mend a severely broken bone.”

“So my rib is not fully healed?”

“Not yet.”

Pip raised his finger again.

Luca pointed at him. “I will bite your finger.”

Pip put both hands in his pockets.

***

Near evening, they encountered four people traveling in the opposite direction.

An old man pulled a small handcart with a cracked wheel. Behind him walked a young woman, a boy, and a man whose leg was wrapped in cloth.

They carried nearly all their belongings in two sacks.

The group stopped when they saw Zerak.

Serena slowly moved her hand away from her sword. Luca stepped forward and displayed the Guild insignia on their assignment papers.

“We are from the Arklune Guild,” he said. “Are you heading to the city?”

The old man nodded, although his eyes remained fixed on Zerak.

“We are not looking for trouble,” Luca continued.

Zerak stood behind the others. His spear was upright and pointed at no one.

The boy peeked out from behind his mother.

“Is that armor?” he suddenly asked.

Everyone went silent.

Zerak looked down at his own body, then back at the child.

“No.”

“Can you take it off?”

“No.”

The boy considered the answer with great seriousness.

“Bathing must be difficult.”

“Hah—”

Luca turned his laugh into a cough.

Pip covered his mouth with both hands. His shoulders shook.

The old man turned pale. The boy’s mother quickly pulled him back.

Zerak merely tilted his head.

“I have not determined that yet.”

Pip could no longer contain himself.

He snorted loudly and turned away.

Some of the tension faded.

Luca looked at the damaged cart wheel. “Where did you come from?”

“A small settlement about a day from here,” the old man answered. “Trade wagons used to pass through. We have not seen one in months.”

“Why did you leave?”

“Monsters have been coming closer to the fields. Forest Boars destroy the crops. Wolves take livestock at night.” He glanced at the man with the injured leg. “We tried to endure it. But people began leaving one by one.”

“No adventurers came to help?” Luca asked.

“We sent a message to the old Guild post. No one answered.”

Luca looked down the road behind them.

“How many people remain?”

“Not many.”

The old man gave no number.

Luca thought of Aven.

If a field in Aven was damaged, the villagers repaired it together. If a monster approached, someone warned the entire village. It was not always orderly, and rarely happened without an argument, but no one truly stood alone.

Until now, Luca had assumed every small settlement had something like that.

He had been wrong.

Pip opened one of their supply bags.

“We can give them enough food for two days.”

Serena looked at him. “We still have a long journey ahead.”

“I counted before I spoke.”

“Are you certain?”

Pip removed two packets of dried food. “I always count the food.”

Luca accepted the packets and gave them to the old man.

“Arklune’s southern gate remains open to travelers until sunset. If you maintain your pace, you should arrive before nightfall tomorrow.”

The man bowed several times.

Serena helped secure the cart wheel with extra rope. Luca noticed the bandage around the younger man’s leg.

Faint light began to gather around his hand.

Lysara touched his wrist.

“The injury is old,” she whispered. “Closing the surface now will not repair the damage underneath.”

The light in Luca’s hand faded.

Heal was stronger than his old spell.

But it still could not fix everything.

They parted after ensuring the cart could still move. The boy continued looking back at Zerak until his group disappeared around the bend.

Luca remained there for some time, watching the empty road.

“He was right,” Pip said.

Luca turned. “About what?”

Pip pointed at Zerak. “Bathing must be difficult.”

Zerak looked at him.

Pip immediately walked faster.

***

They found a campsite shortly before sunset.

The place had once been a small roadside shelter. Most of its wooden roof had collapsed, but three stone pillars remained and offered some protection from the wind. The surrounding ground was open enough that nothing could approach unseen.

Serena inspected the entire area before allowing them to put down their supplies.

Pip inspected it again afterward.

“You do not trust me?” Serena asked.

“I trust you,” Pip replied. “I do not trust the bushes.”

Lysara chose a place near the fire for her notes. She opened the waterproof wrapping, removed several sheets of paper and one bottle of ink, then began recording the day’s observations.

Zerak planted his spear in the ground within reach.

Luca looked at the Giant Boar meat Pip had prepared.

“I can roast it.”

Pip narrowed his eyes. “Can you?”

“I helped cook in Aven.”

“That is not the same answer.”

“I can put meat over a fire.”

Pip handed him one piece. “That is the first step.”

Serena began arranging the watch schedule beside one of the stone pillars.

“We rotate every two hours. I will take the first watch.”

“I can remain awake longer,” Zerak said.

“Everyone still gets time to rest.”

“I do not mind.”

“I did not ask whether you minded.”

Zerak regarded Serena for several moments.

“Do all Auregrad soldiers speak like that?”

“When circumstances require it.”

“Circumstances seem to require it often.”

Lysara lowered her face toward her notes, but a small smile appeared on her lips.

Luca placed the meat on a thin metal plate above the fire. Fat immediately began to hiss, releasing a rich aroma.

Soon the surface turned brown.

Then black.

Pip approached.

“It is cooked,” Luca said.

Pip pierced it with the tip of his dagger.

Red liquid ran from the center.

“The outside is burned.”

“That means it is cooked.”

“The inside is still raw.”

“That can be fixed.”

Zerak studied the meat from the other side of the fire.

“The center is moving.”

Luca stared at him. “It is not moving.”

The meat made a small sound as the fat popped.

Zerak pointed. “It moved.”

Luca immediately put it back over the fire.

Lysara laughed.

It was more than her usual faint smile—a brief, genuine laugh that made everyone turn toward her.

She quickly covered her mouth.

“I’m sorry.”

“You do not need to apologize,” Luca said. “Unless you also think my cooking is terrible.”

Lysara looked at the meat, black on the outside and red at its center.

“I do not yet have enough information to reach a conclusion.”

Pip nodded. “The scholarly way of saying your cooking is terrible.”

Pip eventually took over.

He sliced the meat into thinner pieces, adjusted their distance from the fire, then added a little salt and some fragrant leaves he had found near the road.

Luca sat with empty hands.

“I feel as though I have been removed from my own task.”

“You were reassigned to a more suitable position,” Serena said.

“Which is?”

“Watching the fire.”

The meal was ready after the sky had grown dark.

For the first time since their party had formed, all five sat together without ruins, monsters, or Guild officials waiting for an answer.

Serena placed her shield on her right, still close enough to reach. Lysara put away her notes before eating. Pip sat on a fallen log so he was level with the others.

Zerak chose a place just outside the circle of firelight.

Luca patted the empty ground beside him.

“You can keep watch from here.”

Zerak studied the spot.

“I can see farther from where I am.”

“You can see the forest from here too.”

Zerak did not move immediately.

Luca took another piece of meat. “Besides, if the food makes us sick, someone will need to carry us back to Arklune.”

“Pip cooked it,” Serena said.

“Then our chances of survival are better.”

Zerak finally sat in the open place.

He accepted the meat Pip offered, examined it briefly, and took a bite.

Everyone waited.

Zerak chewed.

“Well?” Pip asked.

“It is not moving.”

Lysara laughed again.

Luca stared into the fire. “I will remember this.”

Their conversation gradually drifted away from the road and toward smaller things unrelated to their assignment.

Pip explained that some Goblins found direction by studying where moss grew, then admitted that he preferred the sun because moss often grew in unhelpful places.

Lysara began explaining that moss could actually—

Pip raised both hands. “No. We do not need three pages about moss.”

“I was only going to explain—”

“Two pages is still too many.”

Serena mentioned that her Auregrad training had once required her to sleep in the rain without a tent.

Luca frowned. “Was that training, or punishment with a different name?”

Serena took another piece of meat.

“It built discipline.”

“So it was punishment.”

Serena did not answer.

Zerak mostly listened.

But he was no longer sitting outside the circle of firelight.

For a moment, the old road, the missing relic, and the mark on Luca’s hand all felt very far away.

Then Pip lifted his head.

His smile vanished.

“Quiet.”

The fire crackled between them.

There were no insects.

No small animals rustled through the brush.

The forest had become too silent.

Lysara closed her eyes. A thin current of air circled their camp, passing through the grass and between the trees.

She opened her eyes.

“Movement from three directions.”

Serena was already standing with her shield in hand.

Zerak pulled his spear from the ground.

Beyond the firelight, a pair of yellow eyes reflected in the darkness.

Then another pair.

Then four.

A wolf stepped between the trees. It was thin, but its shoulders still reached Pip’s waist. Its gray coat was dirty and covered in small wounds.

“Forest Wolf,” Lysara said.

A second wolf appeared from the other side.

Then a third.

They did not attack immediately. They spread out and circled the camp at measured distances.

“They are coordinated,” Serena said.

A low growl came from the darkness.

A larger figure entered the moonlight.

The wolf was nearly twice the size of the others. Its fur was dark gray, with a black line running down its back. One ear was torn, and a scar crossed its muzzle.

“Dire Wolf,” Lysara said. “An evolved Forest Wolf.”

Pip raised his crossbow. “Another honest name?”

“Unfortunately, yes.”

The Dire Wolf growled.

The pack moved.

“Stay near the fire!” Serena shouted. “Do not let them separate us!”

Three Forest Wolves approached from the left.

Serena raised her sword and struck the flat of the blade against her shield.

The metallic crash split the night.

“Shield Challenge!”

She advanced with her shield raised, making herself the greatest threat in the center of the camp.

Two Forest Wolves immediately turned toward her.

Serena met the first with her shield.

“Ngh!”

The wolf struck the metal surface and was thrown back when she drove forward.

The second tried to bite her leg.

Serena stamped the ground. A low layer of stone rose before her boot, and the monster’s jaws snapped against the hard surface.

The third Forest Wolf ignored the others.

It circled around Serena and ran toward Lysara.

Lysara turned her staff. Wind gathered along the red crystal at its tip, compressing into a thin curve.

“Wind Blade.”

A crescent of air flew forward.

It did not cut the Forest Wolf apart, but it opened a wound across its shoulder and spun it through the air. The monster hit the ground with a yelp and retreated with one leg raised.

Luca looked over. “Since when can you do that?”

“I could already do it.”

“You only used Gust before.”

“Gust was safer inside the ruins.”

Luca blocked a Forest Wolf’s claws with his sword.

“Fair enough!”

Another wolf leaped from behind Lysara.

Zerak turned faster.

He could not bring his spear around in time, so he used his own arm as a barrier.

The wolf’s claws scraped across the carapace on his forearm.

Zerak released a low growl.

He seized the creature by the neck, turned his body, and threw it to the ground.

Pip fired his crossbow.

Tak!

The bolt struck the earth directly in front of the wolf’s nose.

Zerak looked at him.

Pip lowered his crossbow slightly. “You threw it.”

“Yes.”

“After I aimed.”

“Aim faster.”

Pip clicked his tongue and loaded another bolt.

The Dire Wolf had not moved.

It stood outside the light and watched them.

Then its eyes shifted toward Pip.

Pip had barely raised his crossbow when it began to run.

“Ah, no. Not me again!”

Pip fired.

The bolt struck the Dire Wolf’s shoulder, but not deeply enough to stop it.

Serena struck her shield again.

“Over here!”

Two Forest Wolves reacted to the noise.

The Dire Wolf did not.

“The large one did not take the bait!” Luca shouted.

“I noticed!”

Pip jumped from the fallen log.

The Dire Wolf chased him even as Serena tried to cut across its path. It changed direction quickly and slipped past the edge of her shield.

“Pip, behind me!”

“I’m trying!”

Pip ran toward one of the ruined stone pillars.

Zerak moved in from the side.

Black light gathered along the shaft of his spear, flowing forward until it covered the spearhead.

Unlike Dark Bolt, the darkness was not released.

It remained bound to the weapon.

Zerak lowered his body.

“Dark Lance.”

The first thrust struck the Dire Wolf’s foreleg.

Dark light spread from the wound, weakening the muscles around it.

Zerak pulled the spear back, turned the shaft, and thrust again into its shoulder.

The Dire Wolf roared and swung one claw.

Zerak jumped back.

The tips scraped across the carapace on his chest.

“Ngh!”

The dark coating on his spear faded.

His breathing grew heavier.

The Dire Wolf had lost strength along one side of its body, but it continued chasing Pip.

Pip disappeared behind the stone pillar.

The Dire Wolf followed.

A heavy impact sounded from the other side.

“Pip!” Luca shouted.

The Dire Wolf emerged from behind the pillar.

Pip was not in front of it.

He was behind it.

The Goblin sprang from the pillar’s shadow with his dagger drawn. He used a low stone as a foothold and threw himself against the Dire Wolf’s side.

His first thrust sank behind the foreleg weakened by Dark Lance.

Before the creature could turn, Pip ripped the dagger free and thrust again into the gap beneath its shoulder.

Two rapid strikes.

**Double Thrust.**

The Dire Wolf roared and spun wildly.

“Whoa—!”

Pip released his dagger, dropped to the ground, and rolled away just before its jaws closed where his head had been.

He rose, breathing hard.

“Ambush successful.”

“Did you deliberately make it chase you?” Luca asked.

“The deliberate part began after it started chasing me!”

The Dire Wolf tried to attack again.

Its front leg buckled.

The wounds from Dark Lance and Double Thrust left one side of its body barely able to support its weight.

But its ferocity remained.

The creature dragged itself forward, still focused on Pip.

Serena came from the side.

With a sharp cry, she slammed her shield into the Dire Wolf’s jaw.

The monster was thrown from its path.

“Behind me!” she ordered.

This time, Pip did not argue.

The Dire Wolf rose unsteadily.

Behind it, two Forest Wolves tried to approach Lysara again.

Luca raised a hand.

He still felt the emptiness left by using Heal twice. Heat did not gather in his palm as quickly as usual.

“Come on…”

The flame finally formed.

He did not have enough strength to cast Fireball repeatedly.

One would have to be enough.

“Fireball!”

The sphere struck the ground between the Dire Wolf and the rest of its pack. Fire briefly spread across the bare earth, breaking their formation.

Lysara aimed her staff.

“Gust!”

Low wind held the embers inside the cleared ground and kept them from reaching the dry grass.

The Dire Wolf had no path back to its pack.

Serena stood in front of Pip.

Zerak took the left side with a spear no longer coated in Dark.

Luca approached from the right with his sword drawn.

The Dire Wolf growled at all three.

Then it charged.

Serena received the impact on her shield.

“Ngh—!”

Her feet slid, but she held her position.

Zerak hooked his spear around the monster’s weakened foreleg.

Luca slashed its side and forced it to turn its head.

When its jaws moved away, Serena saw her opening.

She drove forward with her shield, forced the creature’s front half down, and plunged her sword beneath its throat.

The Dire Wolf collapsed.

The remaining Forest Wolves stopped.

One by one, they retreated into the trees. They did not attempt to recover their leader. Within moments, the last reflection of their eyes vanished into the darkness.

No one moved until the sounds of the forest slowly returned.

Pip stepped out from behind Serena’s shield and recovered his dagger.

Serena turned toward him.

“When I order you behind my shield, do it before a monster nearly bites your head off.”

“It was not interested in your shield.”

“That is not a reason to keep running away.”

“I needed the stone pillar.”

“You could have told us your plan.”

“I only found the plan when I saw the pillar.”

Serena closed her eyes for a moment.

Luca patted her shoulder. “He is still alive.”

“That does not make his method correct.”

“No,” Luca said. “But it does make him difficult to scold.”

“I can still do it.”

Pip raised his dagger. “I will clean this somewhere Serena cannot see me.”

He quickly walked away.

Lysara knelt beside the Dire Wolf. Like the Giant Boar, its body was covered in old wounds. Several ribs were visible beneath its thick fur.

“They were starving,” she said.

“Starving enough to attack five people beside a fire,” Serena replied.

“And desperate enough to hunt outside their usual territory.”

Luca looked at the meat remaining beside the camp.

“Did they follow the smell?”

“Perhaps,” Lysara said. “But that does not explain why the Giant Boar and this pack were both so close to the road.”

Zerak inspected his spearhead. The trace of Dark that had surrounded it was gone.

“That technique was different from Dark Bolt,” Lysara said.

“Yes.”

“Dark Lance?”

Zerak looked at his spear as though the name had returned before the memory.

“My body remembers the movement.”

“But you do not?”

“Not all of it.”

Lysara did not press him.

Luca stood and carefully touched his ribs.

The battle should have worsened the pain. Instead, Heal had repaired most of the injury. Only a dull ache remained when he breathed too deeply.

Pip returned and noticed his hand.

“I am only checking with my eyes,” he said.

“Good.”

Luca’s magic had undoubtedly grown.

He simply did not know why.

***

They slept less than planned.

Serena revised the watch schedule. This time, no one argued with her caution.

At sunrise, they buried the embers, packed their equipment, and returned to the old road.

Pip inspected his dagger and crossbow several times before putting them away.

Serena walked beside him.

“Tell us your next plan before carrying it out.”

“If I have time.”

“Make time.”

“Do all Auregrad soldiers demand impossible things?”

“When circumstances require it.”

Pip looked at Luca. “She said it again.”

“I heard her.”

They continued for several hours before seeing the roof of a building through the trees.

The old Guild post stood beside the road.

It was a small, single-story structure with a watchtower whose roof had partly collapsed. The Guild emblem above the door was barely visible beneath the moss.

The door stood open.

Serena raised one hand, signaling the others to stop.

Pip approached the ground in front of the building and crouched.

“Tracks.”

“Recent?” Luca asked.

“No more than two days old.”

Lysara examined the ashes in the firepit beside the building. She extended one hand without touching them.

“Someone spent the night here.”

Zerak pointed toward the soft ground behind the post.

Three sets of footprints left the yard. Beside them were the hoofprints of a pack animal.

All of them led southwest.

Pip found something caught on a nail in the doorframe.

A strand of black cloth.

Its surface was coated in wax.

Luca looked at the road stretching beyond the post.

They were no longer following only a direction.

They had found the trail again.

**End of Chapter 10**
