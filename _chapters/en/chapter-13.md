# Sovereign of Aethra

## Chapter 13: The Road to Asteron

They found Harlan in the hollow shortly before midnight.

He was alive, conscious, and holding a broken piece of wood like a weapon.

When Serena emerged from the grass, he raised it with both hands. He stopped when Zerak followed her out of the darkness with Venn over one shoulder.

“You made it,” Harlan said.

Pip pulled the rope binding Calder's hands. “Depends on how you define made it.”

Calder stumbled down the slope. His coat was covered in tower dust, and one side of his face had begun to bruise from striking the stone floor.

Harlan stared at him.

“You left me.”

Calder said nothing.

“I told you we should turn back after the donkey fell.”

“And lose the entire payment?” Calder finally replied. “We had already reached the valley.”

“We nearly died in that valley.”

“But we didn't.”

“Bad argument,” Luca said. “I use it often.”

Serena glanced at him.

“I'm trying to stop.”

“Your effort is not visible.”

Harlan laughed, then immediately held his thigh. Luca's Heal had stopped the bleeding, but the tissue beneath had not fully recovered.

They moved everyone to level ground near the stream. Lysara examined Venn and found a weak but steady pulse. The wound on his head was shallow, but exposure to the relic's Aethra kept his body trembling.

“He needs rest,” she said. “And someone must watch him through the night.”

“I'll take the first watch,” Serena said.

“You just stopped two golems,” Luca replied.

“And you nearly burned both arms.”

“That's an exaggeration.”

“Your glove is smoking.”

Luca looked at his left hand.

She was right.

Thin smoke still escaped through the leather seams.

Pip sat on a stone. “I can watch.”

Serena looked at him.

“I'm not injured,” Pip continued. “My dagger is slightly bent, and my pride is damaged because I was nearly crushed by two golems, but my body is fine.”

Zerak planted his spear in the earth. “I will join him.”

“You absorbed an Aethra overflow,” Lysara said.

“I require less sleep.”

Luca pointed at him. “He says that as though it answers everything.”

“Usually sufficient,” Zerak replied.

In the end, they divided the night into three watches. Pip and Zerak took the first. Serena took the second. Luca and Lysara received the final watch after both recovered enough strength to stand without arguing.

The stone relic lay in the middle of their circle, wrapped in the waxed cloth the thieves had used. The black fracture across its surface had stopped growing, but the metal ring around it shifted every few minutes.

Like a compass needle that had not yet found north.

Whenever it moved, the mark in Luca's palm grew warm.

He did not sleep well.

In his dreams, luminous roads moved beneath the earth. Some ended in darkness. Others led to broken towers. All of them converged on something vast, far to the southwest.

Luca could not see its shape.

He felt only one pulse.

Then a second.

He woke before the third.

***

Dawn arrived with low mist.

Venn finally opened his eyes.

He remembered nothing after the crate broke. His final memory was of someone whispering from inside the stone.

“What did it say?” Lysara asked.

Venn closed his eyes, trying to catch the remains of the memory.

“Not words. More like... a direction.”

He weakly raised one hand and pointed southwest.

The relic moved beneath its wrapping.

Pip took half a step back. “I don't like objects that listen to conversations.”

“It responds to the flow,” Lysara said.

“That is the scholarly version of the same thing.”

The donkey waited where they had left it, grazing near the trees. Its rear leg remained stiff, but it could bear weight again. Luca applied a small Heal, enough to reduce the swelling without draining himself.

“We cannot take four injured people toward Asteron,” Serena said.

Calder raised his head. “I'm not injured.”

Pip pulled the rope at his wrists. “Can be arranged.”

“Pip.”

“I was only presenting an option.”

Serena unfolded the old road map they had found at the Guild post. The main route toward Arklune lay northeast. The road to Asteron went in the opposite direction through Larkhill.

“We return to the junction,” she said. “From there, Harlan and Venn head for Arklune with Calder.”

“With him?” Harlan looked at Calder in disbelief.

“His hands remain bound.”

“He could cut the rope while we sleep.”

Pip raised a piece of Calder's crossbow mechanism. “Not without a knife. There are no knives.”

Calder glared at him.

Pip smiled.

“I checked twice.”

Luca looked toward the eastern road. “They still won't be safe traveling as three.”

“Four,” Harlan said, pointing at the donkey.

“The donkey cannot fight monsters.”

“You've never seen an angry donkey.”

Serena rolled up the map. “We escort them until we find a group bound for Arklune. If we find none, we return to the Guild post and change the plan.”

The decision cost almost half the day, but no one objected.

They moved slowly. Venn sat on the donkey while Harlan walked with a pine branch for a cane. Calder remained in the middle, his hands tied to a long rope held by Pip.

“Why him?” Calder asked.

“Because I won't feel guilty if you fall,” Pip replied.

The forest path that had felt threatening the day before now carried signs of life. Birds called again. Insects moved among the leaves. Monster tracks leading toward the valley crossed newer tracks moving away.

The tower's call had truly ended.

Its consequences had not.

They found a deer carcass beside the river, trampled by a herd that had moved without direction. Young trees had broken. A bird's nest lay on the ground.

Lysara stopped to return two chicks to a low branch.

“Balance does not return in one night,” she said.

Zerak studied tracks in the mud. “But their directions belong to them again.”

Luca looked at him. “Are you talking about the monsters?”

Zerak paused.

“Yes.”

The answer was true.

It was not complete.

Near midday, they heard small bells from the main road.

A caravan emerged between the trees: two covered wagons, six merchants, and four Guild guards. They carried flour, oil, and metal tools toward Arklune from a settlement in the east.

The lead guard immediately raised her spear at the sight of Zerak.

Serena stepped forward and showed her Auregrad insignia and the Guild assignment.

“We are Open Road. These are witnesses and a prisoner from a relic incident in the western valley.”

The guard leader, a middle-aged woman named Mara, read the short report Serena had written on Lysara's paper.

“Three thieves?” she asked.

“Two injured witnesses,” Luca said. “One prisoner.”

Calder scoffed. “They also have what we found.”

Serena did not look at him. “The relic is dangerous and remains evidence until the Guild can secure it properly.”

Mara examined Calder and then the rope in Pip's hands.

“Which one is the prisoner?”

“The one who talks too much,” Pip said.

Mara accepted custody. Venn remained on the donkey, Harlan received a place in the rear wagon, and Calder was chained to the guards' bench.

Before climbing aboard, Harlan stopped in front of Luca.

“I did not ask you to save me.”

“I know.”

“I set a trap for you.”

“I know that too.”

“Why?”

Luca looked at the wound he had closed.

“Because you were alive when we found you.”

Harlan exhaled, as though the simple answer was more difficult to accept.

“If the Guild doesn't hang me, I'll repay the food you gave me.”

Pip raised one finger. “Two packets.”

“Serena said one.”

“Interest.”

Serena caught the back of Pip's collar before he could move closer.

The caravan rolled northeast.

Calder did not say goodbye.

Venn weakly raised one hand.

Harlan struck the side of the wagon twice.

Open Road remained at the junction until the wheels faded from hearing.

Then Serena turned southwest.

“Now, Asteron.”

***

The relic found the road even when the map could not.

Its metal ring moved whenever they approached a junction, then stopped when Luca faced the correct direction. Lysara recorded each change. Pip marked trees and stones so they could find the way back.

Near evening, the remains of an old military road emerged beneath the brush.

Its great paving stones were almost buried, but their arrangement was too straight to be natural. Broken pillars lined the route, each marked with the same emblem engraved at the tower: a great circle connected by six lines.

“Asteron's crest?” Luca asked.

“Possibly,” Lysara replied. “Six routes leading to one center.”

“Six affinities,” Serena said.

“Or six districts. We don't know yet.”

Pip touched one pillar. Short scratches covered its lower half.

“Not writing.”

Serena crouched. “Travel counts. Soldiers or merchants marking distance.”

“So this road was once busy.”

“Very.”

They followed the paving stones until the forest opened.

Larkhill appeared beneath the evening light.

It had no walls. Only a collection of stone and timber houses built around a well. Several roofs had collapsed. Grass grew inside homes. The apple orchard to the west had expanded without an owner, its branches heavy with small, untended fruit.

Luca stopped at the village edge.

“Empty?”

Zerak tilted his head.

“No large breaths. Many small animals.”

Pip was already moving toward the nearest house. He examined the door, the ground, and the windows.

“No recent human tracks,” he said. “Several months at least.”

A small bell still hung from the front beam of the meeting hall. Its rope had rotted, but the metal endured.

Lysara read the inscription beneath it.

“‘For those who depart. For those who return.’”

Serena looked back at the road.

“The Guild record said Larkhill's final three families left about two years ago.”

“Maybe they rang this bell when they left,” Luca said.

No one tried to ring it again.

Pip found a storehouse near the well. He opened the door, looked inside, and immediately closed it.

“What is it?” Luca asked.

“Spiders.”

“Monsters?”

“Worse.”

Serena raised her shield. “How large?”

“Small.”

They stared at him.

“Many small spiders,” Pip explained. “No room to fight them individually. An unreasonable enemy.”

Lysara hid a smile.

Zerak opened the second door on the storehouse. Dozens of spiders scattered into the grass.

Pip jumped onto a barrel.

“A warning would have helped.”

“They are small,” Zerak said.

“That is not a defense.”

They chose a stone house beside the well for the night. Its roof remained intact and the chimney was clear. Serena checked the walls. Pip tied metal scraps to cords across both entrances. Zerak patrolled the village. Luca and Lysara drew water from the well.

The water was clear.

Lysara tested a drop with her cracked crystal.

“No Aethra contamination.”

Luca looked at his reflection in the bucket.

“Why abandon a place with clean water and a fruit orchard?”

“Monsters on the road. Trade stopped. The Guild post closed.” Lysara lifted the bucket with him. “A settlement does not die only because its houses fall. Sometimes it dies because no one has a reason to pass through its doors.”

They cooked a thin stew with dried meat and wild apples. It tasted strange, but Pip took two bowls.

“You like it?” Luca asked.

“I respect available food.”

After eating, Lysara spread her papers across the floor.

She drew the tower, the lines from the stone map, and Larkhill's position. The relic lay in the center. Its ring slowly pointed southwest.

“The tower we stopped was only one regulator,” she said. “This network may once have channeled Aethra toward Asteron from the surrounding region.”

Serena crossed her arms. “If one damaged regulator can displace monsters across a valley, what can the center of the network do?”

“Control every route at once.”

“Or damage them.”

“Yes.”

The room fell quiet.

Pip held an apple. “We can still return to Arklune and bring twenty people with more experience.”

“We can,” Serena said.

“But by the time they arrive, whoever hired Calder may have taken whatever they are looking for.”

“Also true.”

Pip bit into the apple.

“I only want every bad decision discussed before we choose one.”

Zerak returned from his patrol and sat near the door.

“The road is safe for tonight.”

Luca looked at each of them.

“I want to see Asteron.”

Serena did not answer immediately.

“Because of the mark on your hand?”

“Partly.”

“The other part?”

Luca looked at the small bell through the window.

“Someone damaged its Core. Someone else paid to open a path there. Larkhill lost its reason to live when this road died.” He drew a breath. “If we return now, we bring only questions. If we continue, we might learn what we actually need to report.”

Lysara nodded. “I agree.”

Zerak said, “As do I.”

Pip pointed toward the stew. “I will agree after I receive the last portion.”

Serena pushed the pot toward him.

“We leave at dawn.”

***

Luca woke before sunrise.

Not because of a sound.

The mark in his left palm felt as if something were pulling it from beneath the skin.

He left the house without waking the others. Mist covered the village road. The last moonlight reflected from the broken roofs.

Near the well, a narrow line glowed beneath the ground.

He could see it only when he looked slightly away. The line crossed Larkhill from northeast to southwest, following the old road.

Zerak stood beside the well.

Luca nearly drew his sword.

“Do you always stand silently in the dark?”

“Not always.”

“That makes it worse.”

Zerak looked at the luminous line.

“You see it too?”

“I feel its vibration.”

Luca stood beside him.

“You don't sleep?”

“I require less sleep than humans.”

“That isn't a pleasant answer.”

“It was not intended to be.”

They looked southwest.

Beyond the hills, the sky seemed darker even as dawn touched the east.

“At the tower,” Luca said, “I felt your Dark. It wasn't what I expected.”

Zerak remained still.

“What did you expect?”

“Emptiness. Cold. Something that erases.”

“And what did you feel?”

Luca searched for the right word.

“Space.”

The light in Zerak's red eyes dimmed slightly.

“The Chorus called Dark the place that remains after something is lost.”

“The Chorus?”

Zerak looked at him.

For a moment, Luca thought he would not answer.

“I do not remember enough.”

It was the first time Zerak had mentioned his past without being asked.

Luca did not push.

“What if your memories return?”

“I do not know whether I wish to become the one who remembers them.”

“You don't have to become him.”

“How do you know?”

Luca looked at the mark on his hand.

“I don't know who I was before all this either.”

Zerak watched the line of light.

“Yet you continue walking.”

“It has worked so far.”

“Bad argument.”

Luca smiled. “I use it often.”

By the time the others woke, the mist had begun to lift.

They left Larkhill after filling their water skins and taking several apples. Pip drew a small symbol on the door of the house where they had slept: a circle with an open line leading toward the road.

“What does it mean?” Lysara asked.

“Safe place.”

“That is not a Guild symbol.”

“Now it is an Open Road symbol.”

The road descended from Larkhill into a wide plain.

Trees grew sparse. Grass replaced brush. More of the old road emerged intact, as if the earth could not swallow it any farther.

Then the mist ahead of them moved.

The remains of a wall appeared first.

Its upper edge had broken, but the wall stretched far to either side. Beyond it stood roofless towers, stone bridges, and buildings split by great roots.

Luca stopped.

He had always imagined Asteron as a ruin beneath the earth—a sealed chamber swallowed by the ground after the war.

He had been wrong.

Asteron stood directly before them.

Open beneath the sky.

Its roads disappeared beneath wild growth. Towers stood like bones that refused to fall. Birds circled over the central buildings. Morning light reflected from several windows that remained intact.

The city was not hidden by magic.

It merely stood at the end of a road no one used anymore.

As if, for forty-three years, the world had chosen not to see it.

Serena approached the gate.

One door had collapsed. The other remained upright, held by rust and roots. An inscription above the stone arch was almost concealed by moss.

Pip climbed the rubble and wiped it clean with his sleeve.

Lysara read:

**ASTERON GUARDS THE WESTERN ROAD.**

“The road to where?” Pip asked.

No one answered.

The relic in Serena's hand turned.

Its ring pointed directly into the city.

Luca stepped through the gate. The mark on his palm grew hot. He placed his hand against the cold wall.

A single line of light moved from beneath his fingers.

It followed the engraving across the gate, descended through the pillar, then vanished beneath the main road.

Far inside Asteron, something beat once.

The birds above the city took flight together.

Then silence returned.

The city had been waiting.

And now, for the first time in forty-three years, someone had knocked at its door.

**End of Chapter 13**
