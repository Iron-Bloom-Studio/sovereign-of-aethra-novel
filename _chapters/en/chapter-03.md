# Chapter 3: The Adventurer’s Mark

Luca woke before sunrise.

Not because of discipline.

Not because of excitement either.

Someone in the room next door was snoring like a dying Forest Boar.

Luca stared at the inn ceiling.

Once.

Twice.

The sound came again.

“Ghhhhrrr—”

...

Wonderful.

He pulled the blanket over his head.

It did not help.

“GHRRRR—”

Luca gave up.

Maybe this was his first real lesson as an adventurer.

Learning how to sleep somewhere uncomfortable.

He sat up.

Then looked at the Guild registration paper on the small table.

Practical test.

This morning.

The sleepiness disappeared quickly.

Right.

Today.

If he passed, he would officially become an adventurer.

If he failed...

Luca stopped.

No.

Do not think about failing before breakfast.

That sounded like a terrible way to start the day.

He got dressed.

Fastened his sword at his waist.

Then looked at himself in the small mirror hanging crookedly on the wall.

Messy hair.

Still-sleepy eyes.

His shoulder felt much better.

Good enough.

“Do I look like an adventurer?”

The reflection did not answer.

Luca sighed.

“At least don’t look like a lost farmer.”

***

Arklune in the morning was not much quieter.

Just noisy in a different way.

Wagons were beginning to move.

Shops opened their doors.

Forges were already ringing with hammers.

Food vendors had started shouting.

Luca bought a meat-filled roll from a stall.

Bit into it.

Stopped.

Hot.

Very hot.

He tried not to show it in front of the vendor.

Failed.

The woman behind the stall laughed.

“First time?”

Luca looked at her.

“Does everyone in this city like asking me that?”

“When your face looks like that, yes.”

Luca chewed slowly.

Fine.

Starting today, he needed a more experienced-looking face.

Whatever that looked like.

***

The Adventurers’ Guild was already busy when Luca arrived.

Not as busy as yesterday.

But busy enough to make him realize adventurers apparently woke much earlier than he had imagined.

Or had not slept at all.

A few of them looked like the second option.

Luca handed over his form at the counter.

The same clerk from yesterday looked at him.

“You came.”

Luca looked back.

“Was I not supposed to?”

“About one in eight doesn’t.”

“Why?”

“Change of heart.”

“Oh.”

“Or drunk.”

...

Fair.

The clerk gave Luca a small wooden plate with a number written on it.

**17.**

“Training yard. Wait until your number is called.”

Luca took the plate.

“There are seventeen people?”

“Twenty-three.”

Luca looked at the number again.

“Then why am I seventeen?”

“Because six people came after you.”

Right.

Stupid question.

“Thank you.”

“Don’t die.”

Luca stopped.

The clerk had already moved on to the next form.

...

That was a joke, right?

Luca decided to believe it was.

***

The Guild training yard was larger than he expected.

Packed dirt.

Several training dummies.

Racks of wooden weapons.

Archery targets.

And a circular area marked with a white boundary line.

Around twenty people were already waiting.

Some looked close to Luca’s age.

Others were older.

Humans.

A Dwarf.

Two Beastfolk.

An Elf stood slightly apart from the Human group.

And an Orc waited on the other side of the yard, leaning on a spear.

No one was being pushed out.

No one was talking either.

The same distance as yesterday.

Luca was beginning to notice it more easily now.

A large man in a leather vest stood in front of them.

A long scar crossed one cheek.

“My name is Hedd.”

His voice was loud enough to silence the yard.

“Guild Examiner.”

He looked over the applicants one by one.

“This test is not a tournament.”

Good.

Luca did not like where this was going if it was.

“We are not looking for the strongest person here.”

Better.

“We are looking for people sensible enough not to die on their first quest.”

...

That sounded less encouraging.

Hedd pointed toward the yard.

“Three parts.”

“Weapon control.”

“Threat response.”

“Judgment.”

One applicant raised a hand.

“Magic?”

“If you have it, we test what’s necessary.”

“What if it’s strong?”

Hedd looked at the young man.

“If you came here only to show me how big your Fireball is, I can show you the door.”

The applicant lowered his hand.

Luca liked Hedd.

A little frightening.

But he liked him.

***

The first test was simple.

At least it looked simple.

Cut.

Block.

Step.

Keep balance.

Strike the training dummy.

Do not fall over.

Luca was handed a wooden sword.

Hedd looked at his stance.

“You’ve trained before.”

“A little.”

Hedd stared.

Luca immediately added,

“Basics.”

Safer.

Hedd nodded.

“Begin.”

Luca attacked.

One slash.

Step.

Pull back.

Slash again.

Not bad.

Not good either.

When he tried the next combination, the tip of the sword dropped too low.

“Stop.”

Luca froze.

Hedd pointed at his hand.

“Too tight.”

Luca loosened his grip.

“Grip too hard and your hand dies before the fight does.”

“Understood.”

“And your lead shoulder is too far forward.”

Luca went quiet.

Bram had said the same thing.

Good.

So the problem really was Luca.

Not Bram being overly critical.

He tried again.

Better.

Hedd gave a small nod.

“Enough.”

Enough.

That word was beginning to appear frequently in Luca’s life.

***

The second test was done in pairs.

Luca stood across from a young long-eared Beastfolk carrying a short staff.

His name was Teren.

“Don’t hit the face,” Teren said.

Luca nodded.

“Agreed.”

The examiner raised a hand.

“Begin.”

Teren moved faster than Luca.

Much faster.

The staff struck the side of Luca’s sword.

Tak!

Luca stepped back.

Teren attacked again.

Luca blocked.

One.

Two.

Three.

Fast.

Too fast.

Luca tried to counter.

Teren was already gone.

...

Oh.

The Beastfolk appeared at Luca’s side.

Luca nearly took a hit to the ribs.

He jumped back.

“Not bad,” Teren said.

“You’re too fast.”

“That wasn’t a compliment.”

“I know.”

Luca was starting to like people who clarified these things.

Teren attacked again.

This time, Luca did not try to match his speed.

He waited.

One step.

Two.

Teren moved in.

Luca shifted aside.

His wooden sword touched Teren’s shoulder.

Both stopped.

Hedd raised a hand.

“Enough.”

Teren rubbed his shoulder.

“Better.”

Luca smiled.

“Thanks.”

“That was a compliment.”

Finally.

***

Magic came next.

Applicants who could use it were separated from the rest.

There were not many.

Less than half.

Luca stood in front of a wooden target.

A Guild mage checked his form.

“Fire and Light?”

“Yes.”

“Fireball first.”

Luca raised a hand.

Draw Aethra.

Shape it.

He remembered the boar.

Too high.

Do not repeat that.

“Fireball!”

Flame shot forward.

It struck the lower part of the target.

Not the center.

But it hit.

Luca held back a smile.

Progress.

The mage inspected the burn mark.

“Low output.”

Yes.

Of course.

“But control is adequate.”

Luca would take it.

“Lesser Heal.”

“No patient?”

The mage held out his arm.

A small scratch had clearly been made there on purpose.

Luca looked at it.

“You’re sure?”

“If I wasn’t, I wouldn’t be offering my arm.”

Right.

Luca placed a hand over it.

Draw Aethra.

Slowly.

Light appeared.

“Lesser Heal.”

The scratch closed little by little.

The mage moved his fingers.

“Hm.”

Luca was beginning to hate that sound.

“Good hm or bad hm?”

“Good.”

“Thank goodness.”

“Fire and Light together is not especially common.”

Luca raised an eyebrow.

“Rare?”

“Not that rare.”

“Oh.”

“Just enough to make someone note it.”

The mage wrote something on the form.

Luca watched.

“What did you write?”

“That you ask too many questions.”

...

Hopefully that was a joke.

***

The final section was called judgment.

At first, Luca imagined a written test.

Maybe questions.

Maybe maps.

It was not.

Three applicants were placed on one side of the yard.

Luca.

Teren.

And a Human boy named Mikel.

On the other side stood a wooden dummy.

A small cart.

Two sacks.

And one Guild staff member lying on the ground.

Luca stared.

“Is he actually injured?”

The staff member lifted his head.

“No.”

“Good.”

Hedd sighed.

“Scenario.”

He pointed.

“A caravan is attacked by monsters.”

“One civilian is injured.”

“Valuable cargo is left behind.”

“A monster escapes carrying one chest.”

“Your quest is to escort the cargo.”

Mikel immediately said,

“Chase the monster.”

Hedd did not react.

“Begin.”

Mikel ran.

Teren hesitated.

Luca looked at the staff member on the ground.

“Can you move?”

“Leg injury.”

“Bad?”

“Can’t walk alone.”

Luca looked at the cart.

Then at the path the “monster” had used.

Cargo.

Quest.

Civilian.

Hmm.

He pointed at Teren.

“Help me get him onto the cart.”

Mikel was already almost at the end of the yard.

Teren helped.

Together, they lifted the staff member onto the cart.

Luca picked up one of the sacks.

“This one?”

“Supplies.”

“The other?”

“Quest cargo.”

Luca put the quest cargo onto the cart.

The supplies were left behind.

Teren looked toward Mikel.

“The chest?”

Luca thought.

“If the monster is gone, we report it.”

“The quest could fail.”

“What if we chase it and there are more monsters?”

Teren paused.

“Right.”

They started pulling the cart.

“Stop.”

Hedd raised a hand.

Mikel came back looking annoyed.

“I caught the monst—”

“There was no monster,” Hedd said.

“But the chest—”

“And you left the civilian, two party members, and all remaining cargo behind.”

Mikel went silent.

Hedd looked at Luca and Teren.

“Why leave the supplies?”

Luca answered,

“Because the civilian and quest cargo matter more. If the cart gets too heavy, supplies can be left.”

“What if the quest says all cargo must be delivered?”

Luca thought.

“If that means leaving the injured person?”

“Yes.”

Luca was quiet for a moment.

Then shook his head.

“I choose the person.”

Hedd looked at him.

No expression.

“Quest fails.”

“Yes.”

“Reward lost.”

“Yes.”

“Reputation drops.”

Luca was beginning to feel there was a trap somewhere.

“...Yes.”

Hedd made a note.

“Good.”

Luca blinked.

“That was correct?”

“There is no perfect answer.”

Oh.

“What we watch is what you prioritize.”

Hedd looked over all the applicants.

“An adventurer who follows words on paper without thinking will die.”

“An adventurer who only chases rewards will get other people killed.”

“And an adventurer who tries to save everything usually gets everyone killed.”

Silence.

Luca remembered Corvin.

A retreating monster was not necessarily a defeated one.

Maybe the Guild was not simply a place where people took jobs.

Maybe they had tests like this because too many people had learned the same lessons in much worse ways.

***

“Number seventeen.”

Luca stood.

The test had ended nearly an hour earlier.

Now he was back in the Guild hall.

The clerk pointed toward the stairs.

“The Guildmaster wants to speak with you.”

Luca looked at her.

“Why?”

“If I knew, I’d be Guildmaster.”

Fair.

Luca climbed the stairs.

He had only been in Arklune for two days and was already losing far too often to simple answers.

There was a small office on the second floor.

The door stood open.

Luca knocked.

“Come in.”

He entered.

The man from the balcony yesterday sat behind the desk.

Hair beginning to gray.

Body still straight.

Expression flat.

Rolan.

Guildmaster of Arklune.

Luca knew the name now from the plaque outside the room.

Rolan read Luca’s form.

For a long time.

Too long.

Luca started to wonder if his Fireball had really been that bad.

Finally, Rolan looked up.

“Luca from Aven.”

“Yes, Guildmaster.”

“Eighteen.”

“Yes.”

“Fire.”

“Yes.”

“Light.”

“Yes.”

Rolan paused.

Luca waited.

“That’s a lot of yes.”

Luca blinked.

“What?”

“Yes.”

“Oh.”

...

Was that a joke?

Rolan looked back at the form.

“Bram still alive?”

Now Luca really went still.

“Uncle Bram?”

Rolan lifted his eyes.

“You listed a former soldier in Aven as one of the people who trained you.”

“Yes.”

“Bram.”

“You know him?”

“Once.”

Luca waited for more.

None came.

Rolan clearly did not intend to explain.

“You passed.”

Luca forgot about Bram.

“I passed?”

“Yes.”

Rolan took a small plate from a drawer.

Not wood.

Metal.

Simple.

The Guild emblem was engraved on one side.

Luca’s name beneath it.

Rolan placed it on the desk.

Luca stared.

“This is...?”

“Guild plate.”

Rolan slid it closer.

“Proof of registration.”

Luca picked it up.

Heavier than expected.

Not much.

But enough.

Name:

**LUCA**

Origin:

**AVEN — ELDORIA**

Rank:

**F**

Luca looked at the letter.

F.

He looked up.

“Guildmaster.”

“Hm?”

“F is...?”

“The lowest.”

“Oh.”

Rolan waited one second.

“Congratulations.”

His expression did not change.

Luca had no idea if that was another joke.

Rolan studied his face.

Then let out a small sigh.

“Garling says I should smile when congratulating people.”

“Who’s Garling?”

“A problem.”

...

All right.

Rolan stood.

“Rank F takes simple quests.”

“Gathering.”

“Small monster control.”

“Delivery.”

“Basic escort.”

“Do not take anything beyond your ability.”

Luca nodded.

“What if I’m not sure?”

“Ask.”

“What if I’m embarrassed?”

“Better embarrassed than dead.”

Reasonable.

Rolan walked toward a shelf behind him.

“The Guild is not a school.”

“We will not watch you every moment.”

“The Guild is not family either.”

“We will not always come to save you.”

He looked back.

“But if you wear that emblem, your actions carry the Guild’s name.”

Luca looked at the plate in his hand.

Rolan continued.

“Don’t be stupid.”

Luca nodded.

“Understood.”

Rolan paused.

“A little stupid is permitted.”

Luca stared.

The Guildmaster remained expressionless.

...

That was definitely a joke.

Probably.

***

Luca returned to the hall.

The plate was still in his hand.

He turned it over.

Once.

Twice.

Adventurer.

Officially.

He did not feel different.

No new strength.

No voice from the heavens.

Nothing had suddenly changed.

His stomach was even starting to growl.

Maybe becoming an adventurer was simply like that.

More ordinary than he had imagined.

And because of that, more real.

The clerk from yesterday looked at him.

“Passed?”

Luca raised the plate.

“Rank F.”

“Congratulations.”

“That sounded more sincere than the Guildmaster.”

“That is a very low standard.”

Luca laughed.

Then looked toward the quest board.

Dozens of papers.

Gather herbs.

Delivery.

Pest control.

Missing livestock.

Small monster sightings.

Escort.

Luca stepped closer.

This was it.

His first quest.

He read them one by one.

One about Slimes in an irrigation channel.

One about delivering medicine to a nearby village.

One about gathering medicinal herbs at the edge of the Monster Forest.

That one made him stop.

**Medicinal Herb Gathering — Outer Monster Forest**

The reward was not large.

Risk: low.

Recommended for new adventurers.

Luca read it again.

Monster Forest.

His father had once spoken about that place.

Not much.

But enough.

Luca reached for the paper.

Then stopped.

Don’t just take it.

Ask first.

Rolan had literally just told him that.

Good.

He carried the notice to the counter.

The clerk read it.

“First quest?”

“Yes.”

“Suitable.”

“Really?”

“If you don’t go too deep.”

Luca looked at the paper.

“How deep is too deep?”

The clerk pointed toward a small map on the wall.

“Outer Forest.”

“Do not pass the red marker stones.”

“What if I do?”

“Turn back.”

“What if I see something interesting?”

“Turn back.”

“What if—”

“Turn back.”

...

The message seemed clear.

Luca handed over his Guild plate.

The clerk recorded the quest.

Then returned it.

“Quest accepted.”

Two words.

Simple.

But Luca looked at the plate a little longer.

Yesterday he had arrived as a village boy from Aven.

Today his name was recorded in the Adventurers’ Guild.

Tomorrow he would enter the Monster Forest for his first quest.

Luca smiled.

Finally.

Then he looked at the quest description again.

**Medicinal Herb Gathering.**

...

Picking grass.

His first job as an adventurer was picking grass.

Luca looked up at the ceiling.

For some reason, he was certain his father would have laughed.

**End of Chapter 3**
