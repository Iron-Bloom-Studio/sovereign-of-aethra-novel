# Chapter 8: Open Road

Two days later, Luca stood before Rolan's desk once again.

This time, there were no bandages visible beneath his clothes.

His ribs still hurt whenever he breathed too deeply, but the pain had changed from something sharp into something merely irritating.

According to the Guild Healer, that was good progress.

According to Luca, good progress should not hurt every time he bent down to put on his boots.

He had learned, however, that a patient's opinion carried very little weight in the infirmary.

Especially when that patient had once tried to catch the Healer's hand during an examination.

Luca placed his Guild plate on the desk.

Four other pieces of metal lay beside it.

One had been given to Pip as proof that he was permitted to accept quests alongside a Guild party. Its surface still looked far too clean beside the old dagger at his waist.

Another rested in front of Zerak.

The Hiveborn stared at it without touching it.

“What is this?” he asked.

“Temporary identification,” Rolan answered. “You carry it while you are under a Guild contract.”

“Does that make me a member of the Guild?”

“Not yet.”

“Am I a prisoner?”

“No.”

“Then what purpose does it serve?”

“If the gate guards see you walking through the city with a spear, they will ask me questions before attempting to arrest you.”

Zerak considered that.

Then he picked up the plate.

“Useful.”

Pip turned his own plate between two fingers.

“Why doesn't mine stop the guards from asking questions?”

“Because they already know you.”

“That sounds like their mistake.”

Rolan ignored him.

Across the desk, Serena was already wearing her combat gear.

A round shield hung from her left arm. Steel covered its face, with several old scratches surrounding the crest of Auregrad. A one-handed sword rested at her right hip.

Beneath her traveling mantle, leather protection and light metal plates covered her chest and shoulders.

She looked more prepared than she had while escorting the caravan.

Heavier, too.

Luca had no intention of saying the last part aloud.

Lysara stood beside her.

Her dark green cloak was fastened neatly at the shoulder. A wooden staff crowned with a red crystal rested against the desk, while scrolls of parchment, bottles of ink, and measuring tools filled the satchel at her waist.

Pip had been watching that satchel for some time.

“Is there food in there?” he asked.

“No.”

“It's too full not to contain food.”

“Most of it is research equipment.”

“Can research equipment be eaten?”

Lysara smiled gently.

“Some of it, but only once.”

Pip stopped watching her bag.

Luca was not sure whether Lysara was joking.

He was beginning to understand that the Elf woman's gentle smile did not always make it easier to tell.

Rolan opened a letter sealed in dark green wax.

Lysara had been carrying it when they met at the ruins, but the Guildmaster had needed time to verify its marks of authority and review its full contents.

“Under Eldren's direction, Sylvaris requests the Guild's assistance in supporting an investigation into an anomaly in the flow of Aethra within the northern ruin complex,” he read.

Pip raised a hand.

Rolan continued.

“The investigation will include documenting the structure, examining its mechanisms, and identifying any relic that may pose a threat to the trade road.”

Pip's hand remained raised.

Rolan lowered the letter.

“What?”

“Will you be reading the part about payment soon?”

“There is no part about payment in this letter.”

Pip's ears lowered slightly.

“Then why are we listening to it?”

“Because the Guild contract has a part about payment.”

His ears rose again.

“Please continue.”

Rolan drew a contract from beneath the letter.

“The Guild and Sylvaris will share the investigation costs. You survived an encounter with a Ruin Golem in the inner hall, followed by a Stone Golem outside the complex.”

Pip raised one finger.

“Survived?”

Rolan looked at him.

“We defeated both of them.”

“Both of them nearly fell on top of you.”

“That happened after we defeated them.”

Rolan continued.

“You also discovered an entrance and have direct experience with the relic. Based on that record, your temporary team will be registered as a Rank E party.”

Luca blinked.

“Rank E?”

“Yes.”

“We haven't completed a single quest as an official party.”

“That is why you are only Rank E.”

Rolan's tone made the achievement sound much smaller than Luca had imagined.

Pip leaned forward.

“Does the pay increase?”

“Slightly.”

“Rank E sounds respectable enough.”

Serena read her copy of the contract.

“Rank E only permits a party to accept low- to moderate-risk quests within mapped territory.”

Pip looked at her.

“You did not need to explain that part.”

“You just called it respectable.”

“That was before your explanation.”

Luca looked at the plate on the desk.

The Guild crest had not changed. A small new mark had been etched along one edge, however, indicating the rank of the party under which he was registered.

Only a few weeks ago, he had entered the city without ever having accepted a quest.

Now he stood beside a soldier of Auregrad, a Goblin, an Elven scholar, and a Hiveborn who could not remember where he had come from.

Rank E might not sound impressive.

The circumstances that had brought him there were considerably harder to explain.

“Who is the party leader?” Rolan asked.

No one answered.

Then, almost at the same time, Serena, Lysara, and Pip looked at Luca.

Zerak followed their gazes a moment later.

Luca looked behind himself.

No one was there.

He turned back to the desk.

“Why are you looking at me?”

“The primary Guild plate is registered under your name,” Serena said.

“Because none of you are Guild members.”

“I have a plate,” Pip said.

“You only just received it.”

“That still offends me.”

Rolan dipped his pen into the ink.

“Luca will serve as the administrative leader.”

“Administrative?”

“You sign the contracts, receive payment, and take responsibility if one of your members causes trouble.”

Luca looked at Pip.

Pip immediately looked elsewhere.

Then Luca looked at Zerak.

The Hiveborn stood silently.

Strangely enough, he looked like the person least likely to cause trouble on purpose.

“I will command the formation during combat,” Serena said.

Luca stared at her.

“So I am only responsible when something goes wrong?”

“More or less.”

“That is a very convenient division of responsibilities.”

“I consider it efficient.”

Luca was beginning to feel annoyed.

More irritating still, he could not find a strong argument against it.

Rolan began to write.

“Party name?”

Silence returned.

This time it lasted longer.

“Does it have to be now?” Luca asked.

“No. You may stand here until tomorrow.”

Pip tapped the desk.

“Give it a short name.”

Serena thought for a moment.

“Northern Ruins Investigation Unit.”

Pip stared at her.

“Is that a party name or the title of a report?”

“It describes our function.”

“If our next quest is catching an escaped chicken, do we change the name?”

“We are not going to accept a quest to catch a chicken.”

Rolan stopped writing.

“You are Rank E.”

Serena fell silent.

Pip gave a small grin.

Lysara raised one hand to her chin.

“We could use a name that reflects why the party was formed.”

“Aethra Anomaly?” Luca suggested.

Pip shook his head.

“That sounds like something people should avoid.”

“Concordant Wayfarers?” Lysara offered.

“Too long.”

“Fivefold Expedition?”

“Longer.”

Lysara looked faintly disappointed.

Luca turned to Zerak.

“Any suggestions?”

“No.”

“You answered too quickly.”

“I have been considering it since he asked.”

“And you found nothing?”

“I found many names I disliked.”

Pip pointed at him.

“That is the best contribution so far.”

Rolan set down his pen.

“The name does not need to carry some grand meaning.”

“Good,” Pip said. “Then we can call ourselves Paid in Advance.”

“No.”

“Fair Share?”

“No.”

“Five Shares?”

“Especially not.”

Pip folded his arms.

“Then don't say it doesn't need a grand meaning.”

Luca looked at the five plates lined up on the desk.

They did not come from the same place.

They were not taking this mission for the same reasons.

Serena was there because of orders and responsibility.

Lysara because of her research.

Zerak because he was searching for something he could not yet name.

Pip would probably say he was there for the payment, though Luca knew that was not the only reason.

Luca himself was not certain.

He only felt that walking with them was the step he ought to take.

Not because of a prophecy.

Not because of duty.

Simply because, for now, their roads led in the same direction.

“Open Road,” he said.

Pip tilted his head.

“That's all?”

“You said it should be short.”

“I didn't say it had to be that simple.”

“You suggested Five Shares.”

“It was clear and easy to remember.”

Lysara repeated the name softly.

“Open Road.”

Serena looked at Luca.

“Why?”

Luca shrugged, then immediately regretted it.

The motion pulled at his ribs.

His expression tightened for an instant.

Pip noticed.

Of course he did.

Luca pretended not to see him.

“Because none of us knows where this journey will lead,” he said. “And as far as possible, the road remains open to anyone willing to walk it with us.”

Something changed slightly in Pip's gaze.

Not much.

But he stopped playing with his plate.

Lysara smiled.

“Not excessive,” Serena said.

“I'll take that as approval.”

“It was approval.”

Luca looked at Zerak.

The Hiveborn studied the five pieces of metal on the desk.

“A name does not determine who we are,” he said.

“No,” Luca replied. “We can decide what it means later.”

Zerak fell silent.

Then he nodded once.

“Open Road.”

Rolan picked up his pen.

“Good.”

He wrote the name at the top of the contract.

**Open Road — Rank E.**

Pip watched the ink dry.

“It is still not too late to choose Five Shares.”

Rolan stamped the contract with the Guild seal.

Now it was too late.

***

The Guild equipment storehouse stood behind the main hall.

Most of the objects inside were not impressive.

Swords marked by repeated sharpening.

Shields with flaking paint.

Rope.

Spikes.

Lanterns.

Several rain cloaks that looked older than Luca.

The quartermaster took a spear from a rack and handed it to Zerak.

The weapon was simple.

Its shaft was made of dark wood, topped by a leaf-shaped iron point. There were no runes, enchantments, or decorations. Thin leather had been wrapped around the middle to improve the grip.

Zerak received it in both hands.

The moment his fingers closed around the shaft, his body changed.

Not physically.

His black carapace remained the same. His red eyes did not glow any brighter.

But the hesitation that usually followed his movements vanished.

He spun the spear once.

Its point passed close to the weapon rack without touching a single object.

His right foot shifted back. Both hands changed position. The spear stopped in a straight line aimed at the empty space before Luca's chest.

Luca froze.

The iron point was less than an arm's length away.

Zerak immediately lowered it.

“Sorry.”

The quartermaster stared at him.

“You said your memory was unclear.”

“It is.”

“That did not look unclear.”

Zerak looked down at the spear.

“It appears my body holds a different opinion.”

Luca slowly moved out of the weapon's path.

“If your body decides to remember anything else, perhaps do it somewhere other than in front of me.”

Zerak nodded.

“Reasonable.”

On the other side of the storehouse, Pip was examining a small crossbow.

It was short enough to hide beneath his cloak. Its limbs were made of thin steel, while the stock had been repaired with two different kinds of wood.

Pip drew the string using a small hook.

The sound was louder than he had expected.

The quartermaster held out a pouch.

“Eight bolts.”

Pip checked inside.

“Only eight?”

“You paid for eight.”

“I may need more.”

“Pay for more.”

Pip fastened the pouch to his belt.

Then he pointed at a small cloth-wrapped sphere on the desk.

“And that.”

“A smoke bomb.”

“I know.”

“Have you ever used one?”

Pip was silent for a moment.

“I have seen one used.”

The quartermaster immediately reached to take it back.

Pip caught his wrist.

“I will buy it.”

“You may buy one.”

“Why?”

“Because I want to make sure the city is still standing when you return to buy the second.”

Pip snatched the bomb before the man could reconsider.

At the end of the desk sat a small bottle of resin and several strips of cloth.

Pip took one bolt, wrapped the cloth just behind its iron head, and tied it tightly.

Luca watched him.

“What is that?”

“A bolt that can burn.”

“Did you bring fire?”

Pip looked at him.

Then at Luca's hand.

Luca followed his gaze.

“Oh.”

“Do not burn the string.”

“I know.”

“I am only making sure.”

“You have never even used that crossbow.”

“That is why I am making sure.”

Serena stood near the door with her shield strapped to her back.

“A smoke bomb inside the ruins could reduce our own visibility.”

“If used badly,” Pip replied.

“That is what concerns me.”

“I know how to use tools.”

Luca looked at the dagger that still hung at Pip's waist.

“You are keeping that?”

Pip stared at him as though the question were insulting.

“A crossbow needs to be reloaded.”

“And a dagger doesn't?”

“A dagger has never asked to be reloaded.”

That was difficult to dispute.

***

They left the city as the sun rose above the eastern wall.

Serena walked at the front with her shield on her back.

Luca followed two steps behind. Lysara walked in the middle, while Pip repeatedly disappeared along the sides of the road to inspect tracks.

Zerak took the rear with the spear resting on his shoulder.

The formation lasted for less than ten minutes.

“Pip is too far ahead,” Serena said.

A voice answered from behind the bushes.

“I can still hear you.”

“That is not a measure of safe distance.”

Pip emerged beside the road.

“If I walk with all of you, I am not scouting.”

“You still need to remain within reach of assistance.”

“If I need assistance, then my scouting has already failed.”

“If you cannot return to warn us, then your scouting has also failed.”

Pip opened his mouth.

Then closed it again.

Luca stepped between them.

“Let's try this. Pip moves ahead, but no farther than shouting distance. If you find something, come back. Do not face it alone.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

“I just don't like it when she says it like an order.”

Serena stared at him.

“It was an order.”

Pip exhaled and resumed walking.

A short while later, Lysara spoke.

“Technically, Luca is the party leader.”

Serena looked at her.

“Administratively.”

“Rolan did not establish a clear boundary between administrative and field leadership.”

Luca glanced at Lysara.

The Elf woman smiled as if she had merely offered an innocent observation.

Pip laughed quietly from ahead.

Serena turned back toward the road.

“If the situation becomes a battle, I control the formation.”

“That makes sense,” Luca said.

“If we find an ancient mechanism, I lead the examination,” Lysara added.

“That also makes sense.”

“If there is a trap, I lead,” Pip said.

Luca nodded.

Every gaze then shifted toward Zerak.

The Hiveborn considered the question.

“If something tries to kill us, I will stand between it and all of you.”

Lysara glanced at him.

Only for a moment.

But Luca noticed.

“Then what is my job?” Luca asked.

“To take responsibility when everything fails,” Pip answered.

Luca was beginning to understand why Rolan had made him the leader.

No one else wanted the position.

***

The route to the ruins had been marked with Guild rope.

Two warning signs stood around the hill, while the exit they had discovered earlier had been reinforced with wooden beams.

A Guild guard inspected their contract before removing the barrier.

He read the party name.

“Open Road?”

Luca nodded.

The guard looked at the dark opening between the rocks.

“The name does not seem very appropriate for a place like this.”

Pip entered first.

“The road is still open.”

“Unfortunately,” Serena muttered.

They lit two lanterns.

Luca carried one. Lysara carried the other, though the crystal on her staff also gave off a soft red glow.

The passage descended toward the altar hall.

The marks of their previous battle remained.

The section of floor that had melted was now an uneven spread of black stone. The body of the Ruin Golem lay on one side of the chamber, partially dismantled by a Guild team to ensure it would not move again.

At the center of the hall, the pale metal fragment still rested on the altar.

It was no longer floating.

It lay motionless on the stone, surrounded by a chalk line and three paper seals bearing the Guild crest.

Luca stopped several steps away.

The mark on the back of his hand grew warm.

It did not hurt.

It was merely enough to remind him that something had passed from that relic into him.

Lysara approached the altar.

She removed a small instrument from her satchel.

It resembled a copper frame with a crystal needle suspended from a thin thread. When she placed it near the altar, the needle began to turn.

Slowly at first.

Then faster.

“The flow of Aethra is still active,” she said.

Serena studied the metal fragment.

“It is no longer glowing.”

“Not every flow of Aethra produces visible light.”

Lysara moved the instrument toward the right side of the altar.

The needle slowed.

She moved it to the left.

It began spinning again.

“The flow does not end at the relic.”

“Where does it go?” Luca asked.

Lysara walked around the altar.

The crystal needle moved whenever she neared the back.

“Down.”

Pip was already crouching.

His hand brushed dust away from the altar's base.

“There is a seam.”

Serena stepped closer.

“Do not touch anything.”

“I am looking.”

“With your hands.”

“My eyes cannot move dust.”

Pip blew across the floor.

Dust flew into Luca's face.

He coughed and stepped back.

“A little warning.”

“I assumed you could see me take a breath.”

Pip cleared a thin semicircular line behind the altar. He slipped the point of his dagger into the seam but did not apply pressure.

“This is a door.”

Lysara knelt beside him.

“There is no locking sigil.”

“Because the lock is not here.”

Pip pointed to two small openings in the side of the altar's base.

“They need to be pressed at the same time.”

Serena studied the ceiling.

“There is a chance that will activate the defenses.”

“There is always a chance something will activate the defenses.”

“That does not reassure me.”

“It is not my job to reassure you. My job is to find the door.”

Luca looked at the metal fragment on the altar.

“We were sent to find the source of the anomaly.”

“Correct,” Lysara said.

“The flow leads downward?”

“Correct.”

“Then we need to look below.”

Serena looked at Luca's ribs.

“I can still walk.”

“I did not say anything.”

“Your expression did.”

Pip turned toward them.

“You are doing that now too?”

Serena did not answer.

After examining the surrounding floor, Pip used two short spikes to press both locks at once.

Stone shifted beneath the altar.

Everyone stepped back.

No blades emerged.

No golems awakened.

Part of the floor slowly sank, revealing a narrow staircase descending into darkness.

Pip removed the spikes.

“See?”

Serena looked into the opening.

“We still do not know what is below.”

“True. But so far, nothing has tried to kill us.”

A long scraping sound rose from the bottom of the stairs.

Something moving across stone.

Pip drew his dagger.

“So far.”

***

The stairs ended in a low corridor.

Zerak had to lower his head slightly to keep the ridges of his carapace from striking the ceiling.

The walls differed from those above.

There were no reliefs or pillars. Only long stone blocks carved with narrow channels along their surfaces.

Lysara raised her lantern.

“These are distribution channels.”

“Distributing what?” Luca asked.

“Aethra. Energy from the relic was likely carried to other parts of the structure.”

“To power the golems?”

“Possibly.”

Pip studied the floor.

“Claw marks.”

Serena took the shield from her back and fitted it over her left arm.

“Another golem?”

“No.”

Pip touched one of the scratches.

“Four legs. Low. Heavy.”

Lysara studied the wall.

“The marks are recent.”

Pip nodded.

“No more than a few days old.”

Luca lowered his lantern.

A shard of hard gray plating lay near the base of the wall.

Pip lifted it with the tip of his dagger.

“Armored Lizard.”

Luca studied the shard, which was nearly as broad as his palm.

“Do ordinary lizards have skin like that?”

“If its skin were ordinary, it would only be called a Lizard.”

Lysara knelt near the scratches.

“Armored Lizards absorb minerals from the soil and from their food. Their plates harden as they age.”

Pip pointed at the deep claw marks.

“And the one that left these is old enough.”

“How large?” Luca asked.

Pip put the fragment back on the floor.

“Larger than you would want to meet in a narrow corridor.”

“That is not a measurement.”

“Large enough for the measurement not to matter.”

Serena drew her sword.

“Formation.”

This time, no one argued.

Serena stood at the front with her shield raised.

Zerak took position slightly behind her right side, his spear angled past her shoulder.

Luca and Lysara stood in the center.

Pip moved near the wall with his crossbow loaded.

They continued slowly.

The corridor turned twice.

The damp smell grew stronger.

Then Pip raised a fist.

Everyone stopped.

There was breathing ahead.

Not from any of them.

Luca lifted the lantern higher.

A pair of pale eyes opened at the far end of the passage.

Then another pair.

And another.

The first Armored Lizard moved into the light.

Its body was low and broad. Stone-like plates covered its back, head, and the outer sides of its legs. Only its throat, belly, and inner joints appeared unprotected.

The creature opened its mouth.

Rows of small teeth filled its jaws.

Pip raised the crossbow.

“Do not hit the back.”

“Where?” Luca asked.

“Eyes. Throat. Inner legs.”

The Armored Lizard lowered its body.

Pip fired.

The bolt struck the side of its head.

It sounded like metal striking stone.

The bolt bounced away and fell to the floor.

Pip stared at it.

“The armor is thicker than I expected.”

The creature charged.

Serena stepped forward.

Her shield took the impact of the Armored Lizard's head.

The crash of metal filled the corridor.

Serena's boots slid across the stone.

She drove down her heel.

Earth magic flowed into the floor.

Two ridges of stone rose behind her feet, locking her stance before she could be pushed farther.

“Behind me!”

Her sword moved around the edge of her shield.

The blade cut into the inside of the creature's foreleg.

Dark blood appeared.

The Armored Lizard growled and slammed its weight against her again.

Serena did not move.

The second Armored Lizard climbed the wall.

Lysara raised her staff.

“Gust!”

Wind struck the creature and hurled it back to the floor.

The third moved low along the left side, trying to slip past Serena.

Luca stepped forward to intercept it.

His sword struck the plate on its shoulder.

The blade rebounded.

The impact traveled all the way to his elbow.

The Armored Lizard's tail swept around and struck Luca's legs.

He lost his balance.

Pain stabbed through his ribs when his back hit the wall.

His expression tightened.

Pip saw it while loading another bolt.

“Feeling much better?”

“Not the time.”

“I was only checking.”

The Armored Lizard opened its jaws in front of Luca.

A spear passed above his shoulder.

Its point entered the soft flesh beneath the creature's jaw.

Zerak twisted the shaft and dragged its head away.

Luca regained his feet and drove his sword into the same wound.

The Armored Lizard writhed.

Darkness gathered in Zerak's palm.

A Dark Bolt struck its foreleg.

There was no explosion.

But the color of the plates around the joint faded. Its movement slowed as if its strength had been pulled away.

Luca struck again.

The creature collapsed.

“Two more!” Serena shouted.

The Armored Lizard Serena held back pushed its head beneath her shield.

Its jaws nearly reached her leg.

Zerak moved to the right.

His spear struck the seam between the plates over its shoulder and chest.

It did not penetrate.

A second Dark Bolt formed in his left hand and hit the same point.

A thin darkness spread along the seam.

The Armored Lizard weakened for an instant.

Serena drove forward with her shield.

The creature stumbled back half a step.

“Left side of its chest,” Zerak said. “The joint is weakening.”

Lysara raised her staff.

A Wind Blade struck the point.

The attack left a mark but failed to open the plate.

“Not enough,” she said.

Serena absorbed another impact.

The stone braces behind her feet cracked.

The last Armored Lizard began circling them along the wall.

Pip fired.

This time, the bolt struck the inside of its leg.

The creature dropped from the wall, but immediately rose again.

“We need something that can break through those plates!” Luca shouted.

Serena angled her shield to deflect a bite.

Her sword stabbed toward the Armored Lizard's eye, but the creature moved its head and the blade only scored the side of its face.

One of the stone braces behind Serena broke.

She stamped down again.

Earth magic raised a new length of stone from the floor.

It grew at an angle, supporting the back of her shield.

Lysara saw it.

“Do not release your magic!”

Serena held the creature in front of her.

“Why?”

“Make the brace longer.”

“That is not a weapon.”

Wind began to circle Lysara's staff.

“Not yet.”

Serena understood.

She shifted her sword behind her shield, then sent more mana into the stone brace.

The stone lengthened.

Its shape narrowed into a point like a short spear that remained joined to the floor.

Lysara aimed the crystal of her staff.

Gust wrapped around the stone.

Dust rose.

The Stone Javelin began to tremble.

Thin cracks appeared across its surface.

“Too strong!” Serena said.

“If I reduce it now, it will not be fast enough!”

The Armored Lizard struck Serena's shield.

The stone beneath her feet cracked.

Zerak stepped forward and braced the side of the shield with his shoulder.

The Armored Lizard's claws raked the carapace on his arm.

He did not retreat.

“I will hold it,” he said. “Finish.”

Lysara maintained the Gust.

The Stone Javelin spun faster.

Its cracks spread.

Heat rose across the back of Luca's right hand.

The faint mark did not glow.

But the currents around him became clearer.

Serena's Earth magic held the shape of the stone.

Lysara's Wind tried to turn it.

The two were not yet moving in the same rhythm.

“Lysara, ease the left side!”

“I cannot see the pattern of her Earth magic!”

“I can!”

Serena glanced toward him.

“Luca?”

He did not know how to explain it.

There was no time.

“Trust me!”

Lysara shifted her staff.

The flow of wind weakened on the left.

The Stone Javelin's vibration eased.

Earth and Wind met.

Not perfectly.

Only for the length of one breath.

But it was enough.

“Now!”

Serena released the shape of the stone.

Lysara thrust her staff forward.

The Stone Javelin tore free from the floor.

Wind spun it so quickly that it became a gray streak.

The projectile shot past the side of Serena's shield.

It struck the part of the Armored Lizard's chest that Zerak's Dark Bolt had weakened.

A violent crack filled the corridor.

The Stone Javelin did not pass through the creature's entire body.

But its point drove between two plates and shattered their joint.

The Armored Lizard roared.

Its body was forced backward.

Serena lowered her shield.

Her breathing was heavy.

Lysara also staggered half a step.

The crystal on her staff continued to tremble.

“A Dual Art,” she said softly.

Pip looked at the hole in the Armored Lizard's plating.

“Stone Drill.”

Serena turned.

“It was a Stone Javelin.”

“The stone spun and made a hole.”

“Spiral Stone Javelin,” Lysara said, still catching her breath. “Technically, that is more accurate.”

“Stone Drill is shorter.”

Luca raised his sword.

“Discuss the name later!”

The Armored Lizard tried to attack again.

Zerak moved.

His spear entered the crack made by the Stone Javelin.

He twisted the shaft and used the point as a lever.

The plate over the creature's chest opened slightly.

The Armored Lizard thrashed.

Its claws struck Zerak's carapace.

A long scratch appeared along his side.

Zerak kept the spear in place.

“The breach is open!”

Pip dropped his empty crossbow and drew the resin-wrapped bolt.

He loaded it as quickly as he could.

“Luca!”

Luca moved back toward him.

“Light the tip.”

Luca looked at the crossbow string only a few fingers away from the resin wrapping.

“Are you sure?”

“The tip. Not the string.”

“I know.”

“I am only making sure.”

Luca raised one hand.

He formed the smallest Fireball he could.

The flame that was usually the size of his fist was now no larger than a walnut. Its shape was unstable and trembled several times.

Pip immediately pulled the crossbow a little farther away.

Luca stared at him.

“You said you were sure.”

“I am sure about the idea. I am still judging the execution.”

Luca brought the Fireball close to the bolt.

The resin ignited.

Flame spread behind the iron head.

Pip raised the crossbow.

But Zerak still stood directly in front of the target.

“Zerak!” he shouted.

The Hiveborn looked over his shoulder.

The Armored Lizard struggled to close its chest plates.

Zerak held the spear with all his strength.

“Fire.”

“You are still in the way.”

“I will move.”

Pip did not lower the crossbow.

“When?”

“When you fire.”

Pip growled quietly.

“This insect-plated man is more irritating than Luca.”

“I heard that,” Luca said.

“Good.”

Pip pulled the trigger.

At the same moment, Zerak dropped to one side.

The Flame Bolt passed a few fingers above his shoulder.

It entered through the gap in the armor.

Fire burst from the crack in the Armored Lizard's chest.

The creature shrieked.

Its body struck the wall, then collapsed.

Zerak released the spear and rolled away before the massive body could fall on him.

The last Armored Lizard stopped.

It looked at the two bodies on the floor.

Then lowered its stance.

Pip took out the smoke bomb.

Serena saw it.

“Pip.”

“I know.”

“You have never used one.”

“I have seen one used.”

“That is not the same.”

Pip lit the fuse with the fire still burning on the bolt's cloth wrapping.

A spark appeared.

Then went out.

Pip stared at the bomb.

“The quartermaster sold me defective goods.”

The Armored Lizard began to move.

“Pip,” Luca said.

“I am fixing it.”

“Pip.”

“One moment.”

Lysara aimed her staff.

A drop of water left the bottle at her waist and dampened the outer cloth without touching the fuse.

“What are you doing?” Pip asked.

“Preventing it from exploding in your hand.”

“That is the purpose of the thing.”

“Not in your hand.”

Using the tip of his dagger, Pip pulled the fuse slightly farther out and lit it again.

This time, the spark traveled inward.

White smoke poured out.

Pip threw the bomb in front of the Armored Lizard.

The creature retreated with a hiss.

Lysara raised her staff.

Gust drove the smoke along the corridor.

The Armored Lizard turned and disappeared into the darkness.

No one pursued it.

For several moments, only their breathing could be heard.

Serena lowered her shield.

The stone brace behind her feet crumbled into gravel.

Zerak stood and examined the scratch on his carapace.

Pip retrieved the crossbow he had dropped.

Lysara stared at the scattered fragments of the Stone Javelin.

“That truly was a Dual Art,” she said.

“Didn't we already perform one?” Luca asked.

“The Dual Art between Fire and Earth in the upper hall happened by accident and was influenced by the relic. This one was formed deliberately.”

“With a considerable amount of assistance,” Serena said.

Her gaze shifted to the mark on Luca's hand.

Luca closed his fist.

“I only knew when the currents met.”

“That is not something one can normally know by looking,” Lysara said.

“I do not know how to explain it.”

“I did not say you needed to explain it now.”

Lysara took out a sheet of parchment.

“But I will have many questions later.”

Luca looked at the number of scrolls in her satchel.

Strangely, facing an Armored Lizard seemed simpler.

Pip held up a bolt he had recovered from the floor.

“Flame Bolt.”

Lysara stopped writing.

“What?”

“Mine and Luca's attack. It is called Flame Bolt.”

“That was not a Dual Art.”

“Why?”

“Because you did not combine two magical patterns. You used a tool enhanced with Fire magic.”

“Does it pay differently?”

“No.”

“Then I do not object.”

Serena looked at the hole in the Armored Lizard's chest plate.

“And the other technique is still Spiral Stone Javelin.”

“Stone Drill,” Pip said.

Zerak picked up his spear.

“Stone Drill is shorter.”

Serena stared at him.

Zerak paused.

“But Spiral Stone Javelin is more accurate.”

Luca held back a smile.

Zerak was learning quickly.

***

The corridor ended in a circular room.

There were no guardians.

No monsters.

Several short pillars surrounded a stone pedestal in the center. The channels carved into the walls converged beneath it like streams flowing toward a single point.

Lysara stopped at the threshold.

“Do not enter yet.”

Pip, who had already raised one foot, lowered it again.

“I have not touched anything this time.”

“I appreciate that.”

Lysara placed the Aethra-measuring instrument on the floor.

The crystal needle spun rapidly.

Then stopped without warning.

It pointed directly at the pedestal in the center of the room.

Lysara moved the instrument slightly.

The needle continued to point at the same place.

“The source of the flow is there.”

“Another relic?” Serena asked.

“There should be.”

“Should be?”

Lysara pointed to the top of the pedestal.

A curved recess had been carved into its surface.

It was empty.

Its shape resembled a place intended to hold a piece of metal like the one on the altar above—but much larger.

Luca stepped closer.

The mark on his hand grew warm again.

This time, the warmth carried a strange sense of absence.

Like standing before a door that should have opened, only to find nothing beyond it.

“It is gone,” he said.

Serena watched the room from behind her shield.

“Perhaps it was removed when this place was abandoned.”

“No,” Pip said.

He crouched outside the circle of pillars.

“There is dust across the entire floor.”

“There is,” Serena replied.

“Not here.”

Pip pointed to a thin line cutting through the dust.

Something had been dragged.

The mark led from the pedestal toward one side of the room.

They followed it.

A narrow opening had been cut into the wall behind the last pillar. It was just wide enough for one person to pass through sideways.

The stone around it had been cut.

Not cracked by age.

Cut.

Lysara touched the surface without passing through.

“This is recent.”

“How recent?” Luca asked.

“Difficult to determine.”

Pip brushed the ground at the base of the opening.

Then he lifted something.

A strand of black fiber.

Not a root.

Not animal hair.

A fiber from rope or cloth.

“A few days,” he said. “Possibly less.”

Serena tightened her grip on her sword.

“Someone entered this room before the Guild sealed the ruins.”

“Or was still inside when the entrance was sealed,” Luca said.

Silence filled the chamber.

They all looked into the dark opening.

Zerak moved to the front.

His spear pointed into the gap.

“I hear nothing.”

Pip approached and examined the ground again.

“There is one trail.”

“Entering or leaving?” Lysara asked.

Pip raised the lantern.

“Leaving.”

Serena looked at the empty pedestal.

“Someone took the relic.”

“And likely reactivated this place's mechanisms while doing so,” Lysara said.

She opened a sheet of parchment and began sketching the pillars and channels of Aethra.

Her hand moved quickly.

“The fragment on the upper altar may have been left behind when the main object was removed. Its response to Luca then awakened the parts of the system that remained active.”

“Are you certain?” Luca asked.

“No.”

Pip looked at her.

“You write very quickly for someone who is uncertain.”

“I am recording possibilities.”

“How many possibilities do you normally record?”

“All that are reasonable.”

“And the unreasonable ones?”

“Several, as a precaution.”

Pip stared at the parchment already filling with notes.

“I am beginning to understand why your bag has no room for food.”

Lysara smiled without stopping her writing.

Serena walked around the pedestal.

“Whatever they took was fairly large.”

“But not too heavy,” Pip said. “Only one person dragged it. The trail changes after it reaches the opening.”

“They carried it?”

“Possibly placed it in a bag or wrapped it.”

Luca stood before the empty recess.

He raised his right hand.

The lines on his skin appeared slightly clearer beneath the red light from Lysara's staff.

The curve within the mark resembled part of the recess's edge.

Not exactly.

But close enough.

Lysara noticed.

She stopped writing.

“Luca.”

“I know.”

“May I see your hand?”

Luca held it out.

Lysara moved closer but did not touch him.

Her gaze shifted from the mark on Luca's skin to the recess atop the pedestal.

“They come from the same pattern.”

Serena stood beside them.

“What does that mean?”

Lysara exhaled slowly.

“I do not know yet.”

Strangely, the answer brought Luca a little relief.

At least he was not the only one who did not understand what was happening.

Zerak remained before the opening.

“There is a passage beyond it.”

Pip approached.

“Narrow.”

“Where does it lead?”

“Upward,” Zerak answered. “Likely to the other side of the hill.”

Serena looked at the party.

“We found the source of the anomaly. We record the passage and report to the Guild. We do not pursue an unknown person through an unmapped tunnel.”

Pip looked ready to argue.

Then he looked at the smoke bomb he no longer had and the depleted bolt pouch at his waist.

“For once, I agree.”

Serena raised an eyebrow.

“For once?”

“I agree often. Usually not aloud.”

Luca looked into the opening once more.

Part of him wanted to follow the passage.

To discover who had taken the relic.

To understand how it was connected to the mark on his hand.

But his ribs still hurt. The mana he had used was not fully restored. Serena and Lysara had only just formed their first deliberate Dual Art. None of them knew what waited beyond that narrow passage.

Perhaps being a leader did not only mean walking first.

Sometimes it meant knowing when to stop.

“We go back,” he said.

Serena nodded.

No one argued.

They marked the opening, mapped the room, and took the black fiber as evidence.

Lysara spent longer than the others documenting the empty pedestal.

But when Luca called her, she closed her parchment and followed.

***

Rolan read their report to the end.

Then he read it again.

The five members of Open Road stood around the same desk as they had that morning.

Only now their clothes were covered in dust.

Serena's shield bore a fresh dent.

Zerak carried a spear with new scratches along its shaft.

Pip had one fewer smoke bomb and one more reason to complain about the price of bolts.

Lysara carried seven additional pages of notes.

Luca stood slightly crooked despite his attempt to hide it.

Rolan placed the report on the desk.

“So you found another relic.”

“We found where another relic should have been,” Lysara said.

“In other words, you did not find it.”

“We found that it was missing,” Pip said.

Rolan looked at him.

“That is still a discovery.”

“Technically,” Lysara added, “he is correct.”

Rolan closed his eyes for a moment.

Perhaps he was suppressing his exhaustion.

Perhaps he regretted promoting them to Rank E.

“And someone made another exit from inside the ruins.”

Serena placed the black fiber on the desk.

“Recently used. We did not pursue them because we did not know the condition of the passage.”

“That was the correct decision.”

Serena glanced at Luca.

Luca noticed.

“I also agreed to return.”

“I did not say anything.”

“Your expression.”

Pip sat on the corner of the desk.

“Everyone is doing that now.”

Rolan placed the fiber into an evidence pouch.

“The entrance remains sealed. Tomorrow I will send people to watch the far side of the hill and locate the other end of that passage.”

Lysara held her rolled report.

“I need to continue the investigation.”

“That is not surprising.”

“The pattern on the pedestal and the mark on Luca's hand come from the same structure. In addition, someone removed a relic before we could understand its function.”

“How long will you need?”

“I do not know.”

“Estimate.”

Lysara looked at the seven pages in her hands.

“More than a few days.”

Pip sighed.

Rolan turned his attention to Serena.

“And you?”

“My captain's order remains in effect until the threat to the trade road is declared resolved.”

“Does a missing relic count as a threat?”

“It may have activated the Ruin Golem and drawn Armored Lizards up from below.”

“That sounds like a threat.”

“That is why I am remaining here.”

Serena's tone was still formal.

But Luca noticed that she had not read from an order when she said it.

Rolan looked at Zerak.

“Your temporary permit expires tomorrow.”

Zerak remained silent.

“The Guild has found no report of other Hiveborn in this region,” Rolan continued. “You may leave, or extend your permit by remaining under Open Road's contract.”

No one answered for him.

Luca did not ask.

Neither did Lysara.

The choice was placed in Zerak's hands again.

The Hiveborn looked at the spear on loan from the Guild.

Then at the temporary plate on the desk.

“I will remain with Open Road.”

“Because of the ruins?” Rolan asked.

“In part.”

“What is the other part?”

Zerak looked at Luca, Serena, Lysara, and Pip.

“I am still considering it.”

Pip nodded as though that answer were enough.

Then Rolan looked at him.

“I do not need to ask you?”

“This quest pays.”

“Is that all?”

Pip looked down at his new plate.

One finger touched the Guild crest on its surface.

“For now.”

Luca did not point out that the answer sounded less convincing than usual.

Rolan took the Open Road contract and added one line at the bottom.

**Status: Active.**

He pushed the contract toward Luca.

“Rank E party. Five standing members for the duration of the investigation.”

Luca read the five names written there.

His own.

Serena.

Lysara.

Zerak.

Pip.

That morning, **Open Road** had merely been a name required to complete a form.

Now it felt slightly different.

Not yet like a promise.

Not yet like a family.

Perhaps not even entirely like a party.

But when Luca raised his head, all five of them were still there.

Serena was inspecting the new dent in her shield.

Lysara was arranging her notes according to an order only she understood.

Zerak stood near the wall with his spear in hand.

Pip counted his remaining bolts, then frowned.

“Eight bolts,” he muttered. “I brought eight.”

“You fired three,” Luca said.

“I only recovered two.”

“One is still inside an Armored Lizard.”

“I know.”

“The Armored Lizard is still in the ruins.”

Pip put away the pouch.

“We will go back for it tomorrow.”

Serena looked at him.

“No.”

“Bolts are not cheap.”

“No.”

Pip looked at Luca.

Luca looked down at the contract in his hands.

Being the administrative leader was already beginning to feel heavy.

“We will discuss it tomorrow.”

“That is not an answer,” Serena said.

“It is an answer that postpones the argument.”

Zerak gave a small nod.

“A good decision.”

Lysara hid her smile behind a roll of parchment.

On the desk, the ink beneath the name Open Road finally dried.

Somewhere else, someone carried a relic that should still have been inside the ruins.

They did not yet know who.

They did not know where.

But for the first time, Luca did not have to search for the answer alone.

The road before them was open.

That did not mean it was empty.

**End of Chapter 8**
