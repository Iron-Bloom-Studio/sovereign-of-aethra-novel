# Chapter 4: A Friend

Luca set out before the sun had fully risen.

He carried his sword, a waterskin, a small map from the Guild, and an empty basket.

The basket looked too large for a herb-gathering quest.

Or perhaps it only looked that way because there was nothing in it yet.

Luca glanced at it.

“Don't worry. We'll find some grass.”

The basket did not answer.

Good.

The road to the Outer Monster Forest passed through farmland south of Arklune. Several farmers were already at work. Some checked the fences. Others pushed carts or carried sacks of harvested crops.

At a crossroads, Luca noticed a notice nailed to a wooden board.

**Help needed — drive a band of Goblins from the fields.**

The reward listed below was larger than the one for his herb-gathering quest.

Luca stopped for a moment.

“Pick grass or drive off Goblins.”

He looked at his basket.

“For my first day, grass.”

He continued down the road.

Before long, a red marker stone appeared beside it. Luca checked its position against the Guild map.

The Outer Forest began there.

He remembered what the clerk had told him.

*Don't pass the red marker stones.*

And if he found something interesting?

*Turn back.*

Luca put away the map and began looking for medicinal herbs along the forest's outer edge.

***

The first plant he found had long leaves with a pale stripe down the middle.

Luca compared it with the drawing on his quest sheet.

Close enough.

He picked it carefully and placed it in his basket.

“One.”

He needed quite a few more.

He was searching for the next plant when a commotion rose from the fields.

Shouting.

Wood breaking.

Then someone shouted louder.

“Goblins!”

Luca turned.

Several small figures ran between rows of crops. Farmers chased them with sticks and pitchforks.

“Drive them out!”

Luca reached for his sword.

Then he noticed something strange.

One of the Goblins being chased was pulling an older Goblin away from an overturned cart. He was not carrying any crops. He was not even running toward the farmhouse.

He was trying to get someone injured to safety.

A stone flew past and struck the ground near them.

The smaller Goblin flinched.

Luca remembered the two Goblins at the roadside post.

They had only wanted to pay for a room.

People had tried to turn them away anyway.

Luca left his basket beside the road and ran toward the field.

***

“Don't come any closer!” a farmer shouted when he saw Luca.

“What happened?” Luca asked.

“Goblins attacked the fields!”

The farmer pointed toward the overturned cart.

“They've been causing trouble since morning.”

“Which ones?”

The farmer stared at him.

“Which ones?”

Luca was about to point out the smaller Goblin when a roar came from the other side of the field.

A large figure burst through the crops.

He stood much taller than the Goblins being chased. His shoulders were broad, and he gripped a crude axe.

A Hobgoblin.

Behind him, several armed Goblins dragged sacks of harvested grain.

“Hurry!” the Hobgoblin barked. “Take the food!”

One of the farmers advanced with a pitchfork.

The Hobgoblin's axe swept toward him.

Luca pulled the farmer back. The axe blade struck the fence and split the wood.

They both fell to the ground.

The farmer looked from the armed band to the smaller Goblin still helping his companion.

“All Goblins are the same,” he said.

Luca got to his feet and drew his sword.

“Those ones are stealing the harvest.”

He pointed toward the Hobgoblin's band.

“The one by the cart is helping someone.”

“How do you know they aren't working together?”

“I don't,” Luca said. “But I can see what they're doing.”

***

The smaller Goblin had taken cover behind the overturned cart.

The old Goblin beside him held his leg. Several others hid behind the broken fence.

As Luca approached, the smaller Goblin raised a dagger.

“You're Human.”

“Yes.”

“Humans threw stones at us.”

“I saw.”

Luca kept his distance.

“I want to help the injured one. Can he stand?”

The old Goblin tried to move his leg, then shook his head.

“My name is Pip,” said the smaller Goblin. His dagger remained raised. “He's my grandfather.”

“I'm Luca.”

Luca pointed toward the fence behind them.

“Take him there. The others can help.”

Pip watched him suspiciously.

“Why?”

“Because that axe doesn't care who is standing in front of it.”

Pip glanced toward the Hobgoblin.

“He isn't from our tribe.”

“Why is he attacking the farm?”

“They're starving. They've been searching for food for days.”

That explained the sacks of grain.

It did not make the axe in the Hobgoblin's hand any less dangerous.

“Get your grandfather out of here,” Luca said.

“And you?”

“I'll make him chase me.”

Pip stared at him.

“That's a bad plan.”

“If you have a better one, tell me while you're moving.”

Pip did not answer. He tucked the dagger into his belt and helped the other Goblins lift his grandfather.

***

Luca cut the rope holding an empty cart in place and pushed it across the path between the fields.

The cart overturned.

Two of the attacking Goblins stumbled and dropped the sacks they carried.

The Hobgoblin turned.

“Human!”

“Yes,” Luca answered. “I heard you.”

The axe swung at him.

Luca stepped back. Its blade struck the fence where his body had been a moment earlier.

Too close.

He kept his distance and moved sideways.

Bram had told him that he left his lead shoulder exposed too often.

Hedd had said the same thing.

Luca tried to remember that before the next swing came.

The Hobgoblin attacked again.

Luca raised his hand.

“Fireball!”

A small ball of fire struck the ground at the Hobgoblin's feet. It was not strong enough to hurt him, but it stopped him for a moment.

Luca retreated toward an empty part of the field.

“Why are you leading him there?” a farmer shouted.

“Because there's no one over here!”

“That means you're the only one over there!”

“Yes, I'm beginning to realize that!”

The Hobgoblin gave chase.

Luca ran past the end of the broken fence. Heavy breathing grew closer behind him.

He had to turn around.

He did not want to turn around.

Before he could decide, Pip moved from behind the cart.

Fast.

Low.

He appeared behind the Hobgoblin and slashed the back of his thigh with his dagger.

The cut was shallow, but the Hobgoblin flinched and spun around.

Pip had already leaped away.

“I thought you were getting your grandfather out of here!” Luca called.

“He's with the others!”

“Then get farther back!”

Luca used the moment while the Hobgoblin was turned away. His sword grazed the arm holding the axe.

The Hobgoblin's grip loosened.

The axe fell.

For a moment, Luca thought they had done it.

The Hobgoblin slammed into him with his shoulder.

Luca was thrown back and rolled across the ground. Pain shot through his side as he tried to breathe.

The Hobgoblin reached for his axe again.

“Luca!” Pip shouted.

“Stay back!”

Luca raised his hand.

He tried to draw Aethra. A small flame appeared in his palm, flickering unsteadily.

“Fireball!”

The fireball missed.

It hit the ground beside the Hobgoblin and burst with a small flash. He flinched. The axe slipped from his hand once more.

“Now!” Luca shouted.

The farmers finally moved together.

Two pushed a cart into the Hobgoblin's path. Another threw a net normally used to cover cargo.

The Hobgoblin stumbled.

He tore part of the net apart with his bare hands, but the cart blocked his way toward the farmers.

Luca rose with difficulty.

He still held his sword.

The Hobgoblin looked at Luca, at the farmers, then at his followers, who had already begun retreating with a few sacks.

They had not taken much food.

If the fight continued, they might lose all of it.

The Hobgoblin growled, threw off the remains of the net, and withdrew toward the forest.

No one followed.

Luca did not either.

He was not sure he could still run.

***

The field fell quiet.

Luca sat beside the fence, holding his side.

Pip approached. His dagger was back in his belt.

“You're hurt.”

“A little.”

“Do Humans always say that?”

“The ones who don't want to be examined, perhaps.”

Pip offered him a clean piece of cloth.

Luca took it and pressed it to his side.

“You came back to help me,” Luca said.

“I saw his back was exposed.”

“And you knew it was dangerous.”

“Of course.”

“But you went in anyway.”

Pip gave a small shrug.

“He was going to catch you.”

Luca watched him for a moment.

“Thank you.”

Pip seemed to have more trouble accepting those two words than facing the Hobgoblin.

“I only scratched him.”

“It was enough to make him turn.”

Pip looked toward the farmers.

“Usually they don't care which Goblins attacked.”

“Some of them still don't.”

“Then why did you get involved?”

Luca looked at the basket he had left by the road.

One medicinal herb lay inside it.

His first quest was not finished yet.

“I saw you helping your grandfather,” he said. “That was the first thing I noticed.”

Pip did not answer at once.

***

By the time Luca returned from the forest's edge, his basket held enough herbs.

He had stayed on the outer side of the red marker stones.

His legs ached.

So did his ribs.

For a low-risk quest, the day had felt far too long.

Near the field, some of the farmers repaired the fence. Goblins from Pip's group helped gather the scattered wood and sacks.

Not every farmer was pleased to see them.

But no one threw another stone.

Pip's grandfather sat by the storehouse with his leg bandaged. Two members of his tribe watched over him.

Pip stood beside the road.

“Will your grandfather be all right?” Luca asked.

“He needs rest. The others will take him away when he can walk.”

“Where will they go?”

Pip looked at the field, then at the forest.

“Somewhere they won't be blamed for another tribe's actions.”

Luca had no answer to that.

He looked toward the road to Arklune.

“I have to report to the Guild. About my quest and the attack.”

“I'm coming.”

Luca turned to him.

Pip touched the hilt of the dagger at his waist, as if checking it was still there.

“If only Humans tell the story, people might blame my tribe too. I want them to hear it from me.”

“What about your grandfather?”

“He told me to go. He said there were enough people here to watch over him.”

“And you agree?”

“I didn't say that.”

Pip started walking.

Luca caught up.

“You're walking to Arklune.”

“I know the way.”

“That isn't an answer either.”

Pip glanced at him.

“You ask a lot of questions.”

“I've heard that before.”

They walked a few steps in silence.

Ahead, Arklune grew larger at the end of the road.

“The city won't be easy for you,” Luca said.

“I know.”

“I only arrived two days ago myself. I may not be the best guide.”

“I know that too.”

Luca looked at him.

“You seem to know a lot.”

“More than someone who pushes a cart toward a Hobgoblin and then stands in front of it.”

“It worked.”

“You were nearly hit with an axe.”

“That part worked less well.”

Pip finally smiled.

Luca held out his hand.

“After we report, we can figure out what comes next. You decide whether you want to keep traveling with me.”

Pip looked at his hand.

“People will stare.”

“Probably.”

“Some might think I stole your basket.”

Luca glanced at it.

“Then don't carry the basket.”

Pip gave a short laugh.

Then he took Luca's hand.

On the road to Arklune, a Human and a Goblin walked side by side.

People might stare at them.

For now, Luca did not think much about it.

He had found the medicinal herbs he came for.

And, without planning to, a friend.

**End of Chapter 4**
