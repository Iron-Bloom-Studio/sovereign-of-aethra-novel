# Sovereign of Aethra

## Chapter 9: After the Ruins

The clear crystal above the table showed nothing once again.

Lysara raised it toward the light and carefully examined each side. It was no larger than the tip of her finger, held in place by a thin coil of silver wire and suspended from a single thread.

“No change in color,” she said gently.

Luca looked at his left palm.

The faint circular lines were still there. They were not dark enough to be called a wound, yet too distinct to be mistaken for a shadow. The shape resembled an unfinished symbol—several thin curves surrounding the center of his palm without ever truly meeting.

“So, is that good news?” Luca asked.

“It means this instrument found nothing.”

“That sounds like good news.”

“Not always.”

Luca exhaled. “Of course.”

They were gathered in one of the small meeting rooms on the second floor of the Arklune Guild. Rolan had lent it to them so Lysara could examine Luca’s hand without attracting the attention of the other adventurers.

Serena stood near the door with her arms folded. Her sword and shield rested against the wall within easy reach. Zerak remained in the corner farthest from the window, silent as usual, while Pip sat on a chair that was slightly too tall for him.

A plate of bread that was meant to be shared sat in front of Pip.

So far, the concept of *sharing* did not appear to apply to him.

Lysara set the crystal down and took a sheet of paper from her bundle of notes.

“Let us try once more. Use your simplest magic.”

Luca raised his right hand. A small flame appeared above his palm, wavering like a candle in the wind.

The mark on his left hand did not react.

“A little stronger,” Lysara said.

Luca enlarged the flame until it formed a ball the size of an apple.

Serena immediately glanced at the wooden table beneath it.

Luca caught her look. “I’m not going to burn the table.”

“The last time you said that, your sleeve caught fire.”

“That was different.”

“The fire came from your hand.”

“Which is exactly why my sleeve shouldn’t have been there.”

Pip stopped chewing. “Your explanation does not make that incident sound any better.”

The flame in Luca’s hand went out.

The mark on his left palm remained unchanged.

Lysara wrote something down. “Now use Lesser Heal.”

Luca pressed his right hand against the ribs that still ached from the battle in the ruins. Pale light spread between his fingers, gentle and warm.

The pain eased.

When Luca drew a deeper breath to test it, Pip reached out with one finger and pressed the injured spot.

Luca jolted.

“Hey!”

Pip immediately withdrew his hand. “Not healed yet.”

“I already knew that without your help.”

“I helped confirm it.”

Serena turned her face away, but Luca still caught the slightest movement at the corner of her mouth.

Even Zerak seemed to lower his head a fraction, as if hiding something.

Lysara covered her mouth with one hand before clearing her throat. “Lesser Heal closes minor wounds and helps the body recover. It cannot instantly restore injured bone or muscle.”

“In other words,” Pip said, “don’t press it.”

Luca glared at him.

Pip took another piece of bread.

Once again, the mark on Luca’s hand did not react to the light of Lesser Heal.

“Fire has no effect on it. Neither does Light,” Lysara said. “At least not in the amounts you can produce at present.”

Luca studied the thin lines. “Are you certain this is connected to the relic?”

“No.”

The answer came so quickly that Luca looked up.

Lysara met his gaze calmly. “I am certain the mark appeared when you touched the relic. That is not the same as understanding the connection between them.”

“Then what do you know?”

“That I need more information.”

Pip raised his piece of bread. “That is how a scholar says she does not know.”

Lysara smiled faintly. “Correct.”

The door opened before Pip could respond.

Rolan entered carrying a rolled map and several report sheets. He looked more tired than he had the day before.

He closed the door behind him.

“What did you find?”

“No measurable reaction so far,” Lysara replied. “The mark is stable. It is not spreading or fading, and it does not respond to basic uses of Fire or Light.”

Rolan looked at Luca. “Does it hurt?”

“No.”

“Feel hot?”

“No.”

“Whispering for you to do anything foolish?”

Luca paused. “No more than usual.”

Rolan nodded. “Good. Then we are still dealing with the same Luca.”

This time, Serena genuinely smiled, if only for a moment.

Rolan unrolled the map across the table. It showed Arklune and the surrounding region, including the hills where the ruins had been found. Several new lines of ink had been added to the south.

“From this point forward, any discussion of the relic and the mark on your hand stays inside this room,” he said. “The public report mentions only the underground passage, the Stone Golems, the Armored Lizards, and the structural damage.”

“What about the Ruin Golem?” Pip asked.

“It was already included in the earlier report.”

“And the Dual Art?”

Rolan sighed.

“Half the people in the hall downstairs have already heard about that.”

Serena frowned. “We did not announce it.”

“You returned with a Goblin, an Elf from Sylvaris, and a Hiveborn. Then one of the workers saw a shard of stone buried almost half an arm into the ruin wall.” Rolan looked at each of them in turn. “You do not need to announce anything. People will create the story on their own.”

“Whatever version is spreading must be exaggerated,” Luca said.

“According to the latest version, you defeated fifty golems and brought down half the hill.”

Pip looked interested. “How much were we paid in that version?”

“Nothing.”

“A bad story.”

Rolan did not respond. He pointed to the ruins on the map.

“The rumors are not what concern me. This is.”

He placed a scout’s report over the map.

“The inspection team found another passage leading out of the complex. It is narrow and partly collapsed, but one person could still pass through without carrying much.”

“The relic was taken out through there,” Serena said.

“That is our best assumption.”

Rolan moved his finger along a fresh ink line drawn south from the hills.

“They found black cloth fibers coated in wax near the exit. Waterproof material. The same as the scrap you found in the relic chamber.”

Lysara stepped closer to read the report.

“Footprints?”

“One person left the ruins. They later met at least two others and a pack animal.”

“At least?” Luca asked.

“The ground was hard. The scouts could only confirm three distinct sets of tracks.”

“Where did they go?”

Rolan slid his finger farther down the map.

“South. Once they reached the old road, they turned southwest.”

The room fell silent.

The road Rolan indicated had once been one of the minor trade routes connecting Arklune to the settlements in the southern region. Most of it had long since fallen out of use. The newer route to the east was safer and had more regularly maintained guard posts.

“The scouts followed them as far as a shallow river,” Rolan continued. “Their tracks vanished after the crossing. They knew how to break pursuit.”

“Or they were lucky,” Pip said.

“They removed a relic from the ruins without activating its guardian,” Serena replied. “I would not depend on the possibility that they were merely lucky.”

Zerak, who had remained silent until then, stepped closer to the table.

“How long would it take us to reach that river?”

“On foot? Nearly two days if the weather holds.”

“And how old are the tracks?”

“At least three days.”

Zerak studied the map. “They cannot be pursued directly.”

“Correct.” Rolan rolled one side of the map so it would not cover the other reports. “Which is why I will not send you to capture anyone.”

Luca raised an eyebrow. “You are sending us?”

“Not yet. I am considering it.”

Rolan regarded them seriously.

“If word of the missing relic spreads, people will swarm those hills. Treasure hunters, hired companies, nobles who think every old object should belong to them—they will all come. If the relic is dangerous, panic will only make matters worse.”

“So the Guild will conceal it?” Serena asked.

“The Guild will investigate without inviting fools with pickaxes into an unstable ruin.”

“That is a very cautious answer,” Lysara said.

“That is why I still have a Guild building.”

Rolan took out another document.

“Officially, your next assignment is to survey the old road south of Arklune. You will visit the last Guild post still recorded along that route, inspect the condition of the road, and ask about a suspicious group transporting covered cargo.”

“Unofficially?” Luca asked.

“Look for signs of the relic. Observe, document, and return. You are not bounty hunters.”

“What if they attack us?”

“You may defend yourselves.”

Pip raised a hand. “What if they abandon their cargo and run?”

“You may not sell it.”

Pip lowered his hand.

Lysara arranged the three copied pages she had brought from the Guild archive beside Rolan’s report.

“There is something else,” she said.

She pointed to a copy of an old illustration. It consisted of layered circles with lines leading toward the center.

“Sylvaris records concerning the old structures around Arklune are extremely limited. From two shelves of documents, I found only three pages that may be relevant.”

“Two shelves for three pages?” Pip asked.

“Some of the records discussed varieties of moss.”

“Was the moss important?”

“Apparently, it was very important to the author.”

Lysara turned one of the pages so Luca could see it.

“This record states that the structure was built to direct Aethra toward a central point. The original term is difficult to translate. It could mean heart, core, or axis.”

“Important object in the middle,” Pip said.

“Your translation is not entirely wrong.”

“Pip the scholar,” Luca murmured.

Pip nodded as if accepting an official title.

Lysara pointed to the next illustration. A shape at the center of the circle resembled the arrangement of lines on Luca’s palm.

Serena noticed it immediately.

“It resembles the mark.”

“It does,” Lysara said. “That does not mean they are the same.”

Luca placed his palm beside the illustration. Several of the curves did look similar, but the mark on his skin was incomplete. It was as though only part of the intended pattern had appeared.

“Does the record explain what it does?” he asked.

“No. The section after the illustration is damaged.”

“Of course it is.”

“Old documents rarely survive in a condition convenient for their readers.”

“It seems every important thing in the world was deliberately written on the part most likely to be ruined.”

Lysara gave a small smile. “I sometimes feel the same way.”

Rolan studied the illustration for some time before covering it with the report.

“The fewer people who know about this, the better.”

Luca closed his left hand. “If the missing relic really was the center of the ruins, what happens if it is taken too far away?”

“I do not know yet,” Lysara answered.

“Could the ruins collapse?”

“Possibly.”

“Could something inside them wake up?”

“Perhaps.”

“Could this mark do something?”

Lysara was silent for a moment.

“Perhaps.”

Luca stared at her flatly. “I am beginning to dislike that word.”

“So am I,” Serena said. “But it is better than a false answer.”

Luca knew Serena was right.

That only made it more irritating.

He exhaled and leaned back in his chair, then immediately grimaced when his ribs objected to the movement.

Pip extended a finger toward him.

“Don’t.”

Pip withdrew it with an innocent expression.

Rolan gathered the documents on the table.

“I will not order you to accept this assignment before everything is clear. The old road is barely guarded. Several settlements along the route have been abandoned, and the reports on monster activity have not been updated in months.”

“Are any settlements still inhabited?” Luca asked.

“At least one, according to the last report. There is also an old Guild waystation. I do not know whether it is still in use.”

“When was the last report?”

Rolan did not answer immediately.

Pip stopped chewing.

“How long?” he pressed.

“Eight months.”

Pip put down his bread. “Finally, something frightening.”

Luca looked at the members of Open Road one by one.

Serena studied the map again. Lysara touched the edge of her notes, lost in thought. Zerak showed little outward change, though his body leaned slightly toward the table. Pip regarded the remaining bread as if calculating how many provisions the assignment’s payment could buy.

“We do not have to decide now,” Luca said. “This may be more dangerous than an ordinary Rank E mission.”

“It is more dangerous,” Serena replied.

“You are not helping.”

“I am stating a fact.”

Luca began to feel annoyed, but he could not deny it.

Serena continued, “If someone is carrying a dangerous object along a trade route into unguarded territory, this is no longer only a problem concerning the ruins. I will go.”

“So will I,” Lysara said. “Eldren sent me to study Aethra’s behavior in this region. Ignoring a relic that may have formed the center of that structure would go against my assignment.”

Every eye then turned toward Zerak.

He studied the mark on Luca’s palm before speaking.

“I do not know this region. Nor do I know why I was near the ruins when you found me.” His voice remained calm. “If that road can provide answers, I will follow it.”

Pip released a long sigh.

“The road is bad, the reports are outdated, there may be monsters, and we are following people who know how to erase their tracks.”

“Correct,” Luca said.

“The pay?”

Rolan named the amount.

Pip considered it for several seconds.

“I’m going.”

Luca rubbed his face. “Of course.”

“I also need to make sure none of you packs supplies like an idiot.”

Serena raised an eyebrow. “I made long journeys before I met any of you.”

“You wrote down one spare shield on the equipment list.”

“Shields can break.”

“We are not bringing a cart.”

Lysara raised the bundle of paper in her hand. “I need to bring the copied Sylvaris records, blank paper, spare ink, and the measuring crystal.”

Pip looked at her equipment pouch. “How many bottles of ink?”

“Three.”

“One.”

“Two.”

Pip considered it. “As long as they are wrapped in cloth and kept away from the food.”

“That is quite reasonable.”

“I am always reasonable.”

Luca raised an eyebrow. “You just asked whether you could sell the missing relic.”

“That was also a reasonable question.”

Rolan handed the assignment form to Luca.

***

Their preparations continued until late in the evening.

Pip took charge of distributing their supplies before anyone officially asked him to. He inspected the rope, waterskins, lamp oil, medicine, and dried food one item at a time.

Lysara placed the copied records about the ruins and several sheets of blank paper inside a waterproof wrapping. Two bottles of ink were cushioned with cloth so they would not break during the journey. She also tucked away several small charcoal sticks for times when using ink would be impractical.

Serena still carried the tools required to maintain her sword and shield, but the idea of a spare shield was abandoned.

Zerak asked for little. His spear, water, food, and a piece of thick cloth to protect his equipment from the rain were enough.

Luca was assigned the rope, a small cooking pot, most of the dried food, and an additional waterskin.

He was beginning to suspect that being the leader was simply an excuse to give him the heaviest supplies.

When the others went downstairs to purchase the last of their provisions, Rolan asked Luca to remain on the upper floor.

They stood on a small balcony overlooking the Guild hall. Below them, the voices of adventurers mingled with the clink of cups and the scrape of moving chairs.

From there, Luca could see Serena speaking with the quartermaster. Pip stood on top of a crate so he could inspect the contents of a shelf. Lysara was explaining something to Zerak, while the Hiveborn listened without interrupting.

They looked like a party.

Luca simply remained uncertain why he was standing as its leader.

“Serena has more experience traveling,” he said. “Lysara understands magic better. Pip is better at managing supplies. Zerak…”

“Is better at making everyone in the hall keep their distance?” Rolan suggested.

Luca held back a smile. “I was going to say he is calmer in battle.”

“That is also true.”

“So what exactly do I do?”

Rolan rested his arms against the balcony rail.

“You listen.”

“That’s all?”

“Then you make a decision when everyone else has a different reason.”

Luca looked down at the members of his party.

“I feel as though they are following this trail because I touched that relic.”

“Serena is following it because she feels responsible. Lysara wants to understand the truth. Zerak is searching for answers. Pip is getting paid.”

“That last one is easy to believe.”

“Just because they walk with you does not mean they all walk for the same reason.” Rolan looked at Luca. “And do not mistake being at the front for always knowing the way.”

“That is comforting advice.”

“It is not my job to make you comfortable.”

Rolan patted Luca’s shoulder, then stopped when he saw him grimace.

“Your ribs?”

“A little.”

“Use Lesser Heal again before you sleep.”

“I know.”

“And if you find another relic—”

“I will not touch it.”

“Good.”

Luca fell silent.

Rolan narrowed his eyes. “You just considered an exception.”

“No.”

“Your face is easy to read.”

“I will tell the others before I touch it.”

“That is not the same sentence.”

***

Open Road left Arklune before sunrise.

The morning air was still cold when they passed through the southern gate. The guards checked only the Guild assignment before allowing them through.

Serena walked at the front beside Luca. Her shield was secured across her back, while one hand remained close to the hilt of her sword.

Lysara walked in the middle with Zerak. From time to time, she looked toward the hills as though trying to sense a change in the flow of Aethra through the air.

Pip stayed at the rear. His dagger rested at his waist, a small crossbow was secured to his pack, and two smoke bombs hung from a side pouch.

He was also carrying the lightest pack.

By contrast, Luca’s pack seemed to grow heavier every time the road climbed.

“Did you deliberately put all the heavy things in my pack?” Luca asked.

“Not all of them.”

“That answer is not reassuring.”

“You are the largest.”

Luca looked toward Zerak, who towered behind them a short distance away.

Pip followed his gaze.

“He is carrying a spear.”

“That is not a reason.”

“I did not say it was a good one.”

Luca turned to Serena. “You allowed this?”

“He rejected my spare shield.”

“That does not answer my question.”

“It means we are both making sacrifices.”

The main road continued south through fields slowly filling with morning light. Several hours later, they reached an old fork.

The eastern route still had stone markers and fresh wagon tracks. The other road was narrower and overgrown with weeds, gradually curving toward the southwest.

A signpost stood at the edge of the fork, its lettering nearly erased by years of weather.

Luca stopped in front of it.

Behind them, Arklune’s walls had already grown small.

Ahead, the old road stretched between trees and abandoned fields—toward territory no longer regularly patrolled by the Guild.

Serena studied the path. “Still want to lead?”

Luca looked at the road ahead, then at the members of Open Road standing behind him.

He sighed.

“No.”

Pip nodded in approval. “An honest answer.”

Luca began to walk.

“But we’re still going.”

For the first time as a complete party, Open Road left the safety of Arklune behind.

And the road ahead began to turn southwest.

**End of Chapter 9**
